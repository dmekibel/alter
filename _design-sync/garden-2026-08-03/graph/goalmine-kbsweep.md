**Lane:** the wider KB sweep (fieldguide thinker-profiles + concept-dictionary + alter's BOOKS-BUSINESS-CANON + BOOKS-MIND-PSY-CANON + JOURNEY-OS §2 Goal Loom).
**Brief:** David's critique — "just because we made cute animals doesn't mean this menu is simplistic. It should be very clever at helping people achieve their goals." ## 0. What's already designed — do not contradict

`_specs/JOURNEY-OS-2026-07-21.md` §2 (THE GOAL LOOM) already specifies the planner-side goal spine, and it is smarter than the garden menu currently exposes:

- **SMART with therapist add-ons** (real worksheet David sent): Specific & Simple · Measurable & **Meaningful** (what would it mean to you) · Achievable & has-it-worked-for-someone-else & as-if-now & all-areas-of-life · Realistic & Responsible & toward-what-you-WANT (approach not avoidance) · Timed.
- **Two-column steps**: every goal step is tagged **setup** or **system** (Clear's systems-over-goals, enacted as a form field, not a lecture) — this is the KB's James Clear mechanism already built in.
- **WOOP obstacle field** already exists on `S.goals`, cited to Johnson II.3/VI.8.
- **MI importance/confidence rulers** ("why a 4 and not lower") for the interview.
- **Weekly preview/review + month close (roll forward, no shame) + quarter close chronicle ceremony.**
- **ADHD floor**: a quarter can be ONE goal; the loom never renders as a wall; today only ever shows the one next step.
- **Data model**: `S.loom = {q, theme, months:[{goals:[{title, why, area, steps:[{t, kind:setup|system, due, done}], woop}]}]}` — flagged as needing to unify with `S.goals` or bridge to it; **never run two goal models at once.**

The Goals Menu (statue system) is presumably the visual skin sitting on top of this same `S.goals`/`S.loom` data. Every mechanism below is written to **deepen the loom's existing SMART+WOOP+setup/system spine**, not replace it — and to close the specific gaps David is pointing at: the obstacle line has no plan attached, the "3 numbers" are status not diagnosis, and nothing in the menu currently catches a vague or oversized next-step before it gets written.

## 0.1 The current menu's three shallow points (grounded in the actual spec text)

Reading `PROMPT-goals-menu-2026-08-10.md` literally:

1. **Q4 (First Cut / creation) writes only HALF of WOOP.** The flow is "name the goal, break it into passes, **write the obstacle line**, choose the creature, place the boulder." Oettingen's own finding (cited in Johnson's profile) is that naming the obstacle **without** a paired if-then plan is *worse than plain positive visualization* for follow-through — it surfaces the threat without giving the brain an exit. The obstacle line as currently scoped is O-O-O with no P.
2. **The "3 honest numbers" are pure status, not diagnosis.** "passes done · days since the first cut · passes to the next stage" — none of these is a *lead* measure (process you controlled) as distinct from a *lag* measure (outcome), and "days since the first cut" is a raw countup that, on a stalled goal, silently becomes a shame number even though the spec explicitly bans shame lines ("no decay, no shame, resumable forever"). A big countup next to a stalled stone reads as a countdown to guilt no matter what the copy says.
3. **The next-pass CTA has no concreteness gate.** Q1's example ("Next pass · book the venue") is good — but nothing in Q4 stops a user from writing a pass like "get healthier" or "figure out the business plan," which cannot become a CTA at all. The menu currently trusts the user to already know how to write a good next-action; that's the exact SMART-style intelligence David is asking for.

## 1. Mechanisms (source → mechanism → menu mechanic)

### 1. WOOP completed (the missing P) — Gabriele Oettingen, via Brian Johnson SN-233
**What it is:** W(ish) → O(utcome, vividly imagined) → O(bstacle, the biggest **internal** one) → P(lan): "if [obstacle], then I will [specific action]." Mental contrasting alone (imagining success + naming the obstacle) is roughly 2x more effective than positive visualization alone — but Johnson's profile flags the critical finding: pure positive visualization *reduces* motivation because the brain treats imagining success as partial completion. Adding Obstacle maintains drive. The Plan step (Gollwitzer's implementation intentions — Johnson calls these "Ancient Intelligence micro-algorithms") is what converts the obstacle from a worry into a pre-loaded reflex.
**Menu mechanic:** Q4's "write the obstacle line" becomes two linked lines, not one: the obstacle (kept exactly as-is, in quotes, under the goal name) *plus* a second line that only unlocks after the obstacle is named — "if that happens, I will ___" — rendered as a short chip-picker (2-4 templated actions plus a free-text option, MI-style, never a blank form) rather than an open textarea. On Q2, the obstacle line becomes tap-to-reveal: tapping it shows the if-then plan beneath it. This is a two-line change to Q4's copy shape, not a new screen.

### 2. Abstract goals are neurologically inert — Anokhin's Aktseptor, via Andrey Kurpatov
**What it is:** Anokhin's "Action Result Acceptor" (акцептор результата действия) can only be built from *existing* neural circuits laid down by actual experience. A goal with no experiential basis has no neural infrastructure — "intentions without specific neural encoding are noise." Kurpatov's reframe: willpower-based goals fail because they require circuits that don't exist yet; build the circuits first through small concrete actions, and the larger goal becomes achievable. This is the single most direct KB grounding for "the next-step line must always be a real-life action" — it isn't a copywriting preference, it's the claim that an abstract next-step is *functionally meaningless to the brain*, not just uninspiring.
**Menu mechanic:** a **concreteness lint** on every pass typed in Q4 (and on any pass edit later). Reject silently-fail patterns the same way `_dev/copy-audit.py` already lints copy: a pass name that is a single abstract noun-phrase with no verb+object+place/time ("get healthier," "figure it out," "work on the business") gets a gentle inline nudge — not a blocking error, a chip: "make it something you could point at" with 2-3 auto-generated concrete rewrites drawn from the goal's own area tag (mirrors the existing goal-decomposition templates at app.js ~346, "complex activity → concrete steps"). This is the exact SMART-Specific gate David is asking for, but implemented as a lint, not a lecture — never a wall of validation text.

### 3. Domino Physics — the 2mm domino, via Brian Johnson (Lorne Whitehead)
**What it is:** a domino can topple another domino 50% larger than itself. Starting from a 2mm domino, by the 13th domino you reach Burj-Khalifa height; by the 29th, Earth to Moon. Johnson's practical translation: "don't look for the Empire State Building move. Find the 2mm domino and knock it over. The compound does the rest — IF you maintain consistency."
**Menu mechanic:** the CTA generator (Q1's row CTA and Q2's "that next pass") should not default to *sequential* next-uncompleted-pass — it should default to the **smallest** uncompleted pass among the ready ones. If a goal has both "book the venue" and "send the invite list," and the invite list is smaller, the invite list surfaces first even if it was added second. This directly produces "always a real-life action" because it's optimizing for do-ability, not for list order. When a pass has sat untouched past a soft threshold, the same engine offers (never forces) a "split this into something smaller" chip — the shrink offer, not the guilt.

### 4. The Motivational Calculator — Piers Steel's procrastination equation, via Brian Johnson SN-246
**What it is:** M = Energy × (Expectancy × Value) / (Impulsiveness × Delay). Energy sits outside the fraction — if zero, the whole equation is zero (matches ALTER's own Energy→Work→Love ordering, already in the Big-3 lens cited in the Goal Loom's own evidence line). Expectancy = confidence you'll succeed (raised by small wins, proven protocol). Value = how much you actually care right now (raised by reconnecting to purpose). Impulsiveness = distractibility (lowered by environment design). Delay = how far off the reward feels (lowered by compressing timelines, celebrating process wins). The point of naming all four separately: **the generic fix ("try harder," "add urgency") only ever pulls one of four levers, and it's rarely the right one for a given stall.**
**Menu mechanic:** a stalled-stone diagnostic that fires *only when the user opens a goal that's been untouched a while* (never a push notification, never a badge — matches "no timers" law). One tap-through, four chips, plain language, no jargon: "still want this?" (Value) / "does the next step feel doable right now?" (Expectancy) / "is something in the way?" (Impulsiveness → routes to Clear's environment-design question, below) / "does the payoff feel far off?" (Delay → offers to shrink the next milestone). Each answer routes to a *different* honest fix instead of one generic "keep going" nudge. This is Fogg's own troubleshooting order (Prompt → Ability → Motivation, last) arriving at the same structure from a different book — cross-thinker convergence, safe to build once.

### 5. Systems vs. Goals + the Two-Minute Rule — James Clear
**What it is:** "Winners and losers share the same goals — goals don't differentiate outcomes... true long-term thinking is goal-less thinking." Already partially built into the Goal Loom as the setup/system tag on each step. Clear's Two-Minute Rule extends it: any recurring ("system") step should have a sub-2-minute fallback version, because the point of showing up is casting an identity vote, not optimizing performance — "you are casting an identity vote, not optimizing performance. Optimization is a later problem." Do not raise the bar prematurely.
**Menu mechanic:** on the passes list (Q2), a pass tagged **system** (recurring, per the loom's existing kind field) can carry an optional shrink-fallback baked in at creation ("full version: 30-min gym session · today's-hard-day version: put the shoes on"). The pass still counts as done either way — no partial-credit shame math, just two doorways into the same chisel mark. This is a one-field addition to the loom's existing `steps:[{t, kind, due, done}]` shape, not a new mechanic.

### 6. Environment Design's four principles — James Clear
**What it is:** self-control is a short-term strategy; environment design is the long-term one. Four levers: make good cues obvious / bad cues invisible; one-space-one-use; friction engineering (add seconds to bad habits, remove seconds from good ones); social environment as the most powerful cue of all. Bypasses willpower entirely by removing the cue before the craving fires.
**Menu mechanic:** Q4's creation flow gets one more optional chip after the obstacle/plan pair: "where will this actually happen?" — a single environment question, not a form. If answered, it can spawn a distinct **setup-type pass** ("put the mat by the door") that's visually different from a behavior pass (a small icon distinction on the passes list — not a new sprite family, just a marker), matching the loom's existing setup/system split but making the *first* setup step literally about the physical environment rather than an abstract "getting organized" line.

### 7. Values vs. Goals — Russ Harris / ACT (The Happiness Trap)
**What it is:** goals are a destination, partly outside your control; values are how you travel, fully inside your control, livable in any single moment regardless of whether the goal ever lands. "Success in life = living by your values." This is the honest escape hatch from goal-completion as the only measure of a life going well — and it's the mechanism that makes a *paused* stone genuinely okay rather than merely tolerated.
**Menu mechanic:** the "why"/meaning line the loom already collects per goal (the SMART-Meaningful field) should be independently visible from the goal's completion status — i.e. a paused or abandoned stone can still show its value line as intact ("this was about being present for family" stays true even if the goal itself quietly retires). This directly serves the spec's existing law ("a paused goal simply stands at its carve stage, waiting; no decay, no shame") by giving it something *positive* to stand for, not just an absence of punishment. Pairs with the open "closure ritual for abandoned plans" item already flagged in `BOOKS-MIND-PSY-CANON` Part 6 #4 — a small ceremony (not silent decay) for retiring a stone on purpose, keeping the value, releasing the specific goal.

### 8. HARD — why people don't follow through — Russ Harris
**What it is:** Hooked by thoughts ("I'll screw it up") / Avoiding discomfort / Remoteness from values / **Doubtful goals** (too big, too vague, resource mismatch — antidote: break down to the next small, easy step). Harris's explicit rule for what makes a *good* short-term goal: specific, realistic, and never a "dead person's goal" (something a corpse could do better, like "not smoking") — reframe to the toward-move instead.
**Menu mechanic:** the concreteness lint (mechanism #2) doubles as a **dead-person's-goal filter**: a pass phrased as an absence ("stop procrastinating," "don't skip leg day") gets nudged toward its toward-move equivalent ("open the doc for 5 minutes," "put the shoes on") using the same chip-rewrite UI, not a separate feature.

### 9. Four Disciplines of Execution (WIG / Lead vs. Lag / Cadence of Accountability) — via Brian Johnson's "Discipline 4" material
**What it is:** Discipline 1, a Wildly Important Goal (narrow the goal, don't spray it across everything). Discipline 2, Lead Measures — measure the inputs you control (process score), not just outputs you can't (outcome score); the golfer who shot a mediocre 92 but executed his pre-shot routine on 17/18 holes had a 94% *process* score — Dorfman's line: "0 for 26 — why are you calling me? Keep dominating your protocol." Discipline 3, a Compelling Scoreboard — visible, simple, shows win/lose right now (explicitly **not** a percent bar per the menu's own laws — a scoreboard can be a count, a strip, a fill-state, never a percentage). Discipline 4, Cadence of Accountability — a real weekly ritual: what's the score, did I do what I committed to last week, what's my ONE commitment this week.
**Menu mechanic:** re-cast Q2's "3 honest numbers" as one lead + one lag + one self-referential trend, replacing the raw "days since the first cut" countup (mechanism-critique #2 above): **(a)** passes done this week (lead — process you controlled), **(b)** passes to the next carve stage (lag — the honest outcome distance, kept), **(c)** your own pace vs. your own last-period average (self-referential, never vs. other users — see mechanism #12). The weekly Cadence of Accountability slots directly into the loom's already-planned weekly preview/review (§2.3) — literally the same three questions ("what's the score / did I do what I said / what's my one commitment this week") rendered as the review card, not a new system.

### 10. The Strategic Question — Coaching Habit (Bungay Stanier), via Michael Porter
**What it is:** "If you're saying yes to this, what are you saying no to?" Every yes carries a hidden no of omission or commission. Porter: "the essence of strategy is choosing what not to do." Paired with the AWE Question ("And what else?" — the best coaching question in the world, asked 3-5 times max, never endlessly) and the Learning Question ("What was most useful for you?" — exploits the peak-end rule, the ending shapes the whole memory).
**Menu mechanic:** the Strategic Question fires exactly once, at the point the loom's own 3-5-goal-per-month cap (already spec'd in §2.1) is hit — adding a 4th goal to a full month surfaces "what would this push out?" instead of silently overloading. This is already flagged as a general planner move in the Coaching Habit book-brief; scope it specifically to the Goal Loom's month cap so it doesn't duplicate the planner's separate commitment-flow version. The Learning Question becomes the optional, skippable line on Q3 (THE WAKING) — after a statue wakes, one soft prompt: "what was most useful about doing this?" — turning the completion ceremony into real reflection weight instead of pure spectacle, without adding required friction (matches the canon's Law 9: never force reflection on a schedule).

### 11. Sacred Vow — daily/periodic recommitment, via Brian Johnson
**What it is:** Johnson recommits every morning, not because he forgot, but because "commitment is a daily fire" (Steve Chandler: the human spirit must be re-lit every day, like a campfire; Phil Stutz: the spiritual chair doesn't stay built). Commitment is architecturally periodic, not a one-time install.
**Menu mechanic:** the obstacle/plan pair from mechanism #1 gets a light, optional re-surface at the natural weekly-review moment (§2.3) — "still true?" with a one-tap yes/edit, never a mandatory gate, never a timer-driven push. This keeps the WOOP plan from going stale on a long-running goal without turning it into homework.

### 12. Compete with your future self, never with others — Kurpatov (Anokhin's comparison mechanism) + Deci (ego-involvement) + Octalysis (Conformity Anchor trap)
**What it is:** three independent sources converge on the same warning. Kurpatov: "most 'motivation' is hierarchy-seeking in disguise... real motivation works on current-you vs. future-you (growth signal), not current-you vs. others (hierarchy signal)." Deci: ego-involved framing (self-worth tied to outcome, especially comparative outcome) produces more pressure and shallower thinking than task-involved framing; social-comparison progress bars are named directly as a design trap to avoid, replaced with self-referential-only comparisons ("your best week," "up from last month"). Octalysis's Conformity Anchor technique carries its own hard warning: **never state the undesired behavior is the norm** — a real study found a "many people steal from this forest" sign *increased* theft 160%, because it legitimized the behavior.
**Menu mechanic:** the self-referential trend number in mechanism #9(c) is the direct build of this; the hard rule to carry into the spec is that no Goals Menu surface ever shows this-user's-pace against other-users'-pace, and no "most goals stall around here" style copy is ever used even as a motivational device — it backfires per the cited study.

### 13. B=MAP + the Ability Chain diagnostic — BJ Fogg (Tiny Habits)
**What it is:** Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt, all three required simultaneously; Prompt is binary (present/absent), Motivation and Ability trade off. The Ability Chain names five weak links (time, money, physical capability, mental/creative effort, fit-with-routine) and the single diagnostic question: "what is making this behavior hard to do?" — find the *one* weakest link, not all five. Troubleshooting order: check Prompt first, then Ability, then Motivation last ("if you're using willpower, you're doing it wrong").
**Menu mechanic:** this is the direct implementation detail behind mechanism #4's "does the next step feel doable right now?" branch — when a user says no, the follow-up isn't generic, it's the five-link picker (too little time / costs money / physically hard right now / too much to figure out / doesn't fit my day), and each answer maps to a different concrete offer (shrink it, do a placeholder version, swap the anchor moment, split the mental step out as its own pass). Also grounds the Dolphins/Turtles/Crabs/Clams segmentation as a soft internal classifier for a stalled goal, never shown to the user as a label: Turtle (wants it, can't execute — Ability Chain applies) vs. Crab (could, won't — route to the value line, mechanism #7) vs. Clam (neither — offer the closure ceremony, mechanism #7, with zero pressure to justify it).

### 14. Octalysis translation layer — Yu-Kai Chou (menu-craft, not new science)
**What it is:** the PBL Fallacy ("Points, Badges, Leaderboards" copy the shell of games without the substance — "learn from the design, don't copy the shell") is the single biggest trap for a stats-and-carve-stage UI like this one. Useful, spec-compliant techniques: **Milestone Unlock** (reveal a new capability exactly when it would help the challenge just completed — e.g. the shrink-pass offer only appears once a pass has genuinely sat, not from day one); **Glowing Choice + the 4-Second Rule** (exactly one visually prominent next action per screen; a user must know what to do within 4 seconds or disengages — matches the menu's own "every card ends in an action" law); **Rightful Heritage framing** ("you've earned this pass" language rather than "here's a task," reframing an ordinary next-step as something already owed to the user's own effort); **CD1 Epic Meaning's believability gate** — invoking a greater cause you don't credibly embody insults the user, which is the sharpest possible warning against overselling a mundane goal ("do the dishes") with epic-quest language just because the visual system is a statue-and-creature mythology.
**Menu mechanic:** treat the carve-strip/statue system itself as the Development & Accomplishment (CD2) + Ownership (CD4) engine it already is — it doesn't need points bolted on top. The one net-new UI move worth taking: gate the "shrink this pass" and "still want this?" chips (mechanisms #3, #11) behind Milestone-Unlock timing (only surface after real staleness, never on a fresh goal) so the smart layer reads as *earned help*, not a nagging feature that's always visible.

## 2. Warnings — what these thinkers would say NOT to do

- **Never a percent bar, never a countdown timer, anywhere in this system.** The menu's own laws already ban both; multiple KB sources independently explain *why* a raw progress number can silently become a shame mechanism (Deci's ego-involvement, the "days since first cut" critique above) — this isn't just a style rule, it's load-bearing.
- **Never force the WOOP interview, the weekly review, or the "still true?" recommit on a timer or as a blocking gate.** MI's documented finding: forced plans on a schedule produce a measurable motivational crash. Readiness-signal gating only (per the loom's own §2.2 design and the canon's Law 9).
- **Never compare one user's pace, streak, or goal count to other users', and never imply "most people stall here" as a motivational device** — the Petrified Forest sign study (theft +160% from a "many people steal" sign) is the citable proof this backfires. Self-referential only.
- **Never a fixed day-count** ("21 days to carve this stone") — Fogg explicitly debunks it as folklore, Harris flags it as unsourced; formation time is a function of the person and the goal, not a universal number.
- **Never affirmation-register copy on any goal or pass line** ("I am disciplined," "I am the kind of person who..."). Harris/ACT: affirmations backfire against self-image and get rejected by cognitive dissonance in exactly the users who need them most. Use noticing/toward-move language instead.
- **Never a demotivator** — guilt pledges, public-shame stakes, streak-loss screens. Fogg names these explicitly and flags that even he is ambivalent about them because they morph into shame; ALTER's own never-punish law already forbids the category outright.
- **Never let the "smart" layer become a wall of text or a mandatory form at creation.** The loom's own ADHD floor — "a quarter can be ONE goal, the loom never renders as a wall" — is the hard constraint every mechanism above must fit inside. Every mechanism here is scoped as a chip, an optional reveal, or a once-triggered prompt, specifically so it doesn't violate this.
- **Never dismiss a stalled goal as a discipline failure before running the diagnostic.** Fogg's troubleshooting order (Prompt → Ability → Motivation, in that order, motivation last) and Steel's four-lever equation both exist specifically to stop the reflexive "just try harder" response, which is statistically the least likely lever to be the actual problem.
- **Never let the statue mythology oversell a mundane goal.** Octalysis's CD1 believability gate: epic framing on a goal that doesn't earn it reads as insulting, not motivating. Some goals are just "call the clinic" — let the carve stay literal and small.
- **Never run a second goal data model.** The Goal Loom's own build-spec flag stands: unify with `S.goals` or bridge explicitly; the statue menu must be a skin on the same data, not a parallel system that drifts out of sync with the planner side.
