## 0. THE DIAGNOSIS, IN JOHNSON'S OWN FRAME

David's complaint is exactly the failure mode Johnson names in the very first minute of the course: **"It's not enough to become the best version of yourself and post a six-pack selfie."** A pretty carve-stage animation is the six-pack selfie. The current Q4 creation flow (name → break into passes → one obstacle line → pick creature → place boulder) is a *container*, not a *method*. It never runs the goal through anything that would catch a bad goal, diagnose a stalled one, or guarantee the next action is real. Johnson's entire Module II.3 + IV.5 + V exist to fix precisely this gap — they are goal-SCIENCE, not goal-VIBES, and they are the actual content of "SMART-style intelligence."

**The one-sentence fix:** every mechanism below either (a) makes goal *creation* diagnostic instead of a form, or (b) makes the *next-action line* structurally incapable of being vague, or (c) makes a *stalled* goal self-repair instead of silently rotting on the island. Those three gaps are exactly what "shallow" means here.

---

## 1. THE MASTER FRAME — read this before touching Q4

Johnson's whole course runs on one compounding sequence (COURSE-MAP.md lines 17-27): **VIA strengths → Top 10 Virtues → Embodied Behaviors → Virtue Meditation → Algorithms.** Applied to goals specifically, his structural claim (III-Theory, stated as "the ×2 lens") is that **every domain of life gets read through Identity × Virtues × Behaviors** — who you're being, what virtue that expresses, what specific action proves it. A goal that only names an outcome ("run a marathon") skips the two layers that actually predict follow-through (identity, virtue). This is the single biggest lever available and it is **already the game's own metaphor** — see Mechanism 1.

## 2. MECHANISMS WITH TEETH

### 1. The Walking Statue / Michelangelo's Studio — identity IS the mechanic, not the flavor
**Source:** III-Theory transcript, lines ~480-565. Verbatim: *"That marble that he sculpted David, the iconic David in, was rejected by two other sculptors... You are a walking statue... Identity literally means repeated beingness... The studio has no walls."*

This is not a coincidence — **the app already independently arrived at Johnson's own central metaphor** (stone statue → carved → wakes). Right now the statue is only decorative skin on top of a checklist. Johnson's version makes the metaphor load-bearing: *what you carve isn't the goal, it's you* — each pass is a chisel strike against the person, not just a task. Concretely: Johnson's own worksheet flow is Identity → Virtue → Behavior (Carpe Diem Journal, III-Theory line 507-520): pick the identity word first ("athlete," "philosopher," "soul mate"), one virtue that identity requires, one behavior that proves it today.

**Menu mechanic:** Q4 (First Cut) gets a mandatory first question before the goal name: *"Who are you becoming by finishing this?"* — one identity word, drawn from the app's existing virtue/identity vocabulary (reuse `VIRTUES`, no new taxonomy). That word becomes a **permanent line on the goal card in Q1/Q2**, sitting next to the carve strip — not just "3 of 5 passes" but "3 of 5 passes · becoming: disciplined." When a pass completes, the guardian's celebration line references the identity word, not just the checkbox ("that's the disciplined you, showing up"). This is a one-field addition with an enormous framing payoff — it is the cheapest mechanism in this document and the most load-bearing.

### 2. WOOP (Oettingen) — the obstacle line must be FORCED, not optional prose
**Source:** II.3-Theory transcript, lines 205-275. Verbatim: *"Wish, Outcome, Obstacles, Plan... it's not enough just to imagine your ideal future. You need to rub it up against reality."* The lab finding, stated directly: one group just imagined the ideal; the other imagined it **then named the obstacle and made a plan** — *"this group that whooped it achieved the goals they wanted to achieve way more than the group that simply imagined."*

The spec already calls Q4's obstacle line "the WOOP move" — but WOOP is four parts and the current spec only implements one (the obstacle). Wish and Outcome are implicit in the goal name; **Plan is missing entirely**, and Plan is the part Gollwitzer's if-then research (cited by name in this transcript, II.3-Theory line ~214: *"if-then is known as implementation intentions"*) shows is the active ingredient — obstacle-naming alone doesn't move behavior, obstacle+plan does.

**Menu mechanic:** Q4's obstacle-line step becomes a two-line pair, not one: *"When [obstacle], I will..."* — an explicit if-then typed as one sentence, not a paragraph. This pair (not just the obstacle) is what shows in quotes under the goal name in Q2. When a pass stalls (see Mechanism 6), the guardian can literally read the user's own if-then back to them — "you said: when tired after work, I'll do 10 minutes not the full session. Want to do that now?" — which is only possible if the plan half exists as data, not prose.

### 3. 4DX (via Johnson) — lead measure vs. lag measure: the pass IS the lead measure
**Source:** IV.5-Theory transcript, lines 626-693. Verbatim: *"They call it a WIG goal, wildly important goal... achieving that goal is a lag measure. It's an outcome that comes as a byproduct of your process... you want to have a lead measure... Wildly important goal, lead measure, scorecard, and consistency."* Johnson's own example: not "publish a body of work" (lag) but "hours of deep work per week" (lead, trackable, controllable).

This is the direct science behind David's ask that "the next-step line should always be a real-life action." The reason today's Q1 CTA line ("Next pass · book the venue") sometimes reads real and sometimes reads abstract is that **passes aren't currently typed** — some are outcomes in disguise ("lose 5 lbs" as a pass), some are real controllable actions ("call the clinic"). 4DX gives the mechanical test: a lead measure is something you can do TODAY, fully within your control, that you have good reason to believe drives the lag outcome.

**Menu mechanic:** Q4's pass-breakdown step runs each pass through a one-tap gate at entry: *"Is this something you do, or something that happens?"* If the user types an outcome-shaped pass ("lose 5 lbs"), the creation flow doesn't block it, but auto-suggests the lead-measure rewrite ("3 gym sessions this week") the way the existing WOOP-plan bead already does obstacle-rewriting (`engine-v1.json`, `motivation-diagnostic`/`woop-plan` beads — this is the same move, just applied one step earlier, at pass-authoring instead of at stall-diagnosis). This is the mechanical guarantee behind "next action is always real" — it's enforced at the data-entry step, not hoped for in copy.

### 4. Floor + Ceiling on every pass — "we'll have a basic floor and then a target"
**Source:** O-Theory line 1191, IV.7-Theory lines 850-865, VI.1-Theory lines 64-90. Verbatim (VI.1): *"Imagine if somehow you were able to build [a skyscraper] without any floors or any scaffolding... one slip and boom, you go back down to the bottom... imagine if you built scaffolding such that you didn't drop so far."* Verbatim (O-Theory): *"We are going to challenge you to do some simple floor behaviors and some aspirational targets."*

This is Johnson's name for exactly the mechanic ALTER's own Sequencer already runs app-wide (`engine-v1.json` `_tier()`/`_resolve()`: floor/standard/ceiling, "FLOOR can never fail (8s) — that's the MVP law") — but it is **not currently wired into goal passes.** A goal pass today is presumably binary done/not-done. Johnson's floor/scaffolding metaphor is literally the app's own carve-stage logic (a statue can't fall back down a stage) applied one level down, to the individual pass.

**Menu mechanic:** every pass optionally carries a floor form (can't-fail, ~seconds) alongside its full form — reuse the exact `bead.floor/standard/ceiling` pattern already proven engine-wide, don't invent a second system. On a bad-energy day, tapping a pass offers the floor version ("today: just put the shoes on") and it STILL advances the chisel a sliver (not a full stage-jump, a partial-fill within the stage — visually: the current carve stage's silhouette gets a thin highlight edge, not a full stage advance). This directly prevents the failure mode where a hard week makes a whole statue look abandoned; the statue **stays warm** instead of going cold. Ties to `floor-parity-of-warmth` already specified in `canon-fold.json` (line 248) — reuse that law, don't re-derive it.

### 5. Prior Best as New Baseline — never start a goal from zero
**Source:** IV.1-Theory, lines 384-410. Verbatim: *"Make your prior best your new baseline... What is your prior best? Step back... look at who you are in your past, who you have been at your absolute best... Do more of the things you were doing and do less of the things you weren't doing."* (Josh Waitzkin's frame, per Johnson.)

Right now Q4 asks the user to author passes cold. Johnson's method is to mine the user's own history first — this is a diagnostic question, not a platitude, and it changes the actual numbers the user picks (someone who already hit the gym 2x/week last spring should not set a pass at 5x/week from a standing start).

**Menu mechanic:** Q4 adds one optional question before pass-authoring: *"Closest you've ever gotten to this, even briefly — what did that look like?"* If answered, the suggested first-pass sizing is pinned to that number, not a generic default. This is cheap (one text field + one default-suggestion tweak) and it is the single most direct fix for the "goals set too big, abandoned in week 2" failure mode that every habit book (and this course, repeatedly) names as the #1 killer.

### 6. The Motivation Equation as the stall-diagnostic — "Why It Stalled" already exists, wire it to goals
**Source:** II.3-Theory, lines 277-330. Verbatim: *"Motivation equals... Value times Expectancy over Impulsivity times Delay... if you don't really want it and or you don't think you can have it, your motivation is going to wane."*

`engine-v1.json` already contains a bead literally named `motivation-diagnostic` / "Why It Stalled" (purpose: *"diagnose which lever is weak — desire, confidence, distraction, or distance"*) that triggers when *"a goal is repeatedly stalled — WOOP'd but no progress over multiple active days."* This is Johnson's Motivation Equation, already built as a generic Sequencer bead — but the Goals menu spec (PROMPT-goals-menu-2026-08-10.md) never mentions it. Today a paused statue "simply stands... no decay, no shame, resumable forever" — which is correct for *tone* but means a stalled goal gets **zero diagnostic help**, just silence.

**Menu mechanic:** a statue that hasn't had a pass land in N days (reuse the existing `staleGoals()` read, `app.js:1427` — no new engine) surfaces a **quiet, non-shaming diagnostic prompt** the next time its row is tapped, before the normal unfold: four short taps — "still want it? / still think you can? / what's pulling your attention instead? / does it feel too far off?" — each answer routes to a different, already-designed fix: low value → offer to retire the statue with dignity (Win-or-Learn framing, Mechanism 7, never "failed"); low expectancy → shrink the next pass to its floor; high distraction → name the specific competing pull as the obstacle-line's new obstacle; too-distant → insert one nearer pass between the current stage and the next. This is the actual SMART-intelligence David is asking for: **the app diagnoses WHY a goal isn't moving and prescribes the matching fix, instead of just showing the same static row.**

### 7. Win-or-Learn — the retro after a missed pass, never a failure state
**Source:** VI.7-Theory/Practice, lines 700-727 + Practice transcript lines 573-707. Verbatim: *"What Worked? What Needs Work? What Am I Gonna Do Differently? ... we simply use this moment to do the needs work equation on it and get back to work a little stronger... You get a mistake learner's high."*

This is the exact mechanism that keeps the diagnostic in Mechanism 6 from ever reading as punishment — it's already a house law (`no-emojis`, `reward-never-shame` per memory index) but Johnson supplies the concrete **three-question shape**, which is more structured than a generic "how'd that go?" box.

**Menu mechanic:** any pass marked missed/skipped (not just stalled goals — any single miss) can optionally open a 3-tap micro-retro: *worked / needs work / next time.* The third answer — "next time" — **overwrites the if-then plan from Mechanism 2**, so the goal's obstacle+plan line literally gets smarter with use instead of staying frozen from creation day. This closes the loop between Mechanisms 2 and 6: the plan isn't authored once and forgotten, it's a living line the goal's own history keeps sharpening.

### 8. Targets + Agency + Plans (hope theory, via Johnson's Prosper module) — require a SECOND pathway
**Source:** VI.8-Theory, lines 5-11 + 730-745. Verbatim: *"Prosper = Go Forward with Hope... A target, a better future, a goal, the agency to believe we can achieve it, and the plans to go do it."* (This is Snyder's hope theory, which Johnson explicitly cites as three parts — target, agency, **plural plans**, not one plan.)

WOOP (Mechanism 2) gives one if-then plan. Snyder's hope research (which Johnson is directly quoting) is specific that high-hope people hold **multiple pathways** to the same target — if plan A is blocked, plan B exists — and that this plurality, not raw optimism, is what predicts persistence through real obstacles.

**Menu mechanic:** this is deliberately NOT a mandatory field at creation (Q4 is already asking a lot) — it's a **second-obstacle offer** that appears only after the FIRST obstacle has actually been hit once (the goal's if-then got used, per Mechanism 7's miss-tracking). At that point the unfolded card (Q2) offers: *"That plan didn't hold last time. Want a backup?"* — a second if-then line, stored alongside the first, and the guardian picks whichever one matches the obstacle that's actually live. This keeps Q4 lean while making the goal's resilience compound with real use — exactly Johnson's "aggregation and compounding" language (III-Theory line ~562).

### 9. "Fifty pounds equals an A" — volume passes for craft/skill goals
**Source:** IV.1-Theory, lines 425-460. Verbatim: *"the group that was challenged to create a high quantity of art... fifty pounds equals both an A and the highest quality art... Go create a masterpiece day. Go for it again and again and again."*

Not every goal is a countable checklist (call the clinic, book the venue). Skill/craft goals (learn guitar, write a novel, get better at public speaking) fail the current pass model because "practice guitar" isn't a single completable task — it's a rep count. Johnson's own resolution (borrowed from the classic ceramics-class study) is to make the goal's unit of progress **volume**, not a quality judgment on any single rep.

**Menu mechanic:** at Q4, when the user's goal is skill-shaped (heuristic: no natural single finish-line action — offer this as a second creation template, not a forced branch), passes become **rep-counted tiers** instead of named one-off tasks — "10 practice sessions," "25 pages," "20 cold calls" — and each individual rep just ticks a counter toward the next carve stage, with zero judgment on any single rep's quality. This is a second, parallel pass-authoring mode in Q4, not a replacement for the task-list mode.

### 10. The archer / Target Practice — the CTA line must name process, never outcome
**Source:** II.3-Theory, lines 155-190. Verbatim: *"An archer has a clear target... the good philosopher knows that ultimately the only thing they can do is control the process of aiming well, preparing well, practicing. But the moment they release the arrow, they've given up ultimate control. What they are responsible for... is doing our best in pursuit of a goal."*

This is the copy-law version of Mechanism 3 (lead vs. lag) — it governs how the CTA line should be WRITTEN, not just what data it's built from. A CTA that reads "Next pass · lose 2 lbs" violates this even if "lose 2 lbs" happens to be technically achievable — it names the arrow's landing spot, not the draw of the bow.

**Menu mechanic:** a copy-gate rule for every pass CTA string (add to `_dev/copy-audit.py` per `alter/CLAUDE.md`'s existing copy-gate law): the verb must be a controllable action verb (call, write, show up, do, practice — not lose, gain, hit, reach, achieve). This is nearly free to enforce and it's the exact mechanical guarantee behind "the next-step line should always be a real-life action" — codify it as a linter rule, not a hope.

## 3. WHAT ALREADY EXISTS AND SHOULD BE REUSED, NOT REBUILT

The Sequencer in `_course/_build/engine-v1.json` already contains Johnson-derived goal beads — `woop-plan`, `obstacle-crusher`, `motivation-diagnostic`, `micro-win`, `wildly-important-targets`, `philosophy-handbook`, `week-scorecard` — built for the *daily journey surface*, not the Goals menu. **Do not re-derive these; wire the Goals menu to read/write the same fields** (`g.woop`, `g.woop.obstacle`, `g.woop.plan`) so a goal authored in the statue-wakes flow and a goal surfaced by the daily Sequencer are the *same object*, not two parallel goal systems. This is the single biggest engineering trap available: building the Goals menu's "smart" layer as its own thing would silently fork state the Sequencer already depends on (`goalNoWoop`, `staleGoal`, `goalNoStep` in `userModelSchema`).

## 4. WHAT JOHNSON WOULD FLAG AS TRAPS (do NOT do these)

- **Don't ship an obstacle line without a plan line.** Oettingen's own lab data (II.3-Theory) is that naming the obstacle *alone*, without the if-then, does not outperform pure fantasizing. Half-WOOP is close to no-WOOP.
- **Don't let a "pass" be an outcome in a task's clothing.** ("Lose 5 lbs" is not a lead measure.) This is the exact 4DX distinction Johnson draws a hard line on (IV.5-Theory) — get this wrong and the whole "next step is always real" promise breaks silently, one bad pass at a time.
- **Don't build a goal system with no floor.** Johnson's skyscraper metaphor (VI.1-Theory) is explicit that a system with peaks and no floors produces *worse* outcomes than slower steady progress — "one slip and boom, you go back down to the bottom," and the user "feel[s] worse than... before" the inspiration hit. A statue that can only advance in full stages, never partially warm on a bad day, reproduces this exact failure.
- **Don't diagnose a stall as a character problem.** The Motivation Equation (II.3-Theory) is explicitly a *mechanical* diagnostic (which of 4 levers is weak) — never let the copy imply the user lacks discipline. Pair every diagnostic with Win-or-Learn's non-punitive frame (VI.7).
- **Don't make the identity/virtue layer decorative.** If "becoming: disciplined" is just flavor text that never changes copy or behavior, it's the six-pack-selfie trap all over again — the celebration lines, the stall diagnostic, and the retro must all actually reference it.
- **Don't invent a second obstacle/plan/goal-state system parallel to the existing Sequencer's `S.goals[].woop` shape.** (See §3.)
- **Don't ask for both pathways (Mechanism 8) at creation.** Snyder's hope-theory plurality is a *resilience* mechanic, not a front-loaded form field — Johnson's own course sequences WOOP (single plan, Module II.3) long before Prosper's multi-pathway idea (Module VI.8). Front-loading it at Q4 would just make an already-heavy creation flow heavier for zero behavior-change gain on day one.

## 5. BUILD-ORDER RECOMMENDATION (cheapest teeth first)

1. **Mechanism 1** (identity word, one field) + **Mechanism 10** (CTA copy-gate rule) — near-zero engineering, immediate framing upgrade, both are additive.
2. **Mechanism 2** (WOOP's missing Plan half) — one more field in Q4, but it's the one that makes Mechanisms 6/7/8 possible later; do this before anything downstream.
3. **Mechanism 3** (lead-measure gate on pass authoring) — the mechanical guts of "next action is always real"; pairs directly with the existing `woop-plan`/`motivation-diagnostic` beads, so this is mostly wiring, not new logic.
4. **Mechanism 4** (floor/ceiling per pass) — reuses the engine-wide `_tier()`/`_resolve()` pattern already proven; the main work is the partial-carve visual (a stage's silhouette gaining a thin highlight short of a full stage-advance).
5. **Mechanism 6 + 7** (stall diagnostic + miss retro) — wire the Goals menu to the *already-built* `motivation-diagnostic` bead rather than inventing a new one; add the 3-tap Win-or-Learn retro as the natural companion.
6. **Mechanism 5** (prior-best question) and **9** (volume-pass mode) — defer to a Q4 v2 pass; both are creation-flow additions that can ship independently once the core loop (1-2-3-4) is solid.
7. **Mechanism 8** (second pathway) — defer until real usage data shows goals actually hitting their obstacle and needing a backup; per the trap note above, this is a resilience layer, not a launch requirement.
