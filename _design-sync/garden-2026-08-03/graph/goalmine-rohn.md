## The one thing under all of it

Rohn's goal machinery isn't a planning technique bolted onto a philosophy — it IS the philosophy, applied. His five-link chain (what you believe → how you feel → what you do → what you get → how you live) means a goal card that only tracks "what you get" (percent done, passes left) is answering the wrong layer. The menu's job, in Rohn's terms, is to keep the WHY and the BELIEF visible at every touch point, not just the checklist under it. That's the specific gap between "cute animals" and "methodologically load-bearing" — the current frames (per the Q1-Q5 build) already nail the visual honesty (stone-is-earned, no shame, no percent bars); what they're missing is anything that makes the GOAL ITSELF smarter than a checklist with a statue skin.

## MECHANISMS

### 1. The Wish-to-Goal Gate (write-it-down as a real gate, not a formality)
**What it is:** Rohn draws a hard line between a wish and a goal, and the line is the act of writing it down with enough specificity that the mind treats it as decided rather than hoped-for. A goal only "casually pursued" — named but never pinned down — stays a wish no matter how much the person wants it.
**Source:** atom 12, *7 Strategies for Wealth and Happiness* / *The Five Major Pieces to the Life Puzzle*.
**Menu mechanic:** Q4 (THE FIRST CUT) currently asks for name + passes + obstacle line + creature. Add a real gate: the boulder doesn't get placed on the island (i.e., the goal doesn't leave draft state) until the WHY field (mechanism 2) and at least one concrete first pass are filled with real content — not just a name typed into a box. A goal saved without those stays off-island, visually a loose stone in a "wishes" tray, not yet planted. This makes "casually pursued" a literal, visible app state instead of a Rohn line nobody enforces.

### 2. Reasons-First Creation (the WHY gates the HOW, not the reverse)
**What it is:** Rohn's stated order is reasons before methods — a strong enough reason produces its own how; a person who starts with tactics and no real reason quits at the first obstacle because there's no fuel behind the goal. This is also where his identity claim lives: the actual payoff of a goal is who you had to become to hit it, not the object itself.
**Source:** atom 12; quote, *7 Strategies for Wealth and Happiness*: "Reasons come first, answers second."
**Menu mechanic:** in Q4, put the WHY field BEFORE the passes-breakdown field, not after, and frame it as identity, not outcome — "who does finishing this make you," not "why do you want this." Lightly validate the answer isn't just the goal restated (a lone-noun-phrase or a bare restatement like "because I want to hit X" should re-prompt once — not a hard block, a single nudge, since the entire law of this system is forward-only and non-punishing). Re-surface that exact WHY text at every stage-wake in Q3 and at the finished-goal state in Q1, per the ALTER-APPLICATION note already on file — this is the single cheapest, highest-leverage addition: one field, one storage key, reused at three existing surfaces.

### 3. The Ambition-Floor Check (goals set too low are a hazard, not a safe choice)
**What it is:** Rohn's counter-intuitive claim: an easy goal isn't the safe option, it's the dangerous one, because a goal that doesn't stretch you doesn't grow you. Most goal-setting UX treats "smaller and more achievable" as always virtuous; Rohn explicitly argues the opposite failure mode is just as real.
**Source:** quote, *The Treasury of Quotes*: "Don't set your goals too low. If you don't need much, you won't become much."
**Menu mechanic:** at Q4, if a user sets a goal with very few passes (say 1-2) relative to the size/duration language in the goal name, show one soft, single-instance nudge — not a blocking gate, not repeated: "is this the whole climb, or the first landing?" with a one-tap "add more passes" vs "no, this is right." This is the app disagreeing with the user exactly once, which is the honest version of a coach who doesn't just validate everything typed into a box. Must stay optional and non-repeating per the app's forward-only law — this is a stretch INVITATION, never a gate that blocks saving.

### 4. The Root-Cause Ladder (diagnosing a stalled goal by layer, not by symptom)
**What it is:** when results stop coming, Rohn's chain says don't fix results directly — walk up the chain and find which layer actually broke: did activity stop (didn't do the thing), did attitude sour (stopped believing it mattered), or did the underlying philosophy change (you don't actually want this goal anymore)? Each is a different problem with a different fix; treating all three the same is what makes a check-in feel generic.
**Source:** atom 1, *The Five Major Pieces to the Life Puzzle*.
**Menu mechanic:** this is the actual "SMART-style intelligence" David is asking for. When a goal has no passes advanced in N days, instead of one generic nudge, the re-engagement prompt asks a single diagnostic tap: "stuck on doing it, stuck on wanting it, or stuck on whether it's still the right goal?" Each answer routes differently — doing-it problem → the app shrinks the next pass into something smaller and offers to schedule it directly on today's planner (ties to mechanism 7); wanting-it problem → resurface the WHY text from mechanism 2, unedited, as a mirror, not a lecture; wrong-goal problem → offer an honest archive/rescope path instead of leaving the statue standing half-carved forever with no exit. This turns the goal engine from "track passes" into "diagnose what actually stalled," which is the real difference between a checklist and a coach.

### 5. Season-Aware Next-Action Copy (the goal's own life has phases, not just a percent)
**What it is:** Rohn's seasons aren't decorative — spring is committing with no guarantee of payoff, summer is unglamorous defense of what's already planted, fall is the truth-telling harvest, and winter is neutral until the earlier seasons decide its character. Crucially, effort and reward are deliberately disconnected in time: you cannot ask for an advance on a harvest that hasn't grown yet, and winter can arrive mid-any-season as a real setback, not just a calendar slot.
**Source:** atoms 4, 5, 7, 8, *Seasons of Life*.
**Menu mechanic:** the "next: call the clinic" live-line on a goal row (Q1) and the CTA in Q2 should be phase-aware copy, driven by real state (passes done vs. days since last pass), not a flat template. A brand-new goal gets spring language (start, no guarantee, that's fine). A goal with several passes done but the payoff not yet visible gets copy that names the lag directly — "no visible change yet; that's the gap between planting and harvest, not a signal something's wrong" — so silence from the app doesn't read as the app not noticing. A goal that's gone quiet for a while gets winter language: rest without abandonment, and the CTA should offer a deliberately SMALLER next pass to re-enter rather than the original-size one, which is what actually turns "I haven't touched this in three weeks" into a 30-second re-entry instead of a wall.

### 6. Drifting vs. Paused (neglect named honestly, not left ambiguous)
**What it is:** Rohn's account of failure isn't dramatic collapse, it's undetected drift — small non-action that nobody consciously chose and nobody notices happening, the boat drifting toward the falls while the person's attention is elsewhere. He's explicit that the fix isn't more resources, it's naming the drift before it compounds.
**Source:** atom 13, *The Five Major Pieces to the Life Puzzle*.
**Menu mechanic:** the goals list currently (per the "no decay, no shame" law) treats a stopped goal as simply "paused, waiting." Rohn's distinction argues for splitting that into two honest states: PAUSED (the user explicitly chose to set it down — still shown with full respect, zero pressure) vs. DRIFTING (nobody chose anything, it just went quiet). Drifting is not a shame state — no red, no percent, no timer, per the existing laws — but it should be a state the user can SEE and resolve with one tap: "still drifting" (fine, no judgment, dismiss) or "actually let's park this" (converts to Paused honestly). The point isn't to guilt the user, it's to stop zombie goals — statues nobody killed but nobody's carving — from sitting in ambiguous limbo forever, which is exactly the shallow-menu failure mode David is naming.

### 7. Week-Seal's One Best Opportunity (cross-goal prioritization, not per-goal isolation)
**What it is:** Rohn's planning is explicitly nested by timescale (day inside week inside month) and explicitly must be closed on paper before the next level starts or it lives only in the head and decays. Separately, his broader teaching on daily opportunities argues that of everything available on a given day, usually only ONE thing is the actual best use of it — most people treat all options as equally worth doing, which is itself a planning error.
**Source:** atom 18, *7 Strategies for Wealth and Happiness*.
**Menu mechanic:** this is the sharpest fix for "the next-step line should always be a real-life action." Right now each goal's CTA lives inside its own card, isolated from every other goal's CTA — nothing ranks across goals. At the existing week-seal ritual (already a build surface per the ALTER-APPLICATION notes), add ONE cross-goal question: of every open goal's next pass, which is this week's single best opportunity? Not a full replan, not a checklist of all of them — one picked pass, handed straight to the planner as a real scheduled block on a real day. This is also the literal fix for "next action must always be a real-life action": the goal card's CTA should hand off into an actual calendar slot, not stay an abstract line living only inside the goal menu.

### 8. Pass-Complete Feeling Capture (review as a felt ritual, not a percent readout)
**What it is:** Rohn kept a journal specifically to catch feeling before it faded, not to log facts — his stated reason for spending money on nice journals was to force himself to find something worth writing, not to record events. Applied to review: the value of looking back at a goal isn't the number, it's what completing a piece of it proved about you (loops straight back to mechanism 2's identity WHY).
**Source:** atom 17, *The Treasury of Quotes* / *The Five Major Pieces to the Life Puzzle*.
**Menu mechanic:** when a pass completes (a carve stage advances, per Q3), instead of only the visual wake moment, log one short, optional, un-scored line into the chronicle: "what did that prove?" — pre-filled with nothing, genuinely optional, never required to proceed. This is intentionally small (matches Rohn's own idea-catch journaling, not long-form) and it's the mechanism that keeps the identity thread (mechanism 2's WHY) alive between creation and completion instead of only bookending it.

## WARNINGS — what Rohn would say NOT to do here

- **Don't let the app supply the WHY.** Rohn's entire claim about reasons is that they have to be the person's own, self-generated, or they carry no fuel — an AI-suggested "why" (auto-filled identity language, a picked-from-a-list reason) is exactly the "mild disgust" version he says can't propel anything. The WHY field must stay a genuinely blank prompt the user fills, never a picker.
- **Don't turn the ambition-floor check (mechanism 3) into a recurring nag.** Rohn pushes back once, hard, at the moment of commitment — he doesn't hover. A repeated "are you sure this is big enough" prompt would violate the app's own forward-only, no-shame law and read as judgmental rather than clarifying.
- **Don't moralize the drifting state (mechanism 6).** Rohn names neglect honestly but never punitively — the fix he describes is awareness, not guilt. Any red, any streak-loss framing, any "you're failing this goal" language on a drifting goal breaks both his own logic (drift isn't a moral failure, it's just unattended attention) and the app's existing "no shame" law.
- **Don't treat the seasons (mechanism 5) as a calendar.** Rohn is explicit that winter can appear inside any season — it has to be detected from the user's actual recent pattern (misses, gaps, energy), never scheduled by month or by goal-age alone. A goal that's three weeks old can already be in "winter."
- **Don't let the root-cause ladder (mechanism 4) become a mandatory gate before every check-in.** It's a diagnostic offered when a goal has genuinely stalled, not a form the user fills every time they open a goal card — Rohn's chain is a lens for when something's wrong, not a checklist for when things are fine.
