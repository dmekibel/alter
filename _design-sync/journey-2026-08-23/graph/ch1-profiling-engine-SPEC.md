## 1 · THE LAWS (from the raw books — these govern every question)

1. **Never ask what manufactures resistance.** MI names the exact trap: "avoid asking questions the answer to which would be sustain talk... 'Why haven't you changed?' / 'What keeps you from doing this?'" (MI txt:4045). The sticking-point finder must NEVER be phrased "what's stopped you before."
2. **Menu, never serial.** Options offered one at a time to an ambivalent person get rejected one at a time; "commitment to freely chosen options sticks." Every question = 4-6 chips at once, self-portraiture not gate.
3. **Reflect as statement, not question.** "In response to a question, people may feel defensive" (MI txt:2670). App plays back what it heard as a flat statement + confirm/correct chips: "Mornings are the hard part." [that's right / not quite]
4. **Ask permission before revealing its read.** "Unsolicited advice... the junk mail of life" (MI txt:8353). The profile reveal is offered, never announced — and declining must be a real path.
5. **Never label.** No diagnosis words, no trait names shown back, no "shy," no "addicted" ("their desires are being hacked... not addicted," Anxious Gen). Modes are "today's mode," re-askable.
6. **Readiness is sorted per sticking point, not once globally.** CFG's own data: <20% of any problem population is ready for action, yet >90% of programs are built for that 20%; jumping a precontemplation smoker to action = 6% success vs 24% preparation-start (CFG txt:144). This is WHY the survey exists — the mismatch stat is the engine's justification.
7. **One metaphor, locked.** When a profile answer selects an operating metaphor (Ferrari engine / bicycle brakes), the app uses it consistently forever after (ADHD 2.0's own law).

## 2 · THE DIALS (the profile model each answer feeds)

**DIAL 0 — USE-MODE (THE WAY-OUT LAW, round 9; the explicit fork question KILLED round 11 — a new user has no context to choose between rooms).** Use-mode is INFERRED from behavior, never asked; the way out lives in the structure (nothing locked, no nag, the journey a room you enter when ready). Tools-only → breathwork handed over NOW, home+tools unlock immediately, the journey parks with its place held, zero nag; the philosophy said out loud: take anything you want, no shaming, no forcing, the core free forever. Journey lock-in applies ONLY to users who chose the path. "Not sure" → the two-minute tour, then the same fork again. This dial also identifies who wants the lecture vs who would bounce off a self-help course — reactance theory says the way out IS the persuasion.

## 3 · THE PAYOFF MECHANICS (what makes it feel clever, not quiz)

- **The ruler + why-not-zero:** "how much does this matter right now, 0-10" → "You said 6. Not 0. What's the 6 about?" (chips built from their prior answers). Their own number becomes the next beat's material — MI's engineered change talk (txt:4006).
- **Lending change talk:** after 2-3 answers the app finishes their sentence as a statement: "Sounds like you're already halfway out the door on this one." [fair / not quite] — a miss costs nothing, the correction discloses more (MI txt:4267). THE mechanism behind "each answer visibly pays off."
- **The permission-gated reveal:** mid-chapter, the app offers: "Want to see what I'm picking up so far?" → a short mirrored portrait in plain words (no trait labels), each line traceable to a tap they made. This is the survey's designed payoff moment.
- **Coming alongside:** the most defensive chip ("nothing works for me") gets flat agreement, not reassurance: "Yeah. Nothing's worked." — engineered to produce THEIR walkback (MI ~txt:10362).
- **The hinge out:** Brief Action Planning's line, adapted: "Is there anything you'd like to try with [their thing] this week?" + confidence check; low confidence branches to "what would make it easier?" — the survey stays adaptive past session one (MI txt:5007).
- **Values card-sort (bank for later chapters):** MI's 100-value sort (public domain, txt:7103) is tap-native — a candidate for the garden/goals ceremony, not ch1 (too long for session one).
