## 1. PT consensus for a morning routine, desk-slumped adult

**Dynamic first, static after warmth, not before.** Static holds on cold tissue reduce pliability and short-term power output; the PT-favored morning structure is light movement, then dynamic range-of-motion work, with static holds layered in once the tissue has some circulation — which a "stand next to the bed and move" routine naturally provides by move 3-4 in. [CC-DYN] [MAYO]

**The one thing nobody put in the founder's brief: don't open with deep spinal flexion.** After a night lying down, spinal discs are osmotically swollen with fluid; loaded or repeated forward-bending in roughly the first 30-60 minutes after waking measurably increases stress on the disc wall (McGill's group cites up to ~300% higher flexion stress than the same movement done later in the day), and occupational studies tied to this finding show fewer disabling back-pain days when morning flexion is minimized. This doesn't mean "never bend forward in the morning" — it means don't make a deep, passive, hang-off-the-hips fold the *second thing* the routine asks for, and cue any forward fold as an active hip-hinge with soft knees rather than a passive dangle. [MCGILL] [SQUATU-MCGILL]

**Controlled articular rotations (CARs) as the organizing idea for "joint circles."** Slow, full-range, actively-controlled circles at a single joint (shoulder, hip, ankle, neck within safe range) are the modern default for "wake the joint up" work — deliberate and controlled, not the fast loose arm-flailing older warm-ups used. Good fit for a spoken, eyes-elsewhere routine because the cue is simple: big, slow, controlled circle, one joint at a time. [CARS-JG] [CARS-SYM]

**Spinal segmental movement: cat-cow is the one PTs treat as near-universal.** It mobilizes cervical, thoracic and lumbar segments together, is synced to breath (inhale = arch/cow, exhale = round/cat), and is specifically recommended first thing in the morning for exactly the stiffness a desk-slumped adult wakes up with. The one safety rule that shows up specifically for cat-cow: don't fling the head back into the arch — keep the neck long, in line with the spine, "cow" is a lift of the gaze, not a head-snap. Thoracic rotation (twisting from the ribcage, not the low back) matters for a separate anatomical reason: the thoracic spine is built to rotate and the lumbar spine isn't, so a stiff mid-back forces the low back to do rotation it isn't built for — the cue that keeps a spoken twist safe is "let the turn come from above the waist, hips stay put." [CC-CATCOW] [RH-THOR]

**Hip hinge as the safety pattern underneath every hamstring- and hip-flexor-adjacent move.** The recurring PT correction across hamstring stretches (standing or seated) is the same one: don't reach down, push the hips back / hinge forward with a long spine, and stop rounding at the low back. The same hinge pattern is what keeps a forward fold or a heel-out hamstring stretch from turning into the "deep flexion" problem above. For hip flexors specifically, the standard fix for sitting all day is a half-kneeling or standing lunge with the back heel lifted, hips driven forward, and the back glute actively squeezed (posterior pelvic tilt) — without the glute-squeeze cue, people just arch the low back instead of lengthening the hip flexor. [HH-HAM] [HOPT-HINGE] [DJP-HIP] [HBH-HIP] [LPT-HIP]

**Ankle/calf.** Ankle circles and a standard standing calf stretch (heel down, back leg straight) are low-risk, commonly limited in desk workers (most people are short on ankle dorsiflexion specifically), and require no real coaching beyond "heel stays down." [RH-CALF]

**Neck — what's safe, what's discouraged.** Safe: slow chin tucks (retraction — drawing the chin straight back, not bowing the head down), slow ear-to-shoulder tilts with no forceful hand-pulling, slow rotation with the chin staying level and shoulders still. Discouraged in current PT guidance: neck *extension* (tipping the head backward) as a stretch, fast or jerky rotations, and — relevant to this routine's very old-school predecessor moves — full circular "neck rolls" that pass through end-range extension, which modern guidance replaces with separate tilt/rotation/tuck movements instead of one continuous circle. [ASP-NECK] [NSNJ] [CC-CATCOW]

**Wrists/hands — yes, keep a sequence, but cue palm orientation first.** The wrist flexor stretch (arm straight, **palm up**, use the other hand to pull the fingers back) and wrist extensor stretch (same arm, **palm down**, gently press the back of the hand down and in) are standard, PT-endorsed, and worth keeping — they just have to be spoken in an order a listener can execute with their eyes closed: state which way the palm faces *before* asking for the pull, every time. A bare "pull fingers back" or "turn the hand down" with no stated starting orientation is exactly the ambiguity the founder flagged. [HH-WRIST] [CC-WRIST]

## 3. Recommended routine — 50 moves, standing to floor

General rule instead of repeating it 20 times: any move that doesn't involve foot/leg position (1-9, 13-24, 31) works identically seated — sit tall instead of standing tall, no cue change needed. Specific seated-at-desk alternates are called out only where the seated version actually differs, or doesn't exist.

Checkpoints: **first 6** = complete neck/shoulder wake-up, no flexion, ~45-60 sec. **First 12** adds spine/scapular/side-body work, ~2 min. **First 24** = full upper body including hands, ~4 min. **First 30** = the complete standing routine, ~5-5.5 min — this is the natural "I only have a few minutes, standing only" stopping point. 31-50 is the floor portion for longer doses, up to ~10 min.

## 4. Safety line

**Opening line, before move 1** (pain rule + tempo rule + breath rule, in plain words):
> "If anything pinches or hurts, back off — you should feel a gentle pull, never pain. Move slowly, especially on the longer holds, and keep breathing the whole time."

Sourced to: stretch to tension not pain [MAYO]; stretching should not hurt, sharp/shooting sensation = stop [general PT pain-rule consensus, cross-checked across HH-MORN and CC sources]; slow and unforced, no bouncing [MAYO]; breathe through it, don't hold your breath [HH-MORN, CC-CATCOW breath-sync].

**Second reminder, at move 31→32** (the standing-to-floor transition, i.e. right where doses longer than ~5 minutes keep going and where several of the longer static holds — figure-4, forward fold, active hamstring raise — live):
> "Same rule down here: nothing should hurt, so ease off anything that pinches, and keep the same slow pace on the floor work."

This is the right placement because it's the exact seam in the routine where a short "standing only" dose would have already ended (checkpoint at 30) and a longer dose keeps running for another 3-5 minutes of deeper holds — the point where a second, brief reminder earns its place rather than feeling repeated for its own sake.
