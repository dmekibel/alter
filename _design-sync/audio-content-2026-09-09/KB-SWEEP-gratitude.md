## B. THE MECHANISM, verified in-source

Organized around the five mechanisms named in the task, plus what else surfaced. Every claim below is tagged **VERIFIED** (researcher + finding + exact source) or **NOT VERIFIED** (flag, do not attribute to a researcher in copy).

**1. Specificity over categories — VERIFIED, three independent sources converge.**
- Emmons & McCullough (2003), *J Personality & Social Psychology* 84(2):377-389 — the foundational RCT; positive-affect boost is the most robust finding. *(SCIENCE-PAGE-EVIDENCE.md §1)*
- Emmons's studied protocol: 5 specific things, written weekly, for 10 weeks. *(arete-q1-BRIEF.md line 144)*
- Emmons's own framing of *why* specificity matters: entitlement is "the #1 obstacle to gratitude," remedied by shifting "take things for granted" → "take them **as** granted." *(arete-q1-BRIEF.md line 53, 57)*
- Neff/self-compassion brief, citing an (unnamed-in-brief) 10-week study: gratitude journal must be "daily, specific ('the way my cat purrs against my leg' not 'my cat'), always find something new" — outperformed an annoyance-writing and a neutral control group. *(selfcompassion-BRIEF.md line 63)*
- Stutz & Michels' own raw instruction (see mechanism 5 below) independently arrives at the same requirement from a completely different angle: **don't repeat items, and the strain of finding a new one each time is itself part of the mechanism**, not friction to remove.

**2. The cause/attribution step — VERIFIED as Emmons's framing, NOT independently effect-sized.**
- Emmons's reframe (take-for-granted → take-as-granted, arete-q1-BRIEF.md line 53) is specifically about **tracing where a good thing came from** rather than only registering that it happened — this is what separates gratitude from mere noticing/savoring.
- I could not find a source on disk that isolates the attribution step's own effect size, distinct from the general gratitude-writing effect. Treat as directionally supported by Emmons's own theoretical framing, not as a separately measured finding.

**3. Savoring duration — PARTIALLY VERIFIED, no named researcher on disk.**
- selfcompassion-BRIEF.md line 61: "15-30 minutes noticing as many pleasant things as possible... Savoring extends to food (engage every sense, slow down, extend the sensation) and to reliving joyous memories." A "positive focus" walking group came back "measurably happier" than a negative-focus or neutral control group — **the study itself is unattributed in the brief** (no researcher named).
- ORTNER-MANIFESTING-BRIEF-2026-08-01.md line 35: savoring is named as one of several "inputs" to nervous-system state regulation (Days 4-6), with no further mechanism detail mined.
- Fred Bryant (the real savoring researcher, Bryant & Veroff) is **not on disk anywhere** — see Provenance. Do not attribute savoring claims to a named researcher in copy; the duration figure (15-30 min) is for an unguided walk, not a 30-90 second spoken beat, so it does not transfer as a specific number either.

**4. Mental subtraction (imagining the good thing's absence) — NOT VERIFIED anywhere on disk.**
- Zero hits for "mental subtraction," "George Bailey technique," "Lyubomirsky," or "Koo" in any mined or raw store, ALTER's or fieldguide's.
- The only adjacent thing found is Stutz & Michels' raw instruction to also silently list "bad things that aren't happening" as part of the *naming* step (coming-alive.txt, p.211) — that is about noticing **absent negatives**, not absent positives, and is not the same move as imagining a specific good thing never happened.
- **This matters directly for the rewrite**: ALTER's own shipped `gratitudeBeat` copy currently states as settled fact, three separate times (`intro.why`, `intro.how[3]`, and beats 7-9), that imagining a good thing's absence "makes the mind register it as a gift again instead of a given." That claim ships today with **no verifiable source in any store I searched.** The writer should either find and cite the real study, or rewrite the claim as the app's own plain reasoning without borrowing the authority of "science."

**5. The "feel it in the body" step, and its duration — VERIFIED, the best-sourced mechanism in this sweep.**
- **The true raw source** — Stutz & Michels, *Coming Alive* (2017), pp. 211-212, read directly (`coming-alive.txt` lines 7245-7289): "State specific things you're grateful for... Don't use the same items each time... you should feel a slight strain from having to come up with new ideas. Stop thinking and focus on the physical sensation of gratefulness. You'll feel it coming directly from your heart... your chest will soften and open." Total tool duration, per `phil-stutz-master-guide.md` line 249: **30-60 seconds.**
- Independent, primary-cited biological grounding for *why* the somatic step needs real dwell time, not an instant tap-and-move-on: heart-focused breathing at ~6 breaths/min (a ~10-second cycle) plus intentional positive emotion (appreciation, care) produces measurable HRV coherence. Sources cited in `hrv-heart-coherence.md`: Shaffer & Ginsberg (2017), *Frontiers in Public Health*; Porges (2011), *The Polyvagal Theory*; McCraty & Childre (2010), *Alternative Therapies* 16(4); Lehrer et al. (2013), *Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback*. The mechanism requires **several breath cycles**, not a single second, before a coherence pattern can form — this is a real constraint on how short this beat can be.
- **A claim to flag, not fully verified**: fieldguide's synthesis layer (`SN-083`, `phil-stutz-master-guide.md` L251) adds that "5 minutes of felt gratitude produces 6+ hours of immune benefit (IgA elevation)," attributed to HeartMath. `hrv-heart-coherence.md` — the more rigorous, primary-cited mechanism file — does **not** mention IgA or immune data at all. Treat the IgA figure as one level removed from a primary citation; safe to say "gratitude activates the parasympathetic system and lowers stress hormones" (well-supported), risky to cite the specific immune-hours number without a primary source check.

**6. Overall effect size — VERIFIED, and it caps how big a claim the copy can make.**
- Emmons & McCullough (2003); Davis et al. (2016), meta-analysis, d=.31; Cregg & Cheavens (2021), depression/anxiety g≈-0.29, authors' own words "relatively modest"; Choi et al. (2025), *PNAS*, largest meta (145 papers, 24,804 people), g=0.19, **shrinks in better-randomized studies** (publication-bias signature). *(SCIENCE-PAGE-EVIDENCE.md §1, read in full)* — STRENGTH: moderate. Not a depression/anxiety treatment. Whatever the rewrite claims, it should not oversell past "a real, modest, mostly self-reported-mood effect."

**7. Why felt-gratitude beats "counting blessings" as an argument against negativity — VERIFIED as Stutz's clinical theory, not a Grateful-Flow-specific RCT.**
- Stutz's claim (`SN-083`, `phil-stutz-master-guide.md` L252): negative thoughts carry more neurological momentum than positive ones, so arguing with a negative thought fails — Grateful Flow instead activates "a completely different energy center." The underlying loss-aversion citation (Kahneman & Tversky 1979, 2:1 negativity weighting) is real and independently confirmed in `SCIENCE-PAGE-EVIDENCE.md §7`. Its *specific application* to "gratitude beats arguing with negativity" is Stutz's own theoretical synthesis, not a separately cited study of Grateful Flow itself.

**8. Dispenza's "gratitude before the fact" — a different mechanism, flagged by its own source as partly unusable.**
- Real technique as described (feel the gratitude of a desired outcome *before* it happens, pre-conditioning the nervous system) — `dispenza-BRIEF.md` "Gratitude as a Protocol, Not a Reaction." But that same brief's own Refused section explicitly bars importing "the field responds to who you are being" / "the universe" framing into ALTER copy, and the one concrete study it cites in support (Glen Rein's DNA-in-water HeartMath study) is itself a small, non-replicated claim presented descriptively, not as vetted proof. This mechanism is a different beat than the one the task describes (past-oriented, one-good-thing gratitude) — flagging it mainly so the writer doesn't accidentally blend it in.

**9. The metaphysical framing must not leak into copy — a house-style constraint, not a science finding.**
`SN-083`, `SN-118`, and `phil-stutz-master-guide.md` all wrap the real biological mechanism (parasympathetic/vagal activation) in "the Source," "the Field," "a higher organ of perception," Withers/Goddard/Kybalion language. Per `dispenza-BRIEF.md`'s own Refused section ("Never let charge/intention copy use 'the universe' or 'the field'") and ALTER's general mechanism-honest house style, the **keepable** claim is the ANS/parasympathetic/HRV-coherence mechanism; the "Source"/"perception organ" language is fieldguide's own cross-tradition synthesis vocabulary for a different, esoteric-content audience and should not appear in ALTER's spoken gratitude copy.

## C. THE JOBS of each beat (1-4 minute spoken flow)

Beat order matches `gratitudeBeat`'s existing 10 slots (per `BRIEF-gratitude.md` shape B): arrive, pick one, see it, trace the cause, own your part, feel it in the body, imagine its absence, notice the gap, let it return, land.

**A note on the "how long" answers below**: no source on disk specifies exact per-beat silence lengths for a spoken gratitude exercise. The one concrete number is Stutz's raw total — 30-60 seconds for his *entire* short-form tool. The relative proportions below (naming shorter than feeling; feeling the longest hold in the sequence) are inferred from which step each source spends the most instructional weight on, not from a study measuring optimal beat duration. Flagging this explicitly rather than presenting it as literature-derived.

1. **ARRIVE** — Listener is doing: a somatic down-shift, permission to stop scanning/performing (nothing to solve). Bad beat: jumping straight to "think of something" with no down-shift first — both Stutz's raw sequence and the HRV-coherence mechanism (B5) require the breath to slow before a felt response is even possible. Silence: short, ~3-5s, one breath's worth — this is a beat inside a flow, not a standalone induction.

2. **PICK ONE SPECIFIC THING** — Listener is doing: landing on ONE concrete moment, not scanning a list. End-state: a single image in mind (a look, a smell, a specific five minutes), not a category. Bad beat: list-making, or naming what "should" produce gratitude rather than what actually landed (positivity performance) — the exact generic-category failure B1/B6 independently flag. Silence: should be among the LONGER holds — Emmons's "always find something new" and Stutz's raw "feel a slight strain... coming up with new ideas" (B1, B5) both treat the search itself, including rejecting the first generic answer, as part of the mechanism.

3. **SEE IT** — Listener is doing: reconstructing the scene through their own eyes (where, who else, sensory detail) — not holding the label of it. Bad beat: staying at "yes, that happened" without the scene actually returning (remembering *that* it happened vs. remembering *it*). Silence: moderate, enough for one full sensory reconstruction.

4. **TRACE THE CAUSE** — Listener is doing: naming a real chain (someone's specific choice, a piece of luck, an action taken) — Emmons's anti-entitlement mechanism (B2) made concrete. Bad beat: skipping straight from noticing to feeling grateful with no cause named — that's savoring, not gratitude in Emmons's specific sense. Silence: short-to-moderate; a naming/cognitive beat, not a somatic one.

5. **OWN YOUR PART** — Listener is doing: locating their own agency inside the cause, without it tipping into self-congratulation. Bad beat: omitting it entirely (leaves gratitude passive/external) or overclaiming it (becomes pride, a different emotion). Silence: short; a precise correction, not a dwelling beat.

6. **FEEL IT IN THE BODY** — Listener is doing: locating a physical sensation and staying there without re-verbalizing. This is the step every source treats as the actual mechanism, not garnish (B5): Stutz's raw instruction is literally to *stop thinking* and focus on physical sensation; HRV-coherence requires several breath cycles at a slowed rate before a coherence pattern forms. Bad beat: naming the feeling and immediately advancing — cognitive labeling, not somatic dwelling, and the step most likely to get cut short in a rushed script. Silence: should be the LONGEST or tied-longest hold in the sequence — multiple breath cycles, not a 2-3 second beat.

7. **IMAGINE ITS ABSENCE** — Listener is doing: constructing a real counterfactual scene (this specific day, minus this specific thing) — not an abstract "could be worse." **Flag (per B4): this beat's underlying mechanism claim is unverified anywhere on disk.** Bad beat: a generic "imagine you didn't have this" without rebuilding the actual scene (the same specificity failure as beat 2, transplanted). Silence: needs to be real enough to register as a small loss — moderate, shorter than the felt-body beat since it's imaginative/cognitive work.

8. **NOTICE THE GAP** — Listener is doing: actually registering what beat 7 constructed as an absence, before it's given back. Bad beat: rushing past this into relief before the gap was felt at all, which would make beat 7 pointless. Silence: short, this is the pivot point, not a dwelling beat itself.

9. **LET IT RETURN** — Listener is doing: feeling the CONTRAST between the imagined gap and reality snapping back — where the "gift not given" reframe either lands or doesn't. Bad beat: skipping the contrast and just restating gratitude a second time (redundant with beat 6). Silence: short-moderate; a release/relief beat, quick by nature once the absence was real.

10. **LAND** — Listener is doing: nothing new, resting in an integrated, generalized version of the specific feeling. Bad beat: a summary/recap of what just happened (announces its own structure) or a moralizing wrap-up ("and that's why gratitude matters") — see D below, this is exactly where the current copy fails hardest. Silence: brief; hands off to whatever comes next.

**A structural note beyond the per-beat table**: beats 3, 4, 5, 8, and 9 are all doing fairly similar cognitive work (see the scene / name the cause / name your part / notice absence / feel it return) in five separate steps. Per `SCRIPT-ENGINE.md` PART 4.7 Law 3 (THE DOSE LAW — "explain a little better, never exhaustively"), the writer should treat 10 distinct beats as a ceiling to consider collapsing, not a fixed target to fill — this is a pacing question for the writer, not a mechanism finding.

## E. DAVID'S VOICE FOR SPOKEN SECOND-PERSON LINES (from CLERK-david.md)

1. **Second person, one reader, always "you"/"your"** — never a plural "you all." First person reserved for the guardian's own claims. *"I am the part of you that already knows who you are capable of being."*
2. **Zero em-dashes**, anywhere, no exceptions (project-wide rule, independently confirmed in the Tier-1 corpus).
3. **Zero exclamation points**, even at his warmest — restrained, never cheerleading.
4. **Short-to-medium declaratives**, rarely more than 2-3 clauses, almost no subordinate-clause stacking. Where longer, comma-chained equal-weight clauses: *"You miss one day, decide you have already failed, and quit."*
5. **The paired-short-sentence move** — a longer clause immediately followed by a 2-6 word fragment landing the point. *"That was me, coming back into range." / "That is a yes you can afford to make." / "I move first. The feeling follows." / "There is a space, and it's mine."*
6. **Contractions used naturally, but he favors "you have" over "you've"** more often than not — a register slightly more formal-but-spoken than text-speak, closer to narration than to texting.
7. **Ends on the plain claim, never an image bolted onto the last clause** (FLOURISH-ENDING is a named, hated failure). *"You reached into your own body and mind and changed them. That is the whole skill, and it is already yours."*
8. **Any image used must map 1:1 and stay consistent through the whole passage**, never switched mid-explanation. *"Your self-image is a thermostat. It pulls you back to whatever 'someone like me' does."*
9. **Concrete nouns only** — a reader can point to what's named (a body part, a number, a named person/book, an action), never an unexplained category noun like "the noise" or "your journey."
10. **The ungated check-in** — a question after a practice where both possible answers are pre-dignified. *"Did anything move? Even two percent, or an honest nothing."*
11. **Never a coy tease** — names the thing in the same breath it's raised, never "I want to show you..."
12. **Never a cross-sentence switcheroo** ("That's not X. That's Y.") — zero tolerance, his single angriest-worded kill ("the most heinous AI crime of all").

## F. REGISTER NOTES — the eyes-closed spoken register (COPY-ANCHORS "GUIDED-VOICE REGISTER LAW")

This register is narrower than, and partly *inverted* from, the general app-guardian voice above.

**Allowed:**
- Sounding like "any good meditation teacher" — David's own words: this is the TARGET, not a failure. The tradition's common tongue.
- Amalgamating MOVES from his named influences (Headspace warmth/permission, Sam Harris precision, Blackstone embodiment, Adyashanti effortlessness), phrased in the shared traditional language. Nuance ceiling ≈ Harris/Adyashanti, no higher.
- Generic TECHNIQUE (body-scan, count the breath, notice sounds, feel where an emotion sits, rest/let go) rendered in ALTER's own words — technique is free, expression is owned. For a copyrighted living teacher's *distinctive* framing specifically, sounding like the source is the failure, not the fix — original-but-plain beats distinctive-but-lifted.
- Simple language, because (David's own stated reasoning) "it talks to the subconscious more."

**Killed, even inside this permissive register:**
- Meditation-madness (mystical/weird framing for a beginner).
- Heady/clinical concepts — a full neuroscience-lecture register.
- Cross-sentence switcheroos; flourish-endings.
- **Writer-invented "clever" lines or novel images — explicitly and specifically the opposite of his normal preference.** His words: "I don't like when you come up with your own lines... I'd rather stick to the people who are good at it." This is the one register where his usual drive for originality reverses: traditional-simple is the goal, a distinctive ALTER-invented hook is the failure.
- Generic-category-marketing-beat ("your head clears") — still dead even here; "technique in our own words" is not the same thing as an empty marketing phrase.

**Direct implication for this rewrite**: unlike most ALTER screen copy, a spoken eyes-closed gratitude beat should not reach for ALTER-distinctive cleverness or a novel image at all. It should sound like it could play inside Calm, deploying Stutz's and Emmons's actual technique in ALTER's own plain words — never a metaphor invented to make the beat feel distinctively "ALTER." This also explains why the current copy's individual lines mostly pass (D above): they're already fairly plain. The failures are the sweeping-generalization close, the unsourced science-flavored claim, and two "we"-fillers — not reaching-for-cleverness in the way most other KILLED anchors describe.
