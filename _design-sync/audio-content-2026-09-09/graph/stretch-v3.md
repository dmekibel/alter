### B. Rewrites forced by a gate (source-faithful cue rephrased, never bypassed)
1. **Move 27, the biggest one.** The sweep's cue is "Push your hips back, **not down**, until you feel the back of that leg." The string `, not ` is a ZERO-TOLERANCE hit on copy-audit's negation-contrast regex. Rephrased to the positive form of the same correction: "Push your hips back and lift your chest." Lifting the chest is mechanically what "not down" is asking for, and it is the same hip-hinge cue the sweep sources to HOPT-HINGE. The where-you-feel-it half moved to move 28 so the pair still carries it.
2. **Six em-dashes deleted** (moves 10, 13, 19, 20, 22, 32). Dash is a ZERO tell. Each became a period or a plain "and", with no loss of instruction.
3. **Inert-bridge rule on three `B|` rows.** copy-density FAILS any invented line with no action verb from its whitelist, and the whitelist does not contain pull, press, step, turn, or circle. Moves 23, 24 and 26 were reworded to lead with "Reach" / "Keep" / "take", which are on it. This made 23 and 24 clearer, not worse: both now open by placing the arm before asking for the pull.
4. **Fragment halves made whole**, required by the equal-size law, since both halves now render the same size and are spoken joined. The sweep's "Same slow, easy weight." / "Only as far as feels easy, knees relaxed." / "A few forward, then reverse..." were continuation fragments left over from the old big-label-small-sub design.

### C. Information I added where the source's second line was empty
Tagged `S|` because the technique is the sweep's; the added clause is named here so the deletion-pass agent can cut it if it does not earn its 8 seconds.
- **Move 4** "keep your shoulders down" — the sweep's second half was "Same slow, easy weight", which repeats move 3 and teaches nothing new. Shoulder depression is the standard partner cue for lateral neck flexion (ASP-NECK's no-force rule).
- **Move 11** "keep both feet planted" — sweep had "same easy breath", pure repetition. Names what stays still, which is the side-bend's actual failure mode.
- **Move 37** "let the left hip open" — sweep had "Sit up tall again and breathe", which restates move 36's posture. Replaced with where you should feel it, per the brief's rule that line B adds information.
- **Move 50** "let your breathing slow" — sweep had "Arms by your sides. Stay here and breathe" (three sentences, and the last one empty). The slow-down IS the stated purpose of this beat (HARV1), so it is said plainly.
- **Move 14** "your hips stay put" — carried forward from 13's hips cue, which the sweep added to 13 and then dropped on 14.

### D. Source content I deliberately did NOT speak
- **Every seated alternate.** Per the brief, these live in the table column above and belong to the sitting-vs-standing setting, not to the voice. This includes move 32's "or stay seated tall if that's easier today", which the sweep had inside the spoken line.
- **The cat-cow neck warning** ("don't fling the head back into the arch"). Two sentences could not carry it. It is safe to omit here specifically because move 32 never cues a gaze lift at all: the line asks for belly-drop and round only, so the head-snap the warning exists to prevent is not being prompted. If the gaze lift is ever added back, the warning has to come with it.
- **Checkpoint markers** (the sweep's "first 6 / first 12 / first 24 / first 30" stopping points). Those are dose logic for the engine and screen, not spoken lines.
- **Rep counts on hold moves.** Spoken only where the movement is cyclical and the cycle is not self-evident: shoulder rolls, chin tuck, arm circles, both tendon glides, calf raise, cat-cow. Everywhere else the 8-second beat carries it.

## KNOWN OPEN QUESTIONS FOR THE SKEPTICS
1. **Move 20, the tabletop position.** "Straighten the fingers, big knuckles still bent" is the clearest eyes-closed phrasing I found for the 4-position clinical glide, and it is still the hardest line in the routine to execute blind. If a clarity skeptic can beat it, take theirs.
2. **Move 32 "round it toward the ceiling."** "It" refers to the back from the previous sentence. Vague-pronoun is a named KILLED pattern; I judged the antecedent close enough to be unambiguous when heard, but it is the one pronoun in the file worth a second look.
3. **Move 33** keeps the sweep's "A chair is fine if that's easier" in the spoken line rather than moving it to the seated column, because it is a transition instruction rather than a seated variant of a stretch. Adjudicate if that reads as an exception to the brief's rule.
4. **"Glute"** (moves 25, 26) is the sweep's own word and plain gym English, but it is the only near-anatomical term spoken in the routine.
