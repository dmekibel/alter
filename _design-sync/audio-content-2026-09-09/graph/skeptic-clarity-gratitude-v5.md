# CLARITY SKEPTIC — gratitude v5 (2026-09-16). The MISUNDERSTANDING lane.
One question per line: how could a real person, alone, eyes half-shut, first try, 7am, take this wrong — and what would they DO?
Sources held against: Stutz ch.5 THE GRATEFUL FLOW + FAQ 1 (raw part08.txt L504-620, L756-776), David's four verbatim instructions, COPY-ANCHORS 2026-09-16 + 16b + the STRUCTURAL LAW.
Verdicts across the 30 candidates: 3 CLEAR / 18 AMBIGUOUS / 9 MISLEADING. The set's single biggest defect is not a line, it is that **the eyes instruction is unclosed** and **"feel it" is never an action**.

1 | You'll think of a few things you're grateful for, one at a time. After each one, you close your eyes and feel it.
MISREADS: "You'll think of a few things" is heard as a batch task, so they start picking three or four now, during the explainer, and are already out of step when line 3 asks for one. "After each one, you close your eyes" quietly asserts the eyes are OPEN, which is never said.
GAP: never says eyes start open; never says silently; never says what "feel it" means; never says how many "a few" is.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The explainer must state the loop's two positions explicitly (eyes open while you find it, eyes closed while you feel it) and say the naming is silent, before any item is asked for.
2 | A few things, one at a time. Name it, then close your eyes and feel it.
MISREADS: "Name it" is the standard English for SAY IT OUT LOUD; a person alone says it aloud, and a person beside a sleeping partner opts out of the tool. Also "a few things" then "Name IT" leaves a singular pronoun with no singular antecedent.
GAP: the word Stutz puts first — silently — is absent here and in every spoken line.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The compressed explainer must carry the silence rule, since it is the only line a stack listener hears.
3 | Think of one thing you're grateful for. It can be small, like a smile of a friend, or the sun hitting your face.
MISREADS: "small" is taken as "minor / doesn't really count", so they deliberately pick something they feel nothing for, then fail the feel beat and conclude the tool does not work on them. Both examples are from today, so they also infer the window is today only, and have to re-scope at line 10.
GAP: Stutz's one protective rule is missing — pick what you are TRULY grateful for, never what you feel you SHOULD be. Scope (anything from your whole life, per David) is never stated.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The first ask must say the thing can come from anywhere in your life and must be one you actually feel, not one that sounds right.
4 | Or big, like someone who waited up for you.
MISREADS: the "or" arrives after they already chose. They either drop the item they had started feeling and hunt for a big one, or hold two and are a beat ahead when line 5 says "feel grateful for IT". "Someone who waited up for you" is a specific autobiography many listeners do not have, so they search their memory for that scene and stall.
GAP: never says pick ONE of the two, and never says the example is a size, not a required event.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: Small-or-big must be offered inside one breath before the choice is made, not as an amendment after it.
5 | Now close your eyes, and feel grateful for it. Not the thought of it. The feeling.
MISREADS: "Not the thought of it" is a negative instruction with no positive action attached, so they try to stop thinking about the thing while feeling grateful for it — the one move that cannot be done. They strain, nothing comes, they decide they are bad at this. This is also the sentence-split switcheroo silhouette.
GAP: no action (Stutz: go slowly enough to feel the VALUE of it), no location (from the heart, chest softens), no duration.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: The first feel beat must give one doable move that produces the feeling — the value of the thing, or its absence — and say where in the body to look.
6 | Now another one. Something you'd only notice if it stopped. Hot water this morning, or your key turning in your own door.
MISREADS: "Something you'd only notice if it stopped" is a riddle. Eyes closed, they solve for the CATEGORY instead of finding a real gratitude, which lands them on exactly the should-be-grateful items Stutz warns against. "Your own door" also asserts housing they may not have, at 7am.
GAP: no cue to open the eyes and come back to thinking; the riddle replaces the criterion (does this move me) with a puzzle (does this qualify).
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The take-for-granted ask must be phrased as a place to look, not a test the item has to pass.
7 | Close your eyes again, and feel that one.
MISREADS: "again" is the first admission that the eyes were open, and nothing ever told them to open. Someone who kept them shut hears this and concludes they have been doing it wrong since item one. Someone who opened them only guessed right.
GAP: the missing open-your-eyes instruction breaks the set here, at item TWO, not item three.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: Every return to naming needs its own eyes-open cue, or the whole eyes structure must be dropped and stated once.
8 | Now something bad that missed you. A pain that stopped by lunch, or a message you dreaded that never came.
MISREADS: read as an invitation to scan for near-misses, so the search itself surfaces the dread and they spend the beat back inside the bad thing — the exact state Stutz built this tool to interrupt. "A pain that stopped by lunch" is also yesterday, when the previous two asks were this morning.
GAP: Stutz's version is a present-tense ABSENCE (I'm grateful I'm not in a war zone), which is safe; a past near-miss is not, and nothing warns against dwelling in the bad half.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: This ask must name something absent from your life right now, never a bad thing you narrowly escaped.
9 | Feel it the same way.
MISREADS: the nearest antecedent to "it" is "a message you dreaded", so the sentence literally parses as feel the dreaded message — and some will. "The same way" points at a method that was never given; both prior feel beats named the target and no method.
GAP: there is no "way" on the record to repeat.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: A repeat feel cue can only say "the same way" once an actual method has been taught, and must re-anchor the object it means.
10 | Now go back years. Someone who stayed with you on a bad night and never mentioned it again.
MISREADS: the example is a whole scene, so they hunt for a match to THAT story rather than for anything old they are grateful for; not finding one, they manufacture a near-fit they feel nothing for.
GAP: no feel beat follows before line 11 interrupts with a rule, so the NAME to FEEL loop silently breaks on the ask that most needs it.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The past ask must open a time window plainly and let the example stay an example, with its own feel beat intact.
11 | Make it one you've never named before. Digging for a new one is part of this.
MISREADS: it lands AFTER they chose, so it reads as a retroactive disqualification: throw away what you just found and dig again, mid-practice, eyes closed. "Never named before" is also vacuous on a first run (they have never named anything) and reads as never-in-your-life on the second.
GAP: Stutz's real rule is don't reuse the SAME items each time you use the tool; "part of this" also has no referent.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: The freshness rule belongs before the ask, scoped to previous sessions, and framed as why the effort is wanted.
12 | Last one, and it's the biggest. You woke up this morning. You're alive.
MISREADS: this is the only ask that does not ask — it hands them the item. Four beats trained them to produce an answer, so some keep searching for their own biggest thing and miss that it was given. At a two-item dose, "the biggest" tops a ladder that was never climbed.
GAP: no instruction attached at all; the feel is deferred to line 13, which presumes they already started.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The climax must still be an instruction — point them at it and tell them to take it in — not a statement of fact.
13 | Stay with that one a little longer.
MISREADS: "longer" than what, and until when? With eyes closed and no clock there is no criterion, so in tap mode they tap early, and in auto mode they wait passively for the voice.
GAP: never says to close the eyes for this one (line 12 didn't), and gives no end condition.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The longest hold needs its own eyes cue and something to do inside it, not just more time.
14 | Those were the logical reasons. They remind your body what gratitude feels like.
MISREADS: "logical" is a label they never heard while doing it, so it reads as a downgrade — that part didn't count. At the 30s dose only ONE ask plays and "Those" is plural pointing at a set of one; the whole premise (a stack of reasons built a feeling) is simply false at short doses.
GAP: David's phrasing carries the causal frame "we USED the first five logical reasons TO REMIND you what gratitude feels like in your body"; splitting it into a bare label plus a separate claim loses the method.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The turn's first beat must state the purpose of what just happened in one causal sentence, and must be true when only one or two items played.
15 | Now feel that same gratitude with no reason under it.
MISREADS: "with no reason under it" is a spatial metaphor for a logical relation, so the doable-looking action is to SUPPRESS the reason — push away the thing they just spent a minute feeling grateful for. Suppression kills the feeling, the feeling goes, they fail.
GAP: Stutz's mechanism is the opposite — STOP THE WORDS and let the heart keep generating the same feeling; a continuation, not a subtraction. David's "TRY to feel" also gave permission to not get it; we cut it.
VERDICT: MISLEADING
FIX-DIRECTION: The turn must instruct them to stop the naming and let the feeling keep running on its own, with explicit permission that it may not.
16 | Put your hands on your heart if you like.
MISREADS: a physical change arriving after the hardest instruction becomes a distraction from the feeling they are straining to hold; and it is the set's only body pointer, spent as an afterthought.
GAP: never connects the hands to where the feeling is supposed to be, which is the one thing it could teach.
VERDICT: CLEAR
FIX-DIRECTION: Keep it optional but place it where it does work — as the pointer to the chest, before or inside the turn.
17 | If you felt almost nothing, that's normal the first time. Feeling it is a skill, and it comes in stronger every time you practice.
MISREADS: "stronger every time" is a per-session promise. Run two will not reliably beat run one; when it doesn't, the app is caught being wrong and the whole tool loses credit. Stutz's FAQ deliberately says it may take MANY uses.
GAP: arrives after the turn, so someone who felt nothing at item one spent the entire practice believing they were broken; and it offers no fallback.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: Say it early, promise a direction over many sessions rather than improvement every time, and give the FAQ's off-ramp: just name a few and go slowly, that alone works.
18 | Later today, the first time you catch yourself replaying an argument, name two things and go to the feeling.
MISREADS: "name two things" on a bus reads as out loud, because silence was never established in the spoken set. "Go to the feeling" assumes they have one; the person who felt nothing has no destination.
GAP: Stutz's cue is ANY negative thought — worry, self-criticism, judgment, complaining, obsessing. Narrowing it to replaying an argument means the worry that actually hits them won't trigger the tool.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The transfer line must name the general cue (the moment a dark thought starts) and carry the silence rule with it.
19 | Stay with that, and keep breathing the way you are.
MISREADS: "that" could be the no-reason feeling or the hands on the heart. As a stack bridge it reads like an ending, so they open their eyes and sit up, and the next act starts with their posture broken.
GAP: solo, no breath was ever established, so this calls attention to breathing for the first time on the way out.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS
FIX-DIRECTION: The stack close must say the practice continues into what is next, and only mention the breath when a breath act preceded it.
20 | Open your eyes when you're ready.
MISREADS: none as a sentence. Structurally it is the only open-your-eyes in the entire set against five closes, which confirms the copy's own model is eyes-shut-throughout and contradicts lines 1, 2, 5 and 7.
GAP: nothing here; the gap is everywhere above it.

21 | Silently, in your own words.
MISREADS: "in your own words" invites WORDING, so they start composing a sentence and spend the pause drafting instead of feeling. As a sub-hint it also has no verb, so heard alone it is a fragment.
GAP: this is the only place silence is stated, and it is on the weakest surface — in a stack there is no screen at all, so the silence rule never reaches a stack listener.
VERDICT: AMBIGUOUS

MISREADS: "before the next one" sets a deadline, so the pause becomes a race; in auto mode they do not know how long they have, and hurrying is the one thing that prevents the feeling.

## THE FIVE CHECKS

**1. THE EYES — the set is structurally incoherent, and it breaks at item TWO.**
Evidence: "close your eyes" at 1, 2, 5, 7 ("again"), 22. "Open your eyes" appears exactly ONCE, line 20, at the very end. Nothing between item 1 and the close ever says to open them. Line 7's "again" presupposes an opening that was never instructed, so the contradiction lands one item earlier than feared. What the person actually does: closes their eyes at line 1 or 5 and never opens them again; every later "close your eyes" then reads either as noise or as proof they have been doing it wrong. Consequence for David's own STRUCTURAL LAW: the NAME then CLOSE then FEEL rhythm never happens in a real run, because there is no eyes-open naming phase to close from. The copy teaches a two-position loop and delivers a one-position experience.

**2. SILENT OR ALOUD — never established on the surface that matters.**
Evidence: the only statement is line 21, "Silently, in your own words", a SCREEN SUB-HINT. Per engine-context-ledger.md the spoken pools are open / first / items / feel / noReason / close — sub-hints are a different surface, and in a stack there is no screen being read at all, so a stack listener is never told. Against that, the word "name" carries its ordinary aloud sense at lines 2, 6 (implied), 12, 18 and 25. Stutz's instruction is first and explicit: "Say them to yourself silently." Ours is last, weakest, and outvoted five to one.

**3. THE FEELING — assumed, never taught; and defined only by negation.**
Evidence: our four feel beats are "feel grateful for it. Not the thought of it. The feeling." (5), "feel that one" (7), "Feel it the same way" (9), "Stay with that one a little longer" (13). Every one names the target; not one names an action. Stutz gives two concrete actions we never use: (a) go "slowly enough to feel the VALUE of each one", reinforced by his note that these minor items are ones "you might not notice unless they were taken away" — the value move is the actual mechanism that converts a fact into a feeling; and (b) a location — gratefulness "flowing upward, directly from your heart", "your chest will soften and open". Our set's only body reference is the optional hands at line 16. So the answer is: assumed. And "Not the thought of it. The feeling." is the same defect David killed in v4 ("that doesn't explain the instruction well enough") — it names the target and drops the verb.

**4. THE TURN — does not land, and is false at short doses.**
Evidence (comprehension): "with no reason under it" is a spatial metaphor for a logical relation; the only action it suggests is suppressing the reason, which destroys the feeling. Stutz's mechanism is continuation — stop the WORDS, the heart keeps generating the same gratefulness. David's fuller framing supplies three things our two lines cut: the causal frame ("we USED the first five logical reasons TO REMIND you what gratitude feels like IN YOUR BODY"), the permission verb ("TRY to feel"), and the matched term "logical reason" on both sides of the turn. Evidence (counts): the engine plays 1 to 5 asks and everything past 120s in a stack goes to the feel holds, never to more items, so a 30s slot plays ONE ask — at which point "Those were the logical reasons" is a plural pointing at a set of one, and the premise that a stack of reasons built a feeling is simply untrue. Removing the numeral in v4 hid the symptom; the premise is still false.

**5. THE PACING SETTING — a choice made before the thing being chosen exists.**
Missing, named: (a) the QUESTION LINE itself ("How should the pauses work?") is in pacing-setting.md but absent from the candidate set, so two bare cards appear with no prompt; (b) the footer "You can change this any time" is likewise absent, so the choice reads as permanent; (c) the greyed stack line is absent, so nothing tells the user this setting does not apply in a stack; (d) the two cards are in different grammatical persons, leaving "I" ambiguous; (e) neither card names the consequence that actually decides it — auto means you never touch the phone and your eyes stay closed, tap means you open your eyes and reach for the screen at every beat; (f) per the spec this is shown on FIRST RUN, before the person has ever experienced a pause in this tool, so they are choosing between two descriptions of a thing they cannot picture and nothing tells them what a pause here even is.

## THE THREE A FIRST-TIMER IS MOST LIKELY TO GET WRONG
1. **Line 9, "Feel it the same way."** Nearest antecedent is "a message you dreaded", so it parses as feel the dreaded message; and "the same way" points at a method never given.
2. **Line 15, "Now feel that same gratitude with no reason under it."** Reads as suppress the reason, which extinguishes the feeling; Stutz's move is to stop the words and let it continue.
3. **Line 11, "Make it one you've never named before."** Retroactive disqualification mid-practice, eyes closed, of an item already chosen — and vacuous on a first run.
(Runner-up: line 7's "Close your eyes again", the point where the eyes contradiction becomes visible to the user.)

## WHAT THE SOURCE TEACHES THAT OUR SET NEVER TEACHES AT ALL
1. **Silently** — Stutz's first rule ("Say them to yourself silently"). We have it only as a screen sub-hint.
2. **The sentence stem** — "I'm grateful for my eyesight; I'm grateful I have hot water." A form to fill, so nobody has to compose anything.
3. **Slowly enough to feel the VALUE of each one** — the actual mechanism of the feel beat; entirely absent.
4. **Where it lives in the body** — flowing upward from the HEART, the CHEST softening and opening. We have one optional hands cue and no location.
5. **Truly, not should** — "Stick to things you're truly grateful for, not things you feel you should be grateful for." This is the single rule that would defuse the riddle-solving failure our lines 6 and 8 cause.
6. **The turn's real mechanic** — stop the WORDS and the heart keeps generating gratefulness (continuation), with Stutz's rough duration ("about thirty seconds" of naming) to bound the phase. We teach subtraction and give no duration.
7. **The FAQ off-ramp** — "If all of this feels beyond you, just pick out five items you feel grateful for, go slowly enough to fully feel each one. That part of the tool alone will give you a potent weapon." The person who fails the turn currently has nowhere to land.
8. **The cue is ANY negative thought** — worry, self-criticism, judgment, complaining, obsessing. Line 18 narrows it to replaying an argument.
9. **The ladder** — mechanically name, then feel while naming, then wordless. engine-context-ledger.md already tiers by familiarity; no line states the rung.
