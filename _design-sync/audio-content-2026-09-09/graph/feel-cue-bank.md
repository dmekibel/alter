So there has to be a distinction for that."

THE DISTINCTION, which is the point of this file: a cue must match what the ask actually handed the listener. Some asks produce a MEMORY (a moment, a person, a scene). Some produce a FACT you hold right now (your eyesight, hot water, a problem you do not have, being alive). A memory can be stepped into. A fact cannot, and telling someone to step into their eyesight is nonsense.

## FAMILY A — memory cues (only after an ask that produced a moment or a person)
| Cue | Mechanism | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Now close your eyes. Go back into that moment like you are there again, and feel grateful for it now. | Re-enter the memory as present | Robbins 04:43, his central move |
| Close your eyes. See their face, hear their voice, and let the gratitude come with it. | One sensory channel, so the memory has a handle | Robbins 05:13, whose own cue names three channels; we use one per our rule-of-three law |

## Assignment
- ask 1, one thing, small like a friend's smile or the sun on your face → FAMILY B (his own examples are things as often as moments, so a memory cue is unsafe here)
- ask 2, something you take for granted → FAMILY B
- ask 3, a problem you do not have → FAMILY B (nothing to picture at all, so acknowledge it)
- ask 4, someone from years back → FAMILY A (always a memory, always a person)
- ask 5, you woke up and you are alive → FAMILY C
Within a family the engine rotates across runs, so the practice keeps teaching instead of chanting. It never crosses families.
