## settle — Settle (foundation)

**entry:** Find a position you can stay in for the next few minutes, without needing to change it. Sitting upright is good. Lying down is fine too. Put your hands down on your thighs and leave them there. When you're ready, let your eyes close on the next out-breath, and stop moving from there on.

**entry (stacked):** Nothing more is being asked of your body now, so leave it exactly where it is. If your eyes are still open, let them close on the next out-breath. Stop adjusting your position, including the small corrections you make without noticing. The body is done working now, so keep it still through the next few breaths.

1. [beginner] (Headspace+Blackstone+Harris) Stop using effort to hold yourself up, because whatever is underneath you is already doing that work. Now find the lowest place where you press down into that surface, under your seat or your heels. Stay with that one place while several breaths come and go. You're not trying to change the pressure or make it heavier, only to find where it is.

2. [beginner] (Headspace+Harris) Now take one reading of the body all at once, instead of going through it part by part. See whether it feels heavy this morning or light, and whether it's already settled or still wound up. Take the first answer, and don't go looking for a better one. Nothing there needs fixing, and finding yourself wound up is a normal way to start.

3. [intermediate] (Blackstone+Harris) Now take your attention all the way down into your feet, and get inside them. Be in your feet, not looking at them from your head. Feel the soles, the heels and the toes from the inside, and stay there while several breaths come and go. Each in-breath will tug your attention back up toward your chest, where the breathing is strongest. Let the breath rise on its own, and keep your attention down in your feet.

4. [intermediate] (Blackstone+Spero+Robbins) If your thinking is loud right now, don't try to quiet it down first. Let the thinking carry on in the background, and put your attention on the feeling of breathing instead. Or put one hand flat on your chest and leave it there. Feel your hand rise and fall, and come back to that movement whenever you drift off.

5. [advanced] (Headspace) Settling might take one breath this morning, or it might take twenty. The more often you sit down like this, the sooner your body starts doing this part on its own. If you're not settled yet, stay with the pressure underneath you for a few more breaths. Then begin the rest of the sit from wherever you've got to.

## breath — Breath (foundation)

**entry:** Now put your attention on the breath, and hold it in one place. Start at the nostrils, where the air passes in and out. If you cannot feel it there, drop down to where the belly moves instead. Whichever place you find, that is your place for this sit. Feel the movement there, and leave the rest of your body alone for now.

**entry (stacked):** Your attention has been spread across the whole body. Bring it in now, and hold it in one place. Go to the nostrils first, where the air passes in and out. If you cannot feel it there, drop down to where the belly moves instead. Whichever place you find, that is your place for this sit.

1. [beginner] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) You do not have to steer the breath, or make it deeper. Let the next one arrive exactly as it comes, long or short. Then watch for the moment you start steering it, deeper or longer than it wanted. Look for that moment on purpose, because it is easy to miss. When you catch it, stop steering and let the next breath come by itself.

2. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Pick one in-breath and follow the whole of it. Catch it at the first moment you feel anything move, and keep feeling until the movement stops on its own. Do not stretch it out to make it last longer. You will lose it partway sometimes. Let that breath go, and start again at the beginning of the next one.

3. [beginner] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) Stay at the place you picked, and read the size of this breath. Notice whether it is a big one, or hardly anything at all. Leave it exactly as it is, because reading it is the whole job here. Then read the next breath the same way, and the one after that. Across a few of them you will notice it changing on its own.

4. [intermediate] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Now follow the whole cycle, in and then out, and stay on past the end of the out-breath. There is a stretch there where almost nothing moves, and attention usually slips away in it. Keep feeling through that stretch, and do not hold anything or hurry it along. Your body will start the next breath without you. Do the same on the next three or four.

5. [intermediate] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) At the end of the next out-breath, stop and check where your attention is. Do not wait until you have already drifted off somewhere. If it is on the breath, stay there. If it has moved somewhere else, name where, and put it back. Run that check again at the end of every few breaths.

6. [intermediate] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Find the strongest thing your body is doing apart from the breath. It might be a tight jaw, or a pull in one shoulder. Find its exact place, and how big an area it covers. Now hold two places at once, that one and your breathing place, each staying where it is. Keep both for a few breaths, without dropping one to hold the other.

7. [intermediate] (HARRIS+BLACKSTONE+SPERO) Leave the breath and go to the loudest place in your body. Put your attention inside it and find its edges. Notice its temperature, and whether it holds still or keeps changing. If it gets too strong, go back to your breathing place. Otherwise stay inside it until it eases, then pick the breath up again.

8. [intermediate] (HARRIS+BLACKSTONE) Drop the word breathing, and feel only what moves. Start at the rim of your nostrils, cool on the way in and warmer on the way out. Stay on that one patch of skin for several breaths. Then move down to the ribs and feel them widen. Then the belly, pushing out against your clothes. Keep it at the level of sensation, and go back to the nostrils the moment it turns into a picture.

9. [advanced] (ROBBINS+HARRIS) Sit up taller, and lengthen the back of your neck. Do that whenever the breath has gone soft and hard to find. Your spine has settled down while you sat, and you want some of that height back. Then go to your place and feel for the smallest movement you can still pick up. Look for detail, and do not sink deeper into the softness.

10. [advanced] (BLACKSTONE) Go inside the place you picked, and be in there. Put yourself under the skin of it, and stay there while several breaths move through. Feel the movement from in there, without stepping back to look at it. You will slip out and start watching it from a step away. Move back in each time.

11. [advanced] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Now widen out from your one place until you can feel the whole body breathing. The ribs open, the belly moves, the shoulders lift a little. Hold all of that at the same time, without going round it part by part. Follow one breath the whole way like that, from its first movement to its last. Then let the next one come the same way.

12. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HEADSPACE) For the next few breaths, stop working at this. The work is the checking and the small pressure to get it right. Put that down, and leave the breath to do what it does. Then watch whether your attention stays with it on its own. If it wanders off, pick the effort back up and carry on. If it stays, leave it alone and let it stay.

## count — Count (foundation)

**entry:** Now count the breath, and say each number silently, in your head. One as you breathe in, two as you breathe out, then one again. ## note — Note (foundation)

**entry:** This next part of the practice is called noting. From here you leave the breath behind, and wait for whatever turns up on its own. **entry (stacked):** For the last few minutes the breath was your one thing to stay with. This next part is noting, and it takes that one thing away. 1. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Sound comes first, the easiest of the three to catch. Wait for the next one, and stay with it the whole way through. 3. [beginner] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Now the body, and you wait until one sensation stands out on its own. Maybe a small ache in your back, or the pull of your clothes on one shoulder. Put your attention inside that spot, in there with the sensation, not out here looking at it. From in there, notice whether it holds steady or changes. Then say the word feeling, and that one is finished.

4. [beginner] (SPERO+HEADSPACE) Some of the time the body goes quiet and no sensation stands out at all. When that happens, go back to sound for a while. There is always something going on in the sound, even if it is only the hum of the room. Stay there until a sensation turns up in the body again.

5. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Thoughts come through here as well, and they get a word like everything else. A thought is usually a few words spoken in your head, or a quick picture of something. When one shows up, the word is thinking, said once and no louder than the others. You do not follow it, and you do not push it away. Then go back to waiting until the next thing arrives.

6. [intermediate] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Now aim at the beginning of a thought, at the moment it first starts up. Most of them are already running by then. Take those late ones anyway, with the same word on them. If the beginnings keep slipping past, go back to sound. Catch a few sounds starting there, then bring the same aim back to thoughts.

7. [intermediate] (HARRIS+SHINZEN) From here all three are open, in whatever order they arrive. Sound gets the word hearing, the body gets feeling, and a thought gets thinking. One word each, said once and quietly, and then you are finished with that one. Give the next thing its own word, and then the one after that.

8. [intermediate] (SHINZEN+HARRIS) Now take whichever one is loudest at that moment, from any of the three. A car outside, an ache in your back, a thought about the rest of the day ahead. Give it its word, and leave it exactly as it is. Then wait for the next loudest thing to arrive. Sometimes two of them land together, and it makes no difference which one you take. Let the other one go by without a word on it.

9. [intermediate] (SHINZEN+HARRIS) Now go for the moment a sound stops. Pick a sound that is still going, and stay with it to the end. The word here is gone, said the moment the sound is no longer there. Aim that word at the ending, not a second after it. If it lands late, aim at the next one.

10. [intermediate] (SHINZEN+HEADSPACE) After a sound stops, a stretch of quiet sits there before the next thing arrives. You take that stretch the same way you take a sound. When the next sound ends, the word is space. Stay in that quiet until something new arrives, however long that takes. Some of these last barely a moment, so take the short ones too.

11. [advanced] (SHINZEN+ADYASHANTI) Now cut each word down to its first syllable. Hearing shortens to hear, feeling to feel, thinking to think. No naming now, and no picking one out over another. If your attention scatters, the word goes back on the next thing that arrives. Carry on with that word for as long as you need it.

## scan — Body (foundation)

**entry:** Now scan the body, starting at the top of your head and moving down to your feet. A place is about the size of your hand, so your forehead is one and your jaw is the next. **entry (stacked):** Until now you have been doing things to the body. Scanning only reads the body, and changes nothing in it. Start at the top of your head and work down to your feet. A place is about the size of your hand, so your forehead is one and your jaw is the next. Stop at each place long enough to find out what is there.

1. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) At each place you take three readings, and weight is the first one. Notice whether the place is heavy or light, and take the first answer that comes. Then read the temperature in the same place, warm or cool. Last, read whether the place is tight or easy. Take all three at every place, and keep going right down to your feet.

2. [intermediate] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Now slow the scan down, and stay in one place for a whole breath. Follow the breath all the way in and all the way out, without leaving that place. The breath is only measuring the time here, and the place is what you are reading. Some places show you nothing until the second breath, so give those two.

3. [intermediate] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) When the scan reaches your hands, go down inside them. You are not looking at them from behind your eyes, because you are inside the palms and the fingers. That move is called inhabiting, and it is a different act from observing. Do the same at every place from here down, and get inside it first.

4. [intermediate] (HEADSPACE+BLACKSTONE) Sooner or later you will find a place that is gripped and will not let go. Stay in there with it and read it, the same as every other place. You do not have to loosen it, and you are not here to loosen it. A tight place that gets noticed will often let go later, on its own. Read it, and carry on down the body.

5. [intermediate] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) Let the pace follow what you find. Move quickly through a place that is already easy. Slow right down where a place is holding on, and give that one two or three breaths. Finish the easy ones in one breath, and save the time for the held ones.

6. [advanced] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Now go finer, and take the small places the first scan went straight past. Go into the small muscles around your eyes. Go into your tongue, wherever it is resting in your mouth. Go into the back of your neck, where it meets the skull. Stay in each one until something comes back, and two breaths is usually enough.

7. [advanced] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) Now take one more scan down the body, quicker than the first one. You are checking what has changed since you were last in each place. A place that read tight the first time may read the same now. Another one will have let go on its own, without being asked. Notice the places that let go, and leave them alone as you go past.

## listen — Sounds (foundation)

**entry:** Now take your attention off the body and put it on sound. You don't have to listen for anything, because the hearing is already happening on its own. ## feel — Feeling (foundation)

**entry:** Whatever you are feeling right now shows up somewhere in the body. Find the one place where the feeling is strongest, and put your attention there. If you cannot find a feeling at all, use the plainest sensation in you instead. Stay a few breaths and feel how big that place is. **entry (stacked):** You just let the whole body go soft, and a feeling does not go with it. Find the one place that did not go soft, and put your attention there. 1. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Stay in that one place, and feel the sensation on its own. Pressure, heat, tightness, a dull weight that does not move. 2. [intermediate] (BLACKSTONE+HEADSPACE) Up to now you have been looking at this sensation from just outside it. Move your attention into it, and feel it from in there. 3. [beginner] (SPERO+HEADSPACE) If the feeling is more than you want to hold, go somewhere else for a while. Leave it exactly where it is. 4. [intermediate] (HARRIS+BLACKSTONE) Put your attention inside the sensation and hold it there without a break. Check its size against what it was when you found it. 5. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HARRIS) See whether part of you is waiting for this feeling to be over. That waiting is doing work, and you cannot switch it off by deciding to. **entry:** Now for something different from watching. For the next few minutes you go looking for the one who is doing the watching. There is no answer to get right here. If this gets uncomfortable, or you start to feel far away from yourself, open your eyes and go back to the breath. That is a good way to finish, and it counts as the whole practice.

1. [beginner] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) Wait for the next thought, and try to catch it at the moment it starts. You did not order that thought and you did not build it, and a second earlier there was no sign of it. Stay with it while it runs, all the way to where it ends. It ends on its own, and you did nothing to end it. Most of the time you will notice a thought already halfway through, and halfway through counts. Wait again, and take the next thought from wherever you catch it.

2. [intermediate] (HARRIS+SPERO) Now listen to the next thought the way you are already hearing the sounds in the room. A sound arrives, you hear it, it goes, and you never become the sound. Now bring in one small thing that has been bothering you this week. Keep it small, and stay away from the heavy ones today. Let that annoyance arrive the way a sound does, and hold it there for about five breaths. Then let it pass. If it gets stronger than mild, open your eyes and go back to the breath.

3. [advanced] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) You have a strong sense of looking out from somewhere, usually a point behind the eyes. Point to that place now with one finger, then let your hand come down. Move your attention inside the place you pointed at, instead of looking at it from outside. Be in there, and feel around the inside of it for an edge. See whether it has a border, or whether there is looking going on with no place it starts from. Do not take the first answer that arrives in words. If this gets uncomfortable, or you start to feel far away from yourself, open your eyes and go back to the breath.

4. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HARRIS) You feel like the thinker behind your thoughts, the one deciding what comes next. Look for that thinker now, while the thoughts are still arriving. Is there anybody back there, or is there only the next thought, and then the next? Ask that once, and then keep looking with your attention while no answer comes. Your mind will hand you an easier question to work on instead, and it will sound worth doing. Leave that question hanging, and keep looking for the thinker. If this gets uncomfortable, or you start to feel far away from yourself, open your eyes and go back to the breath.

5. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HEADSPACE) One last question. Look for the center of all this, the one you call I, the thing every sentence in your head is about. Look where it should be, and keep looking past the first answers that arrive in words. If you find nothing where the center was supposed to be, that is the result, and it is the expected one. There is nothing to conclude from it and nothing to do with it. You can put the looking down now, and rest as the awareness all of it was happening in. Then when you are ready, feel your feet, take one full breath, and come back into the room.

## being — Rest (advanced)

**entry:** This one is called resting, and it asks for less than any other practice here. There's nothing to follow here, and no place you're supposed to put your attention. When you catch yourself working at this, stop the working and stay where you are. Thoughts carry on the whole time by themselves, and not one of them is yours to sort out. Whatever turns up in the next few minutes, you can leave all of it alone.

**entry (stacked):** You settled your body a few minutes ago, and now your attention is the last thing still working. These next few minutes are where your attention stops working too. Up to now you've had something to put it on, and from here there's nothing. Leave it wherever it already is, and stop moving it around. When you catch yourself working at this, stop the working and stay where you are.

1. [beginner] (BLACKSTONE+ADYASHANTI) Behind your closed eyelids, your eyes are still pointing at something, so let them stop pointing and sit still. Do the same thing with your attention now, and stop holding it on anything at all. Stop moving it around to check on things, because the checking is the work you're putting down. Wherever your attention lands next is the right place, and you don't have to keep it there. If it drifts somewhere else, that's the right place too, and there's nothing here you're meant to find.

2. [beginner] (HEADSPACE+ADYASHANTI) You'll pick something up in here, a thought or a plan, and you'll be carrying it before you notice. The moment you notice, stop working on it and leave it where it is, without naming it first. There's no breath to come back to in this one, so noticing and stopping is the whole return. You don't reach for anything else on the way back, because there's nothing here to take hold of instead. You'll do this many times in a few minutes, and each of those is the practice working.

3. [intermediate] (ADYASHANTI+HARRIS) Listen for the question that asks whether you're doing this right, in whatever words it uses today. When it turns up, leave it unanswered, and don't reassure yourself about it either. It will come back, and the second time it will sound more reasonable than the first. Leave that one alone as well, and the one after it. Not answering is the whole move here, so each time it comes back you get another go. That question comes from the part of you that likes to get things right. There's nothing in here to get right, so it has no work to do.

4. [intermediate] (SPERO+HEADSPACE) If you've been at this a while and you're still working at it, stop trying to make yourself stop. Look for one place in you that already feels comfortable, and name where it is to yourself. Put your attention there and keep it there for the next while. The feeling that this isn't working can stay right where it is, and you can leave it alone. Check every so often that you are still at the comfortable place, because attention slides back by itself. If nothing in you feels comfortable right now, let this one go and stay however you are.

5. [intermediate] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Bring your attention to the base of your skull, where your head sits on top of your neck. Go inside that place instead of looking at it from outside, so you're in there and not beside it. That's what inhabiting a part of the body means, and it's a different act from watching it. Stay in there through several breaths, and let whatever is in there do whatever it does. You're not softening it and you're not waiting for it to change, because being in there is the whole instruction.

6. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HARRIS) Find the one thing you're still quietly trying to make happen, and put it into a few words for yourself. Most often it turns out to be wanting the mind to go quiet. Sometimes it's wanting these minutes to feel a certain way instead. Whatever you find, stop working to bring it about, starting now. You can want it and stop working for it at the same time, and that's what's being asked here. None of your working in the next few minutes was going to produce it anyway. If you can't find anything you're pushing for, leave it, and carry on as you were.

7. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HEADSPACE) Sooner or later a thought turns up with something useful to work out. It promises the resting gets easier once that one thing is settled. Recognize this one by the promise, which is the only reliable way to tell it apart. Then leave it where it is, and don't start on it. If you've already started on it, stop partway and leave the rest. You lose nothing at all by leaving a thought sitting in the middle. It'll sound reasonable and it may be a good idea for later, and later is where it goes.

8. [advanced] (HARRIS+HEADSPACE) The next time you get the urge to shift your position, stay exactly as you are. What you're following is the ordinary restlessness that wants a small adjustment. Stay with it from the first moment you feel it until it has gone completely. It arrives, it gets stronger for a while, and it goes down again on its own. If something hurts, that's a different thing, so move and settle again. If no urge comes while you're waiting, there's nothing to do here, and you can leave this one.

9. [advanced] (ADYASHANTI+HEADSPACE) Whenever you catch a few seconds that had no effort in them, don't try to get back to them. Don't try to make the next one longer either, because reaching for more is what ends it. A few seconds is the real thing here, and a longer stretch is not a better version of it. How often you come back is what matters, and the length looks after itself. So let each stretch be as short as it is, and let these few minutes be made of short ones.

## bliss — Bliss (advanced)

**entry:** Now go looking for a good feeling somewhere in your body. Ordinary comfort counts as one, and most days that is exactly what it is. The warmth of your hands, or the easy part of the out-breath. Or the place where your body touches whatever you are resting on. Take the first one you find and put your attention on it. **entry (stacked):** Your body is already softer than it was when you started. Your breathing has already settled into its own pace, so leave it alone. Take the first one you find, and put your attention there. If two places feel good, take the clearer one and stay with it.

1. [beginner] (ROBBINS+SPERO) Go back to a time you felt genuinely good. Pick a single moment, not a whole good year. Put yourself back inside it, standing or sitting where you were. Take a few seconds to get properly back in there before you look around. Look at what was in front of you, who else was there, and what you could hear. Let it be happening now, and feel what your body does with it. You can't force this part, so take whatever comes, however faint.

2. [intermediate] (BLACKSTONE+HARRIS) Now find the place in your body where that good feeling sits. If the place you check gives back nothing, use your hands instead. Once you have a place, go inside it and stay in there, not next to it. Take your time getting in. From in there, feel its warmth, its weight, and its edges. 3. [intermediate] (HEADSPACE+HARRIS) Now keep your attention on that feeling while it changes. Notice it getting clearer, or fainter, or moving a little to one side. Finding it again takes a second, so do that as often as you need to.

4. [intermediate] (SPERO+HEADSPACE) Something heavy may be sitting here alongside the good feeling. Leave the heavy one exactly where it is and do not work on it. You are not pushing it away, and you are not fixing it either. You are only choosing which of the two your attention sits on. So put your attention back on the good feeling and keep it there.
