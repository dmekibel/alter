## 1 · The beat-entry sequence (item 1) — what the prototype says, and what was constructed

**Measured in the prototype: the scrim CUTS.** `grep transition` over the whole bundle = **0 hits**; the scrim is a
plain `<div style="position:absolute; inset:0; z-index:5; background:rgba(32,10,36,.62)">` with no animation and no
transition, gated by `showScrim: beat < 10`. And the frame is a STATIC COMPOSITION PER BEAT — every beat re-renders

| phase | value | source |

| 3 · bubble | mounts at scrim-start, animates with the frame's own **.08s** delay | frame-verbatim |
| 3b · lit | `tLight .45s cubic-bezier(.22,.9,.3,1) both`, delay 0 | frame-verbatim |

|---|---|---|---|

## 2 · The raise proof (item 3) — and the structural argument for a compositing scroller

### Why v1416 failed on the phone and passed in the preview
v1416 hung ONE scrim in `.tf-inner` at z40 and raised the target to z41. That is correct CSS, and it is wrong on iOS:
**`#tfWorld` is a composited scroller** (`position:absolute; overflow:auto; touch-action:pan-y`, 27,179px of content),
so WebKit promotes it and flattens its whole subtree into a single layer. A layer cannot interleave with a scrim that
is a SIBLING of the scroller — the scrim's layer simply composites on top of the scroller's, whatever the z-indices
say. That predicts David's two photographs exactly: the day strip (a `#tfWorld` descendant) read dim on beat 2, and
on beat 5 the bubble, the thumb AND the TOOLS cue — all three inside `#tourFlow`, itself a `#tfWorld` child — were
all dark. Chrome, which composites differently, showed none of it.

### The fix: every layer dims itself, from inside
| layer | its dim | how the lit thing escapes it |
|---|---|---|
| `#tfWorld` (composited scroller) | `#tourScrimW`, a CHILD of `#tfWorld`, `position:absolute; top:0; height:scrollHeight; z-index:10` | the target is raised to `z-index:12` **inside the same scroller**, so the ordering is resolved within one layer |
| `#tfHud` (z23) | `.tour-fade > * { filter: brightness(.4) saturate(.8) }` — tLight's own 0% frame | `.tour-fade > .tour-lit { filter:none }` + the running `tLight`; no z-index games at all |
| `.jr-host` (z30) | same filter | same |
| `#guardPuck` (body, z88→92) | `.tour-pdim` inside the puck | the beat that lights it switches that dim off |

**Nothing crosses a compositing boundary any more**, so the raise holds under any compositor — that is the structural
claim, and it does not depend on measuring iOS. It also deletes the `#tfHud` z-index/`translate` borrowing v1416
needed (`zfix` is now empty on every beat), and with it the whole class of bug that borrowing existed to work around.

**The z ladder, measured rather than assumed.** The highest z-index anywhere under `#tfWorld` is **5** (424 positioned
descendants scanned). So: world content ≤5 · `#tourScrimW` 10 · `#tourCatch` 11 · `.tour-lit` 12 · `#tfHud` 23 ·
`.jr-host` 30 · `#tourFlow` 41. **The scrim sits BELOW the chrome on purpose.** The first cut of this fix put it at
39, above the HUD's 23 — and the measurement caught it: on beats 7 and 8 `elementFromPoint` at the lit chrome's
centre returned `tourScrimW`, i.e. the settings icon and the JOURNEY cue were buried under the WORLD's dim, and the
whole HUD was double-dimmed (its own filter plus the veil). Dropping the scrim under the chrome fixes both at once
and is why the HUD's dim is a filter and not a second veil: a veil over the HUD would double-dim its 10px overflow
band into a visible rectangle.

## 10 · Deviations, named
1. **The bright hold is 380ms and the scrim fades over .45s.** The frame cuts its scrim with no transition anywhere in
   the bundle, so neither number is measurable from it. The hold is CHOSEN; the fade borrows `tLight`'s curve because
   tLight is the other half of the same moment. Both are one constant each (`TOUR_HOLD`, the `.tour-scrimw` transition).
2. **The HUD and the journey host are dimmed by a FILTER, not a veil** (`brightness(.4) saturate(.8)` — tLight's own
   0% frame). A second veil over the HUD would double-dim its 10px overflow band into a visible rectangle. Against the
   veil it lands within ~18/255 per channel on the HUD's pink glyphs.
3. **The direction lock is a scroll clamp** — the one place the tour writes `scrollTop`, and the exception the build
   rules name. `touch-action: pan-up/pan-down` was NOT shipped: WebKit does not implement it and an unsupported value
   silently removes the restriction entirely.
4. **The journey cue's ring** is the frame's SIZE around the app's cue CENTRE, starting at `scale(1)` (item 8). The
   frame's own ring sits 6px below its cue's centre; that offset is not reproduced, because the point of the item is
   that the ring must never crop the word.
5. **The bloom bubbles' origin-x follows the app's own tail**, which sits where the app's target is. Origin-Y is
   byte-identical to the frame on every beat.
6. **Beat 7 lights the app's away-puck** and beat 10's bottom-left home button has no counterpart — unchanged from
   DIFF-2026-09-05. No arrow on the settings beat; Skip reads "Skip"; P1 carries the dim (all frame-verified).
7. **Screenshots are not persisted as PNGs.** The Browser pane was hidden for the whole session, so its screenshot
   tool times out and rAF never fires. Everything above is DOM + computed-style assertion instead, including the
   paint-order probe — a stricter test of "does it read lit" than a screenshot would be. PORT-LOCK's numeric-diff
   clause covers this.
