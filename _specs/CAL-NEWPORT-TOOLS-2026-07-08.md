# CAL NEWPORT → ALTER — TOOLS

The tools/timers/guided-protocols layer of the Newport→ALTER study. Every discrete tool-shaped idea in the corpus — the obvious focus timers, plus the long tail of one-off guided protocols and tool-shaped micro_tips. For each: what it is, exact protocol steps, nuances/caveats, charging behavior, what it logs, and which persona/energy-state it serves.

ALTER house law: **no bolt-on widgets.** Everything below must DEEPEN an existing surface — guided tools/timers, the charging primitive (hold-to-invest), AM/PM bookends, the energy/mood gauge (relief door vs structure door), the journey stones, or identity-evidence tracking. Where a Newport idea is a lecture, a policy, or a planner feature rather than a *tool you sit down and run*, I say so and route it elsewhere. Where a tool is a weak fit for ALTER, I flag it rather than pad.

A note on charging: the corpus repeatedly says the same thing in Newport's own words — investment/friction is a feature, not a bug ("The friction is a feature, not a bug"), and gamification cannot out-dopamine TikTok, so it must connect to identity/EFT instead. That is *exactly* ALTER's charging primitive. So the strong tools below almost all charge: the hold-to-invest gesture IS the "cognitive rep," the "act of will against the impulse to stop," the "signal to yourself you're serious."

## TIER A — Core guided tools (strong fit, buildable now)

### A1. Focus Interval Timer (attention interval training)
**What it is.** A timed single-task focus session with hard levels (10→20→30→40+ min) that you climb only by completing clean, zero-distraction sessions. The flagship "attention as a trainable muscle" tool.

**Protocol.**
1. Pick ONE task. State it before starting (forces single-threading).
2. Start at your current tier (new users: 10 min, never an ambitious hour).
3. Zero distraction for the whole interval — a hard rule, not "mostly focused."
4. If you break concentration: the session **resets to zero**, not pause. Partial credit defeats the training.
5. Level up (10→20) only after N *consecutive* clean sessions at the current tier, not one lucky run.

**Nuances / variations / caveats.**
- Zero-distraction is binary. "The training must reset to zero if broken (no partial credit)."
- Progression is gated on *consistency* (multiple clean sessions), not a single success.
- Reframe: most people think focus is a fixed trait; it's rep-based ("You just have to train your brain what it feels like to stay focused").
- The 3–4 hr/day ceiling caps this: past ~3–4 hr of true intensity, returns go negative. Don't let the tool reward ever-larger daily totals — plateau the encouragement past the ceiling. Applies to studying too, not just work ("two intense work sessions is the realistic limit").
- Cognitive-endurance / "VO2-max for the mind": expose a periodic personal-best ("longest clean focus session") as a rising trend line — a single legible number that improves with training.

**Charging.** Yes — strongly. The hold-to-charge *is* the wind-up; the interval itself is the rep. Charge strength should scale with clean-session length, and a broken session docks/voids the charge (models "attention residue" — interrupted charges show visible fog that has to burn off before the next counts at full strength).

**Logs.** Clean-session count per tier; current tier; longest clean session (personal record); daily deep-work minutes (with a soft-cap plateau at ~3–4 hr); a "deep-first ratio" (% of days a focus session preceded any reactive check).

**Persona / energy-state.** Structure door. David and brother (builders). Poor fit for the reels-addicted girlfriend persona at first contact — she needs relief, not a 40-min discipline ladder; start her at 10 min or not at all.

### A2. Thinking Walk (productive meditation)
**What it is.** A phone-free walk where you hold ONE problem in mind; when attention wanders, you notice-without-judgment and redirect. Trains sustained attention ("cognitive pull-ups") while making real progress on the problem.

**Protocol.**
1. Pre-walk: capture the ONE problem you'll hold (one problem per walk, not general rumination).
2. Walk phone-free, holding the problem.
3. On wander: "notice, don't judge, but come back to the original problem." Each return is a rep, not a lapse.
4. Post-walk: capture the insight/resolution (carry a notebook or capture at checkpoints so flow isn't broken).

**Nuances.**
- Explicitly hard at first ("cognitive pull-ups") — early attempts feel like failure; that IS the training. Say this in copy so users don't quit.
- Walking is a distinct modality: motion suppresses competing circuits and boosts *creative/strategic* insight, but *precision* work (exact wording) is actually harder while moving. Tag walk-tool sessions for creative problems specifically, not composition.
- Outdoor > treadmill: the mechanism is motion *plus novelty* (weather, environment), not motion alone.
- Pairs with unconscious-thought: spacing the same problem across 2–3 walks over days beats one marathon walk.

**Charging.** Yes — log a "return" each time the user re-taps/re-affirms focus mid-walk, framed as reps not lapses. Multi-day version: invest in a question over several days (a multi-day charge) that pays off at a synthesis moment.

**Logs.** Total walk-minutes; "returns" per walk (reps); problems captured → resolved; tag (creative/strategic vs precision).

**Persona / energy-state.** Structure door, but gentler than A1 — good for a mid-energy day. Ties directly into ALTER's existing meditation content (the "notice, don't judge, return" meta-skill underlies both).

### A3. Concentration-Calisthenics "Resist" (urge-as-rep)
**What it is.** A moment-level micro-interaction: when the urge to check the phone hits mid-focus, you log a *rep* instead of acting, riding out a short countdown.

**Protocol.**
1. Mid-session, the urge arrives.
2. Tap "resist" → a 2–5 min countdown starts.
3. Ride it out. The craving is reframed as a workout opportunity, not a threat.
4. Diagnostic branch: name the urge — fatigue → take a real break; boredom/confusion → adjust the task or clarify the goal (don't white-knuckle).

**Nuances.**
- Distinct from A1's longer interval training — this is a micro-practice layered *inside* any focus/charge session.
- The diagnostic split matters: not every urge should be resisted; fatigue deserves a real break. Copy must not shame a legitimate break.
- "Urge surfing" variant (5–10 min just feeling the urge pass, from the Discipline corpus) is the calmer, meditation-adjacent sibling — explicitly framed as *training discomfort tolerance*, separate from meditation.

**Charging.** Yes — a mid-charge "urge" prompt lets the user log resistance as a stat; resisting adds to the charge.

**Logs.** Weekly "reps" (resistances); urge-cause tags (fatigue/boredom/confusion); resisted-vs-acted ratio.

**Persona / energy-state.** Relief-adjacent (it meets the user in the distraction moment) but serves a structure goal. Girlfriend persona: the urge-surf variant is the honest entry point.

### A4. AM Bookend — Enter-the-Day Ritual + Deep-First Plan
**What it is.** The morning ritual, already in ALTER, sharpened by Newport's ritual-design and bimodal (deep-first) findings. A fixed, namable, repeatable entry sequence that ends by building today's plan BEFORE any reactive surface opens.

**Protocol.**
1. Same 3–5 steps every day (physical + reflective + nourishing) — user-defined and *named*.
2. A defined hard end-time ("morning ends here") that transitions into work/day mode — the boundary creates the constraint.
3. Build today's plan / first deep block *before* opening notifications or quick-tasks (deep-first, "protect the first hour").
4. Optionally set today's *fixed end-time* (fixed-schedule productivity) as part of morning planning.
5. Low-energy mode: still produce a plan, just a smaller one — "planned less" beats both "unplanned drift" and "forced full plan."

**Nuances.**
- Willpower-free by design: "Your morning is not a test of how much discipline you have; it's a test of how well you've designed your morning." Track binary yes/no completion, NOT a graded score.
- Keep it small (3–5 steps). Periodically ask "is this routine still landing?" and adjust — don't treat it as permanently fixed.
- The energy-door/low-energy variant maps *exactly* onto ALTER's existing mood-gauge routing — Newport's own language validates it ("If you see you have less energy, say: I'm going to work less today, but I'm going to make a plan for this less-work day").
- Optional "carry one question into your day" framing (from the thinking-walk corpus) instead of a pure to-do list.

**Charging.** Light — a hold-to-charge on the "enter the day" step as the wind-up before the first block.

**Logs.** Morning-routine completion streak (binary); today's fixed end-time; energy-state declared; deep-first ratio.

**Persona / energy-state.** All personas; the low-energy branch serves the depleted/relief user, the full branch serves the structure user.

### A5. PM Bookend — Shutdown Ritual with Verification
**What it is.** The evening ritual, sharpened into Newport's shutdown ritual: review the day, process open loops into a trusted store, plan tomorrow, then a discrete "shutdown complete" marker that lets the brain stop monitoring. This is one of the highest-confidence fits in the whole corpus — it recurs across four subjects.

**Protocol.**
1. Review today (plan vs actual) across ALL capture systems (planner + any scratch notes) — partial shutdown doesn't work.
2. Capture stray/open loops into a stored state (moving to backlog is enough; you don't have to *do* them).
3. Name tomorrow's top 1–3 items with rough blocks (this is genuine planning, not a vague list).
4. Say/tap a **completion phrase** — spoken or written, not just thought. The app then visually "closes" the day (dims/locks further planning until morning).

**Nuances.**
- The completion needs a concrete marker (a phrase, a checked box, an animation) — "vague 'I guess I'm done' doesn't work."
- The psychological payoff (not ruminating) takes ~a month of consistent practice before it kicks in — "give it a month to be safe." Set this expectation so early failures don't cause abandonment.
- Rumination-resistance is trainable: "You're filling in the groove until it's shallow enough that your thoughts don't get stuck in it." Each time the user notices a work-thought after shutdown and dismisses it, log a tally.
- Late-night guard: if the user opens the app late, surface their OWN shutdown phrase back to them ("I did shutdown; I checked the box; I can wait").
- Doubles as the day's meeting/interaction **processing ritual** — "processing is a more important and more efficient way of making sense of meetings than prep."
- Sleep reframe: poor sleep is often a work-organization problem surfacing at night; a completed shutdown is the real lever, not bedtime rules. Optional composite "sleep readiness" from shutdown-completion + evening-plan adherence + screen-type choice.

**Charging.** Yes — the shutdown act itself is a charge moment ("investing in tomorrow by closing today properly"); seal the day's identity-evidence only after the checklist completes.

**Logs.** Shutdown-complete streak; rumination-resistance tally; "closed loops" count; sleep-readiness composite (optional).

**Persona / energy-state.** All personas; especially the anxious/overloaded user. The completion phrase is a natural guardian-voice moment ("Work is complete" said *with* the user).

### A6. Evening Menu (sketch-plan, not schedule)
**What it is.** A loose, card-picking evening plan of 3–5 rejuvenating activities — deliberately UNstructured, the UX opposite of the daily time-block planner.

**Protocol.**
1. From a personal "rejuvenating activities" menu (reading, walk, conversation, craft), pick 3–5 for the evening.
2. Loose check-off, not time-boxed. Variety + looseness is the whole point.
3. If a screen activity is chosen, nudge subscription content (film/show) over algorithmic feeds — same duration, less arousal near bed.

**Nuances.**
- "Do nothing" as a plan backfires for organized minds — a light plan of genuinely restorative activities calms better than either rigid scheduling or no plan.
- Explicitly NOT time-blocking — build it as unstructured card-picking, contrasted with the planner's precision. This is a distinct PM mode, not a reskin of the planner.

**Charging.** No — deliberately non-charged, non-scored. Over-gamifying leisure turns it into another chore (see the "celebration bucket, zero productivity framing" law).

**Logs.** Which activities chosen (light), for pattern-surfacing only — no streak pressure.

**Persona / energy-state.** Relief door; the reels-addicted/depleted user; the "leisure architecture" answer to compulsive streaming ("find something even better than Netflix").

### A7. Solitude Timer (quiet-brain / boredom-tolerance)
**What it is.** A 20–25 min sit-with-your-thoughts session — no phone, no audio, no chores — with a gentle close-out to journal what surfaced. Restores the "quiet brain" identity-formation state.

**Protocol.**
1. Alone, no input, 20–25 min (start smaller: a 5-min phoneless errand, laddering up over weeks).
2. Just observe / internal dialogue. Not meditation-with-instructions — unoccupied mental space.
3. Close-out: journal one thought/idea that surfaced.

**Nuances.**
- Distinct from meditation (no anchor, no technique) and distinct from the thinking-walk (no problem held) — this is *unstructured* solitude.
- Takes 4–6 weeks to feel natural — build as a ladder/streak, not a one-off. Milestone badge at "Quiet Brain Restored."
- Boredom is the *training stimulus*, reframed as useful, not a problem to escape.
- Identity-work variant: gate a "who am I becoming?" journal prompt *behind* completion of a solitude block, making the causal link explicit ("solitude → identity formation").

**Charging.** Light — a pre-session hold to signal intent; the sit is its own investment.

**Logs.** Solitude streak; session length ladder (5→25 min); surfaced-thought entries; "who am I becoming" reflections gated on completion.

**Persona / energy-state.** Structure-curious but calm; strong for the contemplation bucket. Note: a hard sell for the acutely distracted persona — ladder from the 5-min errand.

### A8. Long-Thinking / Idea-Document tools
**What it is.** Two linked contemplative tools. (a) **Long Thinking**: a weekly blank-page, input-free timer for original synthesis. (b) **Idea Document**: a persistent, topic-specific evolving doc updated when new thinking occurs (not daily).

**Protocol — Long Thinking.**
1. Weekly ~60-min block. Blank page, no input sources.
2. Sustained original thought on one topic; log the resulting insight as the session's output.

**Protocol — Idea Document.**
1. One doc per topic you care deeply about.
2. Update ~monthly (or when new thinking appears), not on a forced cadence.
3. Simple history/diff so the user sees their own thinking evolve ("To write is to think").

**Nuances.**
- Long Thinking is *production*, not intake — explicitly distinguished from consuming even high-quality books.
- Trainable/restorable even after heavy shallow-consumption damage — not a permanent loss.
- Idea Document is distinct from daily journaling (topic-specific, event-triggered, not daily-reflective).
- The "serious thinking" framing: sustained attention on ambiguous info to build a *new conceptual structure* — distinct from a thinking-walk (which holds a bounded problem).

**Charging.** Long Thinking: yes (the blank-page effort is the rep). Idea Document: light — charge the *act of updating* as an identity deposit.

**Logs.** Long-thinking session count + one insight each; idea-doc update history per topic (identity-evidence of growing understanding).

**Persona / energy-state.** High-agency structure user (David). Weak fit for early-stage / low-efficacy users — route them to keystone habits first.

### A9. Single-Purpose Notebook ("one problem, one page")
**What it is.** A bounded scratchpad committed to ONE active life question, distinct from the daily journal. Retired ("graduated") when the problem resolves.

**Protocol.**
1. Dedicate one notebook/page to one problem; carry it everywhere (low-friction capture beats a phone app that holds every context at once).
2. Add lines as ambient insight arrives (walks, commutes).
3. Weekly check-in: has this reached a conclusion, does it need calendar time, or does exploration continue?
4. On resolution: retire/archive it with a "graduation" animation → a trophy shelf.

**Nuances.**
- Explicitly NOT Zettelkasten/second-brain — only ONE bounded live problem, defined lifespan, not infinite accumulation.
- Keep a *separate* capture notebook for miscellaneous to-dos — never mix idea-notebooks with GTD capture.
- The ritual/tactile aspect is the mechanism ("puts you in a mindset for creative exploration in a way that loading up your laptop does not").
- Ambient capture claim ("~5x more brain power") is anecdotal — soften in copy.

**Charging.** Light — charge the weekly synthesis moment, not each line.

**Logs.** Active-problem count; retired notebooks (trophy shelf) as identity-evidence ("problems you've thought through to a conclusion").

**Persona / energy-state.** Contemplative/creative user. The retired-notebook shelf is a satisfying identity artifact for any persona.

## TIER B — Timer/mode variants and transition tools (strong, but lighter surfaces)

### B1. Re-Entry Ritual (post-break transition tool)
**What it is.** A short (2–5 min) identical ritual (breath/stretch/gesture) run every time you re-enter focus after a break, offered specifically at block boundaries on the timeline.

**Protocol.**
1. At a break→work transition, run the same 2–5 min sequence every time (identical = automaticity).
2. Hard rule: no email/inbox immediately after the break ("do not start your first session after lunch going down a context-switching rabbit hole").
3. Pre-stage the next task's context *before* the break so there's no friction on return.

**Nuances.** Reuse existing PMR/breathwork content, packaged as a quick 2-min variant — distinct from full-length tools. Variability undermines it; keep it identical.

**Charging.** Light. **Logs.** Transition-ritual completions. **Persona.** Structure user mid-day.

### B2. Movement-Break Prompt (interleaved exercise)
**What it is.** A 5–10 min intense movement prompt every 45–50 min of tracked focus, logged as *part* of the focus session.

**Protocol.** At the 45–50 min mark of tracked focus, prompt pushups/jump-rope/short walk; log it inside the session. Weekly planning pre-commits exercise slots (or they get pushed to evening and skipped).

**Nuances.** Distinct from one long workout — the *interleaved cadence* is the claimed focus-protector. Must be planned at the weekly level, not decided end-of-day. **Charging.** No. **Logs.** Movement breaks per focus session; pre-committed exercise slots hit. **Persona.** Body bucket; structure user.

### B3. Focus Ladder (concentration-as-trainable, duration mode)
**What it is.** A variant of A1 that auto-increases session duration as completion thresholds are hit, with a visible chart of session length over time as tangible proof of growth. The proof-of-improvement is the motivational payload.

**Nuances.** Overlaps heavily with A1 — build as A1's "show me my growth curve" view, not a separate tool. Removing all digital distraction during the window matters more than exact duration. Works for kids and adults; only the starting duration differs. **Charging.** Inherits A1. **Logs.** Session-length trend line. **Persona.** Any focus user who needs visible momentum.

### B4. Reading Session + Closing-Push Sprint
**What it is.** A reading-block timer with a sub-tag (dense/fun/audio) and a distinct "closing push" sprint mode (a single timed 2–3 hr sprint when ~100 pages remain, to stop books trailing off unfinished).

**Protocol.** Schedule 3–4 weekly reading slots, tagged dense/fun/audio; keep three books concurrent (mood picks which). Read away from any device. "Closing push" fires near the end. Interval-training on-ramp: start with exciting/page-turner material, step duration up (10→15→20→40 over ~2 weeks); use secondary sources first for hard classics ("read about it before you read it").

**Nuances.** Reading is framed as "brain calisthenics" — track dense reading as its own "cognitive reps," distinct from fiction/audio. Phone-declutter is "the secret weapon" — bigger lever than any scheduling trick. **Charging.** Yes — pre-session hold before a dense-reading session, like a workout. **Logs.** Books completed (library shelf visual), tagged; dense-reading minutes ("Cognitive Fitness" meter). **Persona.** Contemplation/craft bucket; any persona via the "fun book" on-ramp.

### B5. Delight-Project / Flow-State Reset (nervous-system cleaning pass)
**What it is.** A named "delight project" (chosen purely for genuine engagement, unrelated to the user's stressors) surfaced specifically on a self-reported high-anxiety day, instead of a generic breathing exercise.

**Protocol.** Name one delight project at onboarding. On a flagged hard/anxious day, the tool-suggestion logic surfaces THAT project. Flow interrupts rumination by activating unrelated neural networks — a "cleaning pass."

**Nuances.** Must be *genuinely delightful*, not another obligation — the delight is the mechanism. Explicitly contrasted with passive scrolling as numbing; this is active engagement. **Charging.** No. **Logs.** Delight-project sessions on hard days. **Persona.** Relief door; the anxious/overwhelmed user. Pairs with the Hard-Day mode (C7).

## TIER C — Guided protocols that are really *modes* or *rituals* (medium fit)

### C1. Charging as the universal "invest attention" primitive
Not a separate tool — the corpus repeatedly validates ALTER's *existing* charging primitive. Newport's "the friction is a feature," "obsess over quality" (invest fully in one ritual, not skim many), "money/skin-in-the-game as a commitment signal" (frame a hold-to-charge as "you signaling to yourself you're serious, not dabbling"), and the EFT-over-dopamine argument (charge = investing in the future-self, not points) all map onto it. Design rule from the corpus: ALTER's own gamification "must not pretend hard things are easy — it should pair effort with identity-meaning." Two charge modes worth distinguishing: *building capital* (routine daily invest) vs *spending capital* (a discrete leverage-use event).

### C2. Blackout Window / Deep-Work Isolation
**What it is.** A scheduled, *announced* unavailable period — distinct from a simple focus timer because it includes an announce-to-others step (what makes it stick, per the Lincoln/historical framing).

**Protocol.** Schedule the window; announce it (in-app to self, optionally export to calendar/status); track adherence as its own stat. Pair the block with a suggested location change, not just a do-not-disturb toggle.

**Nuances.** The announce step is the differentiator — "clarity beats accessibility 95% of the time." Long unbroken blocks are nonlinearly more productive than fragmented equivalents ("two 2-hour slabs are not one unbroken 4-hour block"). **Charging.** Light. **Logs.** Blackout adherence; unbroken-block length. **Persona.** Structure user with real external demands (David/brother). Weaker for the single-user contexts where there's no one to announce to.

### C3. Environmental / Location Cue prompt
**What it is.** A one-line pre-session prompt ("find your spot, dim the lights, phone in another room") before a guided tool, plus an optional "where are you?" tag surfaced later as a personal insight ("you focus best at X").

**Nuances.** Not a core mechanic — a coaching-line + optional tag. Works even without separate rooms (a different desk/corner). The "phone foyer" is the concrete version. **Charging.** No. **Logs.** Location tags → correlation with session quality. **Persona.** Any.

### C4. Phone-Foyer / Dock-Your-Phone ritual
**What it is.** A PM-bookend-adjacent prompt: "where does your phone sleep tonight?" tracked as a streak (nights docked away from the body).

**Protocol.** Mark a fixed "phone home" location; app celebrates nights docked / phone-free mornings. Whole-household variant (everyone docks together) removes the social awkwardness.

**Nuances.** It's about *ubiquity of the cue*, not willpower — proximity is the failure mode, not visibility (NOT face-down-on-desk). Charging metaphor made literal: charging happens at a station away from your body, symbolically reinforcing the real behavior. **Charging.** A small charge bonus for docking. **Logs.** Nights docked (feeds the Vibe/psychograph). **Persona.** All; the reels-addicted persona most of all.

### C5. Digital Declutter / 30-Day Reset (and RCT 14-day variant)
**What it is.** A structured multi-week challenge arc: remove all optional tech, rediscover values, then zero-based-rebuild. Belongs on the Journey as a stone-arc, but has a *tool* core: a countdown timer + daily "what did I reach for instead?" check-in + an end-of-reset zero-based-budget form.

**Protocol.** 30-day countdown; daily journal (one rediscovered activity + 1–10 feeling rating); day-30 structured zero-based budget per app (value served / rules / what's blocked). The RCT-backed variant is 14 days blocking high-engagement apps (keep functional ones: payment/2FA/weather), with a 4-part before/after self-report (time use, social interaction, sleep, self-control). Post-challenge: an explicit day-14/30 "make it permanent" decision point — relapse-to-old-habits is the named failure mode.

**Nuances.** Declutter ≠ detox — it's vision-pull (rediscover values), not avoidance-push white-knuckling. The RCT variant is the single most *evidence-backed* item in the corpus — flag it as such. For each cut app, design an IRL replacement ritual or the void gets reclaimed. **Charging.** The daily "reached-for-instead" resistance can be logged as identity-evidence. **Logs.** Streak; rediscovered activities; before/after 4-part scores. **Persona.** Any user ready for a big commitment; the app can enter a quiet "sabbath/crisis" minimal mode during it.

### C6. Weekly Sabbath / Digital-Sabbath mode
**What it is.** A once-a-week, day-length ritual where the app itself goes quiet (no notifications, no prompts) for a chosen 24h window and offers a small menu of replacement rituals (gratitude, family, reading).

**Nuances.** Newport is explicit the religious specifics don't matter — the mechanism is *scheduled full disconnection*. Needs concrete replacement activities scheduled in, not just absence. This is ALTER modeling anti-engagement by literally shutting up — a strong moat expression. **Charging.** No. **Logs.** Sabbath windows honored. **Persona.** Any; the anxious/over-connected user.

### C7. Hard-Day Mode (scheduled anxiety check-ins)
**What it is.** An app-wide toggle that reshapes the bookends during a high-stress period: two scheduled check-ins (AM/PM) that name the cognitive distortion, state the plan, and close — with a "we'll handle it at the evening check-in" deferral message between windows.

**Protocol.** Toggle on → two daily 10–15 min check-ins. Each ends with an explicit closing move ("here's reality, here's our plan") — not open-ended venting. Between windows, defer the urge to worry. Pair with the named Delight Project (B5) to actively interrupt loops, not just suppress. Crisis-mode variant: whitelist 1–2 non-alarmist news sources, block the rest; the app can enter a simplified/lower-input state.

**Nuances.** Check-ins must *close*, not ruminate. Scheduling worry gives the nervous system defined recovery windows and makes deferral credible (the check-in is real). **Charging.** No. **Logs.** Check-ins completed; distortions named; mood trend. **Persona.** Relief door; the acutely anxious user. Maps onto ALTER's existing energy-door routing.

### C8. Values / Lifestyle-Vision guided exercises
Several corpus tools are really *reflection exercises* (belong in onboarding/journey, but tool-shaped):
- **Lifestyle Vision capture**: 5 buckets (craft/community/constitution/contemplation/celebration), one first-person present-tense sentence each; monthly "pick one bucket, name one move" loop. Kept "contingent," revisited at birthday/season.
- **Resonance Notebook**: daily one-sentence emotional-resonance capture → monthly distillation into a values list. (Quick-add anywhere; a monthly distill ritual.)
- **Values Document**: rough first draft, revised each season; surfaces "does this serve your value?" before big in-app commitments.
- **Future-Self / EFT visualization**: concrete sensory prompts (where/with-whom/how it feels in 5–10 yrs), plus an "exemplar library" the user revisits *before* hard tools. Newport's rival-to-gamification theory: strengthen EFT, don't try to out-dopamine.
- **Escape-Fantasy extraction**: name a recurring escape fantasy → extract the archetypal element (slowness/autonomy/presence) → 3–5 feasible ways to embody it now → one small prototype.
- **Metric Codes**: nightly ~5-code activity log (EX, STR…), binary done/not-done; "multiple-choice" variant (pick ONE of five per day) and "seasonal" variant (one metric per 4-month season). Newport calls this "the closest thing to a cheat code."

**Nuances.** Keep these *tool-light* (quick capture + periodic distill), not heavy forms. The Metric Codes tool maps directly onto ALTER's tracker as a lightweight alternative mode. Values must be *specific* ("connection via my cycling group," not "social connection"). **Charging.** Light on the capture/update acts. **Persona.** Contemplation bucket; higher-agency users.

## TIER D — Weak fits, poor fits, and things that are NOT tools (flagged honestly)

- **Time-blocking / multi-scale planning / weekly template / capacity meter / overload meter / task-type segregation / progressive task-reveal / "does this fit?" reality-check.** These are the *planner's* job, not guided tools. Strong ideas, wrong layer — route to the Planner/Journey layer. The only tool-shaped fragment: the "50%/doubling estimation" nudge and a calibration stat, which can live as planner micro-copy.
- **Firm-no / 5-question decision gate / workload cap / obligation counter.** Policy + planner-gate, not a sit-down tool. Belongs in goal-intake, not the tools shelf.
- **Batch email / office hours / async-first / hive-mind fixes.** Single-user life-sim has little inbox to batch. The transferable primitive is "defer the urge to your next window" (already covered by A3/C7). Otherwise a poor fit — don't build a comms tool.
- **Feedback Council.** A reflective prompt ("who's in your council?"), not a tool. At most a journey-stone/community-bucket exercise.
- **Burner-phone / inconvenient-phone / app-blocking intensity ladder.** Hardware/OS-level choices ALTER can't execute. Educational tool-card content only; at most optional self-report logging. Flag as out-of-scope for a PWA.
- **Kantian "is this what you actually want?" micro-check.** A single lightweight pre-action prompt — usable as a dignity-respecting nudge, but thin as a standalone tool. Fold into C4/C7 framing.
- **Long-thinking for low-efficacy users, Solitude Timer for the acutely distracted, A1 at 40-min for the reels-addicted.** Right tools, wrong *sequencing* — gate them behind keystone-habit efficacy first. The corpus is explicit: efficacy/discipline-identity precedes depth work.
- **"Cognitive fitness points system" (Cooper-style ~30 pts/week aggregating reading/writing/practice).** Tempting as a meta-score, but risks becoming a pseudo-productivity engagement metric — the exact thing the corpus warns against ("reward completed depth, not raw activity"; Goodhart's Law). Build the underlying trackers (A1, A2, B4) first; only aggregate if it survives a "can this be gamed away from real behavior?" filter. Flag as **defer**.
- **Deconstruction / deliberate-practice-for-office-work / feasibility-audit / dip-vs-cul-de-sac / ground-truth interview.** Career-capital tools — real, but they belong in a skills/journey layer, not the daily tools shelf. Tool-shaped cores worth noting: a "study a master" deconstruction template, a "reality-test gate" (money as neutral signal), a "rejection audit" (tag the failed element), and a "does more success here buy more or less autonomy?" trap-check.

### B6. Incubation Timer (physical vs creative fatigue)
**Protocol.** "I'm stuck, not tired" option → logs task as paused-for-restructuring → 48h timer → on expiry, prompts a short walk/reflection before re-attempting. **Nuance.** Two fatigues need opposite responses; the 48h rest is itself productive ("without the 48 hours before that 20 minutes I wouldn't have had the insight"). **Logs.** "Stepped back to let it restructure" as a *positive* evidence entry, not a miss. **Persona.** Any creative/knowledge user.

### B7. Appreciation Ritual (weak-but-charming)
A 15-min guided beauty/nature/music appreciation session, no phone, no performance motive. Sibling to A7. **Flagged weak fit** — thin as a standalone tool; better folded into the celebration bucket or A6's menu than shipped alone.
