
# CAL NEWPORT → ALTER — CORE DESIGN

*The structural layer. Not features — the shape of the machine. What Newport's corpus says ALTER's daily loop, journey, bookends, planner, and constitution should fundamentally BE. Every move ties to a specific mined mechanism. Where Newport fits ALTER's existing soul, I say so; where he threatens it or fits poorly, I say that louder.*

The headline finding first, because it reframes everything below: **Newport is the single closest external validator ALTER has for its most contrarian design bets.** ALTER already committed — before this corpus — to graduation-as-endgame, anti-engagement as moat, the catch-not-the-completion as the unit, identity-evidence over rep-counting, and a floor/exit everywhere. Newport arrives at all five from a completely different door (knowledge-work productivity, not spiritual self-help) and lands on the same ground. That convergence is not a coincidence to celebrate; it is a structural instruction. **When two independent lineages insist on the same law, that law should be promoted from "David's taste" to "constitution-level invariant" and defended mechanically (a ratchet), not just held as intent.** That is the throughline of this document.

## MOVE 1 — The daily loop is a PLAN-TRUST engine, not a task list. Make the plan's realism the tracked object, not completion.

**Principle.** Newport's single most load-bearing planning claim: *"Your brain is a plan evaluation machine, and when it doesn't trust your plan, it withholds motivation."* An overstuffed plan produces a ten-minute dopamine hit and then five days of drift and guilt — *"a journal of failure."* The brain silently discounts an unrealistic plan and kills it before you start. This is the deepest available explanation for WHY ALTER's planner exists at all, and it is not the explanation ALTER currently leads with.

**Mechanism.** Perceived control (seeing exactly what happens and when) reduces the overwhelm signal that triggers avoidance. Time-blocking's *reality-check* function ("does this actually fit?") matters as much as its scheduling function — *"Time blocking is going to get you way more out of your time, but the other thing it's going to do is give you a reality check."* The discomfort of watching the day not fit is the point, not a bug to smooth over. And crucially: *"There is no gold star for hitting your time estimate exactly... you get gold stars for actually doing your work well."*

**Structural change.** The planner's success metric must shift from **completion %** to **plan-trust / adherence-with-intention**. Concretely:
- Every day the timeline should render a *capacity-vs-load* signal (Newport's "does this fit" meter) that turns visibly heavy when blocks overflow realistic hours — BEFORE the day starts, in the AM bookend. This is the "face the productivity dragon" moment made a UI state, not a lecture.
- The tracker's reward should fire for **"you worked with intention until done,"** not for hitting an exact duration. This is a copy AND scoring change: charge/evidence accrues for intentional engagement, decoupled from whether the estimate was right.
- Apply Newport's **50% buffer heuristic** as an onboarding default: first-time planners get durations silently padded ×1.5, tightening ~10%/week as their own history calibrates. Surface a "calibration" stat that *improves* over weeks — this gamifies self-knowledge, which is exactly the kind of honest metric ALTER wants (see Move 8).

**Constitution law it strengthens.** Strengthens the existing floor/exit and no-shame laws. **Adds a new one:** *the planner rewards intention, never accuracy — a realistic small plan completed beats a heroic plan half-done, and the app must structurally prefer the former.* This directly counters ALTER's own gamification instinct to reward volume.

## MOVE 2 — The daily loop must be a NESTED cascade (season → week → day), not a flat day-at-a-time surface. This is the missing layer above the timeline.

**Principle.** Newport's multi-scale planning is the most-repeated structural idea across his entire corpus: quarterly/seasonal vision → weekly plan → daily time-blocks, each level constraining the one below so daily execution *never re-derives strategy from scratch.* *"The sweet spot where you have some vision to the future, you're out of the short-term context of just reactivity, but it's still tractable."* Without the nesting: daily execution is busywork with no direction (a flat planner), OR big visions are fantasy with no daily mechanism (a vision board). ALTER currently has strong day and week surfaces and a Journey — but no explicit **season/quarter intent layer** binding them, and the Journey is not yet formally the quarterly layer of a planning cascade.

**Mechanism.** Each scale answers a *different* question (season: which 2-3 goals matter; week: this week's shape; day: time-blocks) and feeds back into the one above. The weekly plan is written **last**, after reviewing everything else — synthesis at the end, not the start. Skipping levels collapses coherence.

**Structural change.** Make the cascade the spine of the whole app, mapping onto surfaces ALTER already has:
- **Season/Quarter = the Journey.** A journey chapter IS the quarterly arc. This is the cleanest fit in the corpus: Newport's "quarterly plan" and ALTER's "journey chapter" are the same object. Give each chapter one first-person lifestyle-vision line (Move 6) as its north star.
- **Week = a weekly "shape the week" ritual** (Newport puts it on Friday, not Monday — plan the coming week from a fresh strategic view before the grind). This is a *new bookend variant* — a weekly bookend, sitting above the daily AM/PM ones. It pulls 1-3 items down from the active journey chapter.
- **Day = the existing timeline**, but every daily block auto-tags which weekly focus it serves, so the day is always visibly nested inside the week inside the chapter.

The three should be *visually nested* — the quarterly journey node contains weekly chips which contain today's blocks — so the user can always see the line from "one breath this morning" up to "the person I'm becoming this season."

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Strengthens the three-pane spine (Planner/Journey/Game already locked). **Adds:** *the Journey is not a parallel game — it is the top layer of the planning cascade. Nothing in the daily loop is orphaned from a season.* This resolves a latent tension in the current architecture where Journey and Planner risk feeling like two different apps.

## MOVE 3 — PRIME = episodic future thinking, not hype. SEAL = shutdown/loop-closure, not a confetti pop. Newport gives the two ends of the grammar their real mechanism.

This is the most important move for the PRIME→DO→NAME→PLACE→SEAL grammar specifically, because Newport supplies the load-bearing science for both ends.

### PRIME is Episodic Future Thinking (EFT), and it is a rival to gamification — heed the warning.

**Principle.** Newport's sharpest design-relevant claim: long-term motivation comes from a *distinct brain system* — episodic future thinking, a vivid projection of a desired future self — that can override the short-term dopamine system apps hijack. And the barbed corollary: *"Dingo can't win a dopamine competition with TikTok because learning has strain built in and TikTok is pure reward."* **You cannot out-reward the void with a fun mascot.** Gamification does not remove the inherent strain of a rep; it only works if it connects the strain to identity.

**Mechanism.** Dopamine fires on immediate clean rewards (likes, streaks-as-slot-machine). EFT activates ~11 brain regions and binds the present action to identity and long-term values — *"if you really connect who you are today with a future you viscerally want to wake up in, your brain will choose the hard path over the easy dopamine hit."* This is Dispenza's "rehearse the future self" (already ALTER canon) with a second independent source and a mechanism name.

**Structural change to PRIME.** Every PRIME beat should open with a **future-self visualization**, not a badge promise or a pep line. The Tony-Robbins priming ALTER already does (state, transition-carrying) is correct for *state*, but Newport says the *content* of the prime must be identity-projection: a concrete sensory line of who this rep serves, not "let's go." Channel it as a felt moment per the copy law, never the concept ("episodic future thinking" never appears). Optionally seed a **future-self library** — quotes/people/images the user saved that embody who they're becoming, surfaced right before a hard rep.

**The warning that must become constitution.** Newport is functionally critiquing ALTER's own gamification. The corpus is explicit that badges/streaks *"don't remove the inherent strain of learning."* **So ALTER's gamification must never pretend hard things are easy — it must pair effort with identity-meaning, not disguise effort as fun.** A gamified self-improvement app that leans on dopamine tricks is building the thing Newport says loses. This is a genuine constraint on ALTER's game layer, not a compliment.

### SEAL is the Shutdown Ritual with verification — the most-validated single idea in the corpus.

**Principle.** The shutdown ritual appears in nearly every Newport source and is the closest structural match to ALTER's PM bookend anywhere in the corpus. *"You announce to yourself that work is complete. This is important for your brain — it allows you to actually rest."* Open loops create background rumination; a *verified* closure signal (a spoken phrase, a tapped box) gives the brain permission to stop monitoring.

**Mechanism — and this is the subtle, buildable part.** The ritual has two halves: a **mechanical** half (review today, capture open loops into a trusted store, name tomorrow) and a **psychological** half (*"you're filling in the groove, filling in the groove until it's shallow enough that your thoughts don't get stuck in it"* — repeatedly dismissing post-shutdown work-thoughts). The psychological effect takes **~a month** of consistent practice to kick in — *"give it a month to be safe."* The completion signal must be externalized (spoken or tapped), not merely thought.

**Structural change to SEAL.** SEAL becomes a genuine loop-closure, not a reward animation:
1. A real **review + capture** step (process today's loose threads into the planner) *precedes* the seal — the seal is not "close the app," it's "resolve the open loops."
2. A discrete, ceremonial **completion act** — the hold-to-charge primitive is perfect here: *hold to seal* is the externalized signal, far better than a tap. The charge primitive was made for exactly this.
3. A **late-night re-open behavior**: if the user opens ALTER late at night, surface their own shutdown phrase back to them (*"I did shutdown; I checked the box; I can wait"* → written in ALTER's voice). This is the "filling the groove" mechanism as a live feature.
4. Set the expectation explicitly in Chronicle-stage copy that this takes ~a month to *feel* like rest — so early failures don't read as the ritual not working.

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Strengthens the bookend architecture and the charging primitive (gives SEAL its canonical use). **Adds:** *the day must CLOSE, not just end. A verified seal (externalized, hold-not-tap) that resolves open loops is a hard part of the PM bookend, because an unclosed loop is the mechanism of the rumination ALTER exists to reduce.*

## MOVE 4 — Energy/appetite-aware structure is not a nicety; Newport makes it a law of capacity. The mood-gauge routing gets a hard scientific floor.

**Principle.** ALTER already has the energy-door / structure-door routing (relief vs structure). Newport hardens it from a UX choice into a capacity *fact* with three converging mechanisms:

1. **The 3-4 hour deep-work ceiling.** Even elite performers cap at 3-4 hours of intense focus/day; more produces negative returns. *"June Huh does about three hours of focused work... then I'm exhausted."* Consistency day-after-day beats heroic marathons. **The app must have a ceiling, not just a floor** — it should visibly plateau/discourage past a realistic daily intensity, never reward ever-larger totals.

2. **Seasonality at every scale.** Humans evolved for oscillating intensity (harvest/fallow), not uniform grind — *"the most unnatural possible way of approaching work."* Newport wants explicit *seasons*: growth vs recovery, alternating deliberately, with recovery length proportional to the prior push.

3. **Energy-intentional low-capacity days.** The specific, buildable instruction: on a low-energy day, *"I'm going to work less today, but I'm going to make a plan for this less-work day."* The three-way framing is exact and worth preserving verbatim in design: **planned-less beats both unplanned-drift AND forced-full-plan.**

**Mechanism.** Neurological fatigue is a hard physiological limit; violating it produces the guilt-spiral ALTER is built to prevent. Matching the day's ask to real capacity is what makes the plan trustable (ties back to Move 1 — an over-ambitious plan on a low-energy day is *guaranteed* to be discounted by the brain).

**Structural change.**
- The mood-gauge (Vibe read) should drive not just *which door* but the day's **default block density and expected-win size.** Low energy → the AM bookend still produces a *real* plan, just a lighter one (a smaller intentional day, not no day). This is Newport's exact prescription and it validates ALTER's existing "every input has a rewarded floor" law.
- Add an app-level **season state** (growth / steady / recovery) that changes what counts as a "win," lowers expectations during recovery *without penalty*, and pre-schedules recovery the user can't casually override. Charge thresholds scale *down* in a declared recovery season — no broken streaks for honoring rest.
- Add a soft **ceiling**: once daily deep-work/charge minutes cross a realistic band (~3-4hr equivalent), the app plateaus its encouragement and gently signals "this is enough," rather than rewarding more.

**Where this is second-tier but real:** the *pause, don't quit* mechanic. Newport: when overload breaks your tracking, *"stopping metrics when overloaded is strategy, not failure."* ALTER's streaks should have a formal **pause state** distinct from a broken streak — an explicit "honor a recovery season" option. This directly counters standard gamification (streaks that just break and shame). It also dovetails with the already-canon "average gap is shrinking" ratchet — which is itself Newport's anti-perfectionism instinct made mathematical.

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Strengthens energy-door routing and the sticky-floor ratchet. **Adds two:** (a) *the app has a ceiling as well as a floor — it discourages overwork, never rewards ever-larger totals* (the anti-hustle law); (b) *seasons are honored — a declared recovery season lowers the ask without penalty, and a paused streak is not a broken one.*

## MOVE 5 — Selection before execution. The single biggest leverage point Newport names is the top of the funnel, and ALTER currently under-builds it.

**Principle.** Newport's Productivity Funnel: **Selection → Organization → Execution.** *"Activity selection gets ignored, even though it's the very top of the funnel — so it impacts everything below."* Optimizing execution or organization on an overloaded life just organizes the overload efficiently. The real lever is upstream: saying no *before* things land on the plate. This is echoed by the whole "do fewer things" spine of Slow Productivity, the overhead-spiral, and the 20% overload paradox (*people engineer their load ~20% above sustainable as psychological cover to justify declining*).

**Mechanism.** Knowledge work has no natural cap on concurrent commitments, so overload accumulates invisibly until stress is the only regulator. Each commitment carries *fixed* overhead (coordination, re-loading context) that scales with the *number* of commitments, not hours — so the fix is fewer things, not faster execution. *"The overhead will kill you way before the execution gets close to being too much."*

**Structural change.** ALTER's daily loop is strong at Organization (the timeline) and Execution (tools, charging). It is weak at **Selection**, and Newport says Selection is where the leverage is. Concretely:
- A hard, visible **cap on concurrent active goals / journey stones** (2-3 max active; the rest auto-file to a dormant/"baseline" list). Adding a new one while at cap forces an explicit *"what will you drop or baseline first?"* — Selection made a gate, not a lecture. This is the exact opposite of feature-sprawl and maps onto ALTER's own "no random features" law applied to the *user's* life, not just the codebase.
- A distinct **"considering / inbox" zone** before anything becomes a scheduled block — literalizing the Selection gate. Not everything captured becomes a commitment.
- **Baselining** as a first-class state (from Slow Productivity): non-focus goals get a lightweight *maintenance* checklist (much lighter than an active goal) so they stay alive on the journey map without consuming the attention budget. This is the psychological permission structure that prevents FOMO-driven focus-fragmentation — *"you baseline almost everything and a very small number of things you put time into."*

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** **Adds:** *ALTER manages the user's WORKLOAD, not just their time — the app caps concurrent commitments and makes saying-no a rewarded, first-class action, because selection is the top of the funnel.* This is a genuinely new spine for ALTER: it currently helps you *do* things; Newport says the higher-leverage help is choosing *fewer* things. Logging a "said no / protected capacity" as identity-evidence (a *positive* action) is the concrete hook.

## MOVE 6 — The Journey should structurally BE lifestyle-centric planning + the deep-life buckets + the two-stage capability→depth ladder. This is the corpus's biggest gift to the Journey's architecture.

**Principle.** Three nested Newport structures converge on what the Journey should be:

1. **Lifestyle-centric planning.** Don't chase goals/titles; write a first-person, present-tense, *sensory* vision of your ideal daily life, then reverse-engineer toward it. Vision must be *properties* ("I live in a high-energy city," "my mornings are unhurried"), never *achievements* ("I get this job"). The engine is a *cyclical* loop: *"How do I move closer to these visions? Repeat, repeat, repeat."* And it must stay **contingent** — a good-enough draft, revisited at natural checkpoints, never perfected up front (the "addiction to replanning is a hidden form of anxiety" trap).

2. **The five deep-life buckets** — Craft, Community, Constitution, Contemplation, Celebration — each with one *keystone habit* and periodic 4-6 week *overhaul sprints*, rotated one at a time. Celebration/Contemplation are the ones people most neglect and most regret.

3. **The two-stage Deep Life Stack.** Stage 1 (Discipline/Control/Craft/Simplification) builds a *capable human being*; only THEN does Stage 2 (Values/Service/Transformation/Legacy) become buildable. Depth work on an unstable foundation collapses. *"Becoming a capable human being is foundational before you can do the really cool stuff."* And its prerequisite root: **efficaciousness** — the basic belief "I can take action toward goals at all" — which must be built via absurdly small early wins before anything else.

**Mechanism.** Concrete lived-experience detail generates reliable gut resonance; abstract goals don't. Bucket-splitting prevents overwhelm (you only ever deeply focus one bucket at a time) and produces monthly micro-wins that compound into self-efficacy. Sequencing (capability before meaning) is load-bearing: skip it and the meaning-work has no scaffold.

**Structural change.**
- **The Journey's macro-arc = the two-stage stack.** Early stones build discipline/efficaciousness (absurdly small, identity-forming: "prove you follow through"); deeper stones (values, vision, legacy) *gate* behind sustained Stage-1 evidence. This is almost exactly ALTER's already-canon graduation ladder (teach→mirror→chronicle) — Newport supplies the *content sequence* under it. The two-axis journey (dose × scope) already in ALTER's PRIME law maps onto "Stage 1 depth first, Stage 2 scope later."
- **The five buckets = five parallel journey tracks**, each with one keystone habit (via the charging primitive as the daily deposit) and its own progress ring. This gives the Journey a *life-shaped* structure instead of a generic level-ladder, and it structurally surfaces the neglected buckets (Contemplation, Celebration) that a productivity-only app would starve.
- **A lifestyle-vision artifact** written once at onboarding (5 buckets, one first-person present-tense line each), explicitly framed as a *draft*, revisited each season, and used as the *filter* when new goals are added ("does this serve your stated vision?"). Each journey chapter surfaces the vision line for its active bucket.
- **Efficaciousness-first onboarding.** The day-one micro-loop ALTER already has (plan→do→track in one minute) IS Newport's efficacy-builder — frame it explicitly as *proving "I can follow through,"* not as a feature tour. This is a strong, exact convergence with ALTER's existing day-one contract.

**Where to resist Newport.** He offers *grand-goal* framing in some places and *pragmatic-ambition laddering* in others. ALTER should take the **pragmatic-ambition** version (bounded ~1-year goals, then a *mandatory savor/gratitude phase* before the next rung) and explicitly reject the single-grand-goal strategy, which Newport himself calls a trap (*"limits our options — there's only so many ideas... and they're pretty common"*). The mandatory-savor phase is a genuinely novel mechanic for ALTER: after a real win, *lock* a gratitude period before allowing a new goal in that domain — this operationalizes ALTER's "always a way out / never optimize-forever" tension.

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Strengthens the graduation ladder and the "journey IS the game/world" law. **Adds:** *the Journey is capability-before-depth and life-bucket-shaped — early stones build efficaciousness and discipline, deeper stones gate behind sustained evidence, and the neglected buckets (Contemplation, Celebration) are structurally protected from being starved by the productive ones.*

## MOVE 7 — Anti-engagement and graduation are the same law, and Newport lets ALTER promote it from taste to hard invariant. This is the strongest convergence in the entire corpus.

**Principle.** ALTER already holds these as design values. Newport independently arrives at every piece, from the attention/digital-minimalism corpus, and gives ALTER the *arguments* to make them non-negotiable:

- **Pseudo-productivity is the enemy metric.** *"Lacking more precise measures... we fell back on pseudo productivity, which said the busier you seem, the better."* Visible activity (taps, opens, streak-of-checking-in) is a *false* proxy for value. **ALTER must never reward raw engagement divorced from real behavior change.**
- **Illusory influence / metrics inflate false importance.** Likes/scores create a felt sense of importance not backed by anything real. Applied to ALTER's *own* points system: *keep metrics honest about what they are (private progress tracking) vs what they aren't (status/validation).* No leaderboards, no comparative framing, no social validation language.
- **Autonomy is the real harm.** The deepest damage from engagement-apps isn't the content, it's the *secondary harm* — what you silently stop doing because the app crowds it out. **ALTER's value prop should be framed around reclaiming autonomy, not maximizing in-app time.**
- **The bigger-better-offer, not willpower.** Newport: you beat compulsion by offering a *more meaningful life*, not by restricting. ALTER's relief-door / micro-stack is exactly this — the "bigger offer" competing with the reflexive phone pickup. *"You need a bigger, better offer."*
- **Graduation as openly optimizing for your own fade.** ALTER's most radical bet (the app wins when you need it less) has no external analog in commercial apps because it's commercially suicidal — but Newport's whole trajectory (deep work → deep life → the tool serving the life, never the reverse) is philosophically identical: *"an app is not a substitute for a process."*

**Mechanism.** Any metric you optimize gets gamed and loses signal (Goodhart). Engagement-optimized feeds train the reward circuit toward fast dopamine, which is precisely the appetite ALTER exists to *heal*. An app that heals distraction by *being* distracting is self-defeating.

**Structural change (this is the constitution-level move).** Turn these from intentions into **mechanical gates** — the same way ALTER already ratchets the innerHTML-wipe count and the SCHEMA↔MIG pairing:
- **A metric-honesty design rule, enforced at build time:** every tracked number (charge, evidence, streak) must pass *"can this be gamed in a way that hurts the real goal? does this imply social status?"* before it ships. Prefer outcome-adjacent metrics (session depth, completed real actions) over easily-gamed activity counts (taps, opens). This is a genuine new preship check, analogous to the existing ratchets.
- **No algorithmic engagement curation, ever.** State it as an identity commitment the way the palette and the no-emoji rules are stated. Any content ALTER surfaces is hand-authored or human-curated, never engagement-optimized. This is a moat *because* rivals can't copy it (their business models forbid it).
- **The graduation counter is a first-class object,** not a nice-to-have: each tool/skill carries its internalization ladder (Willingness→Habit→Grace, already built), and Grace-stable-over-2-weeks fires the graduation prompt. Newport's independent validation means this should be *protected* from any future "increase retention" pressure — it is load-bearing, not optional.

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Promotes the existing anti-engagement / graduation values to **hard invariants with mechanical enforcement.** *Adds:* *no ALTER metric may function as social status or engagement bait; every metric must survive a Goodhart/honesty gate at build time; ALTER never algorithmically curates for engagement; the graduation ladder is protected from retention pressure by constitution.* This is the highest-value structural output of the whole corpus for ALTER, because it converts David's taste into a defended law.

## MOVE 8 — The bookends, the planner, and the journey relate as three time-scales of ONE loop, and the charging primitive is the connective tissue at every scale.

This move is about *relationships between surfaces* — the thing the prompt specifically asks for.

**Principle.** Newport's cascade (Move 2) plus his ritual-design work implies a clean three-way relationship ALTER can adopt wholesale:
- **Planner = the day scale** (time-blocks, reality-check, execution).
- **Bookends = the transition rituals** that open and close each scale (AM = enter/prime the day + build blocks; PM = shutdown/seal + capture + plan tomorrow; and a *new weekly bookend* = "shape the week").
- **Journey = the season scale** (chapters as quarters, the lifestyle vision, the capability→depth ladder).

**Mechanism — ritual as mode-switch.** Newport is emphatic that *entry and exit rituals* (same walk, same cup, a completion phrase) signal the brain to change cognitive mode, reducing transition friction. *"Your morning is not a test of how much discipline you have; it's a test of how well you've designed your morning."* The bookends are literally this: they are the *ritual boundaries* between scales. And the shutdown/seal (Move 3) is the boundary that lets the day close so the season can hold coherence overnight.

**Structural change — the relationships, concretely.**
- **AM bookend** = enter-the-day ritual + Move-1 reality-check + Move-4 energy read + pulls this week's focus (Move 2) down into today's blocks. It is *deep-first* per Newport's bimodal rule: build/prime the day's meaningful block *before* any reactive surface opens.
- **PM bookend** = the shutdown ritual (Move 3): review today, capture open loops into the planner, name tomorrow, *hold-to-seal.* This is the boundary that feeds tomorrow's AM and protects the season.
- **Weekly bookend (new)** = "shape the week," Friday-ish, written *last* after reviewing the journey chapter; a carry-over audit (tasks repeated 3× get demoted to backlog, not silently re-listed); sets a realistic ~80%-capacity week so finishing with room is a real win.
- **The charging primitive is the vertical thread through all three scales:** hold-to-charge is the daily keystone deposit (day), the seal act (PM boundary), and the "invest in the season's vision" act (journey). One gesture, meaning "I am putting real attention into this," at whatever scale — this is exactly what David felt made charging the *seed* primitive. Newport's "attention residue / intensity of focus" work is the mechanism: the hold IS the boredom-tolerance rep, the intensity, the earned-not-frictionless investment. *"Focus is hard. It doesn't feel pleasant... but that doesn't make your work better."* The charge should even *degrade* if the user app-switches mid-hold (attention-residue made visible) — modeling degraded-not-zero value, per the "pseudo-deep work" idea.

**Where NOT to over-nest.** Newport warns against over-scheduling (*"just because you're in control doesn't mean you need to schedule every seven minutes"*) and against system-complexity creep (*"ideally you would not need all these systems"*). ALTER must resist making the cascade heavy. The weekly bookend should be light; the season layer should be one vision line, not a planning burden. This aligns with ALTER's own anti-sprawl constitution — build the *minimum* nesting that makes the day feel non-orphaned, not a five-level bureaucracy.

**Constitution law it strengthens/adds.** Strengthens the cockpit-is-home keystone (all flows render in one stage) and the bookend architecture. **Adds:** *the three surfaces are three time-scales of one loop, joined by ritual boundaries (the bookends) and one investment gesture (charging) — never three separate apps. A new weekly "shape the week" bookend sits between AM/PM (day) and the Journey (season).*

## MOVE 9 — Second-tier structural ideas worth building, and the ones to deliberately NOT build.

These didn't warrant a headline move but change architecture at the margins. Grouped by verdict.

**BUILD — real structural fit:**
- **Deliberate-practice as a generic tool template.** Newport's "test at the granularity of uncertainty" + "difficulty just beyond current ability" + "design a stretch rep, get feedback, track a metric" is a *reusable structure* for any skill tool, not just meditation/breathwork. Guided tools should prompt periodic micro-checkpoints (*"pause — did that land? verify before continuing"*) rather than one long uninterrupted timer, and offer an **adaptive difficulty** that sits at the user's edge (nudges up when "too easy," down on abandonment). This is the journey's dose×scope ladder applied inside a single tool session.
- **Attention residue / single-thread as a felt mechanic.** *"It's the shifts that kill you."* A focus tool can visualize residue/fog that clears only after N uninterrupted minutes and burns off on task-switch — teaching the mechanism as a feeling (per copy law), not a lecture. Reinforces charging (Move 8).
- **Full-capture / brain-dump as a distinct always-available surface.** *"If it's not written down, I don't fully trust it, and then I get anxious."* A frictionless capture quick-add, separate from the structured planner, feeding the PM shutdown. This is the "trusted system" that makes the seal actually work — you can only close a loop you've externalized.
- **Trigger-mounted tools** (already in ALTER's soul doc as Fogg-moves) get Newport validation via ritual-design: "after closing laptop → 3 breaths." The tool escapes the appointment and becomes a reflex — which is *itself* graduation made mechanical (the tool goes invisible).

**DON'T BUILD — poor fit or against ALTER's grain:**
- **App-blocking / screen-time features.** Newport's digital-minimalism corpus is huge and tempting, but it points AWAY from ALTER's job. ALTER's move is the *bigger-better-offer* (relief door, micro-stack), not restriction/blocking. Building screen-time limits would make ALTER a nag, violating "never hands over / never shames." Take the *philosophy* (autonomy, secondary-harms, digital declutter as content) as journey-stone *teaching*, not as app machinery.
- **Communication/office-hours/email-batching features.** Rich in the corpus, near-zero fit for a single-user life-sim on a phone. At most, one teaching stone. Don't build inbox mechanics.
- **Heavy quantified-self dashboards / five-pillar point systems / VO2-max-for-the-mind tests.** Newport floats point-systems and cognitive-endurance metrics; these smell like exactly the pseudo-productivity/gamified-metric trap Move 7 says to gate against. Take the *identity-evidence* framing (track proof of becoming, not counts) and refuse the aggregate-score dashboards.
- **The literal notebook/idea-document systems** (single-purpose notebooks, Zettelkasten-adjacent). Beautiful, but they're paper-analog practices; forcing them into ALTER would bloat it. One reflective "one problem, one page" tool at most.
