# THE COPY PROTOCOL — rules inferred from writing "Why one minute" (2026-09-21)
*Born from David: "can you infer rules and principles based on our writing process of the why one minute to ensure writing copy in the future will be more powerful and efficient." One dive of nine beats took SEVEN drafts. Most of that was avoidable, and the avoidable part is written down here. This sits UNDER `SCRIPT-ENGINE.md` (which picks substance and voice) and beside `COPY-ANCHORS.md` (which holds David's rated lines). Those two say WHAT to write. This says HOW TO RUN THE ROUND so it converges.*

---

## PART 1 — THE PRE-FLIGHT (before a single line is drafted)

Four questions, answered IN WRITING, before drafting. Skipping these is what caused six of the seven drafts.

**1. What is this beat's JOB?** Not its topic, its job. The beat that took four attempts was the one whose job I had never stated. Once it was written down as "refuse the reader's forming objection that shrinking means settling," it was solvable in one pass. A beat whose job cannot be stated in one sentence is not ready to be written, and the writing will drift until it is.

**2. What must the reader ALREADY believe for this line to land?** If they cannot have learned it yet, the beat must teach it, not assume it. Every "unclear" and "what's the point of this" verdict David gave was this failure: copy gesturing at a mechanic it had never introduced ("want a smaller version for those days?" assumes the reader knows what a floor is and why it exists).

**3. What is the SUBJECT of the lesson?** Stated, not implied. Dive 1 is about any self-help habit. Writing every beat about breathing made it read as a breath lesson and David caught it. Generalise the teaching, then narrow to the user's actual case only at the beat that changes state.

**4. Where is the RAW source?** Run the KB-SWEEP (CANON rule 3) BEFORE drafting, not at draft three. In this round the source outclassed every paraphrase I wrote: Fogg's own "you overestimated future motivation... You are not dumb or frivolous or easily hoodwinked. You are human," and Johnson's "if it's not laughably easy, it's probably too hard." I spent two drafts writing around ideas whose better phrasing was sitting on disk. **The source's actual words beat your rendering of them, nearly always.**

## PART 2 — THE LINE TESTS (run on every candidate, before any gate)

- **THE PRONOUN TEST.** Every "it", "that", "this", "his", "one" must have an antecedent within the same beat. David killed an entire round on this alone: "please no more vague sentences." When in doubt, name the noun again; repetition of a concrete noun reads as clear, never as clumsy.
- **THE SCAFFOLDING TEST.** Cut every clause that announces the thing instead of being the thing. Dead openers, all killed in one round: "His next line:", "In his words,", "X, the researcher behind Y, says…", "This is the one worth keeping:". Quote the SUBSTANCE in the app's own voice; never build a beat out of who said it.
- **THE DEFINITION TEST.** A term can only be defined after it is introduced. "This is what tiny habits means" died because nothing had said the words. "This method is called tiny habits" survived, and the rest of the sentence was unchanged.
- **THE ECONOMY TEST.** Does this beat re-spend a word or an idea an earlier beat already landed? Two beats both answering "how small" got merged with nothing lost. But see the CLARITY LAW below: cut what repeats, never cut what explains.
- **THE CHIP TEST.** A chip must name something a person would actually say out loud. David killed "the slammed day" and "the day nobody checks" as writerly labels, and then supplied the real register himself: "a day of scrolling", "feeling off", "overwhelmed with work". **When he tells you what an answer sounds like, those are the words. Use them.**
- **THE JARGON TEST.** "Sized at", "a floor under it", "counts as done" all needed plain speech or an explicit definition. Internal mechanics need naming before they can be referenced.
- **THE ANTI-TEMPLATE TEST.** Could a rival app run this exact sentence? "One minute of breath is enough to change a day" is stock meditation-app copy and died for it.

## PART 3 — HOW TO RUN THE ROUND

- **ONE draft while the brief is moving; OPTIONS only once the target is locked.** This held exactly. Every time the brief was still shifting, a tournament would have amplified a wrong brief. The moment the job was settled and only the wording was open, five options resolved it in one pass. David asks for scale when he wants it; before that, one.
- **TRACK LOVED LINES BY NAME.** He restored a line by asking for it back: "don't remove something which I liked." A line he has praised is an anchor from that moment on and may not be dropped in a rewrite without flagging the drop explicitly.
- **LET HIM ASSEMBLE.** The final beat 2 is his own construction from two gated options, and it is better than either. When he starts writing in the chat, the job switches from drafting to supplying parts.
- **HIS EAR OUTRANKS THE JUDGE, ALWAYS.** The judge rated the Fogg exoneration beat the strongest line in the draft. David killed it twice in one message. Record the kill; do not re-offer it.
- **FINISH HIS SENTENCE WHEN HE TRAILS OFF.** "...but you are not good at predicting … idk" is a brief. Completing his own sentence in two or three forms converged faster than any fresh draft.

## PART 4 — THE JUDGE'S KNOWN BLIND SPOTS (feed these into the Gate-2 prompt)

1. **It does not know David's vocabulary.** It killed "the noise" as a vague pronoun. "The noise" is his, shipped in two places. Any kill of a recurring app word is a claim to adjudicate, never an auto-apply.
2. **It shares the reach-for-profound bias.** Known and already in COPY-ANCHORS; still true.
3. **It over-trusts its own prescriptions.** Its rewrite for one beat reused "sized", the exact jargon David had killed two beats earlier. Diff every judge rewrite against the current kill list before adopting it.
4. **It cannot see the surrounding beats' state.** Give it the full sequence and say which beat is being judged, or it will approve a line that repeats its neighbour.

## PART 5 — THE TWO STANDING LAWS THIS ROUND PRODUCED

Both are already in `COPY-ANCHORS.md`; repeated here because they govern the whole process.

- **CLARITY IS KING.** David: "don't shy away from explaining things that prevent vagueness... clarity is king." This OVERRULES the compression reflex. Krug's "halve the words twice" applies to words that do not explain; it never licenses cutting the explanation. When compression and clarity conflict, the line gets longer.
- **NEVER CLOSE ON THE PRODUCT.** A closing line lands on the reader or on the guardian's promise, never on what the app offers. "Now there's a way to learn it" died as horn-tooting; "You were just never taught it" is the EPIC that replaced it.

## PART 6 — THE HONEST POST-MORTEM

Seven drafts. Here is where they actually went, so the same tax is not paid twice:
- Drafts 1-2: written without stating any beat's job. **Cost: 2.**
- Draft 3: the KB sweep that should have been step zero. **Cost: 1.**
- Draft 4: removing the citation scaffolding I had just added. **Cost: 1.**
- Draft 5: the pronoun sweep, mechanical and predictable. **Cost: 1.**
- Draft 6: reversing my own over-compression after the clarity law. **Cost: 1.**
- Draft 7: the real round, where David restored a loved line and assembled a beat himself.

**A disciplined round is two: pre-flight plus source sweep, then one draft, then his verdict, then options on whatever single beat is still open.**
