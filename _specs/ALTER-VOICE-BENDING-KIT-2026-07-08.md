# ALTER VOICE-BENDING KIT (Kurzgesagt / CGP Grey / Nerdwriter)

## How to use this

This is raw material, not a new rule. Every line you bend with these three voices still runs through the gates you already have: load `_specs/SCRIPT-ENGINE.md`, `VOICE-BIBLE.md`, and `WRITING-SYSTEM.md` first, name which thesis truth the line carries, which stage it's for, and which landed line it came from. Then use this kit to pick the *shape* of the sentence. Kurzgesagt, Grey, and Nerdwriter are not new voices for the guardian. The guardian stays intimate and kind. These are borrowed muscles for how a line moves. Channel the mechanism as a feeling. Keep concept-labels off the in-the-moment whispers. No emojis, no exclamation points, no hashtag-motivation. If a bent line breaks any of that, it fails, no matter how good the shape.

## The three voices, and when to use each

### Kurzgesagt — the awe voice (inspiring journey intros, PRIME, the wonder of change)

**Good for:** journey-stone THESIS moments. The "why does this even matter" copy. Any line that has to make the user *want* to do the thing before you tell them how. The de-shaming open, where a small embarrassing struggle becomes a piece of built-in human machinery instead of a personal flaw.

**Structural moves:**
- Open on the small, unglamorous, universal version of the struggle. Stated flat. The 3am worry-loop. The tap you almost skipped. Never "discover the power of."
- Escalate: reframe that small moment as something structural and shared, not personal. This is you, and it is also everyone, and it was built into you.
- Zoom to mechanism: the evolutionary or bodily reason the struggle exists. This is the backbone that earns the feeling later.
- Name the modern trap without judging. The ancient wiring misfires in a world it wasn't built for.
- Hand the practice over as a small, almost anticlimactic action. Flag that it is not a magic fix.
- Close on a direct question or a quiet image handed back to the user. Never a summary. Never a cheer.

**Sentence patterns:**
- Flat short opener, no hedge. "Everybody's mind runs the same loop at 3am."
- List that builds then one word ends it. "Willpower, discipline, a good morning, the right playlist. None of it reaches the loop."
- Concrete-then-biology pivot. "Think of a smoke alarm. It screams at burnt toast because a real fire, once, was worth every false alarm. Your worry does the same."
- De-shaming reframe in one breath. "You are not broken for feeling this. You are a body doing exactly what a body was built to do."
- Close on a question to "you." "So, tonight, when it starts again. What do you want to do with it?"

**Reach for it when:** the user needs to feel awe or relief at the *why*, at the top of a stone. Not mid-breath. In-the-moment cues stay tight and physical.

### CGP Grey — the teaching voice (explaining a stone's concept, plainly and truly)

**Good for:** Teach-stage copy. The moment ALTER hands the user one true, slightly humbling fact about their own mind or a system they're stuck inside. Turning self-blame into mechanism. The reveal that makes the user feel sharper, not lectured at.

**Structural moves:**
- Open on the flat one-line thesis. The reveal *is* line one. "Your motivation is two things, not one."
- Lay out the plain mechanism before you complicate it. The boring plumbing first, so the twist has something to break.
- Introduce the friction case, the thing that breaks the naive picture, or the exact reason it fails for this user.
- Escalate with concrete specifics, not abstract claims. Let the details carry it.
- Land on a compressed restatement that recolors everything before it.
- Undercut the heavy beat with one small warm line so the piece exhales instead of looming.

**Sentence patterns:**
- Flat thesis opener, no qualifier. "Your morning has a shadow self."
- Direct address that assumes shared experience. "You already know the version of you that shows up at 11pm is not the one making today's plan."
- Rhetorical question as a hinge, marking the turn from how-it-works to what-it-means. "So if the cue is gone, who exactly is supposed to start the habit?"
- The "X isn't Y, it's Z" reframe, correcting self-blame with mechanism. "You didn't skip it because you're lazy. You skipped it because the cue was missing."
- One dry, warm aside *after* a dense beat. Never sprinkled. A release valve, not a personality.

**Reach for it when:** you're teaching a real structural fact and want the user to feel humbled and clever at once. Not for comfort. Not for in-the-moment encouragement. Grey's confidence only lands when the payoff is a genuine reframe.

### Nerdwriter — the reflection voice (the deeper Mirror-stage "oh")

**Good for:** the reflection and depth layer. The closing line after a practice. Mirror-stage "here is what that meant" copy, once the user has already done the thing and is ready to be told what it was. This is the voice that earns a real "oh," not an instruction.

**Structural moves:**
- Open on one concrete small moment from what the user just did. The held breath. The pause before answering the angry text. Never on the abstract virtue.
- Ask the craft question in ALTER's terms. "Why did the third breath feel different from the first." Pose it. Let the practice be the answer. Don't answer it for them.
- Build in passes. Point at the thing, then show it again with more context, then again once they can finally see it.
- Zoom from the single moment out to the larger thing it proves about them.
- Land on a reframe or an open pivot, not a summary. A question that keeps returning to them later.

**Sentence patterns:**
- Direct question mid-thought that the line then answers, making it a shared investigation. "Why did that get easier? It wasn't the breathing."
- Concrete-then-abstract. "The camera in your chest never cut away from the feeling. That was the whole practice."
- One longer clause-building sentence per piece as the thesis-carrier. Rare on purpose. It stands out because ALTER's copy is otherwise terse.
- Judgment as plain fact, earned by what came before. "This is the moment the whole practice depends on."
- End on an open pivot. "Notice where in your day this feeling shows up next."

**Reach for it when:** the user has finished and is ready to be shown what it meant. Never for onboarding. Never mid-breath. Too dense to read while doing.

## Script-bending recipes

Each recipe names a structure, then shows a flat ALTER line bent into it. The BEFORE is the kind of line that passes syntax but does no work.

### Recipe 1 — Kurzgesagt: "small image → cosmic stakes → one human action" (Journey intro / PRIME)

**Structure:** small universal moment, flat → reframe as built-in machinery → mechanism → one anticlimactic action → question handed back.

**BEFORE (flat):** "Gratitude is a powerful practice that can improve your wellbeing. Try writing down what you're thankful for."

**AFTER (bent, Teach-stage stone intro):**
> Some nights the day just closes over you and nothing in it stands out.
> That's not a character flaw. Your brain is built to flag threats and forget the rest. Good things don't leave a mark unless you make them.
> So we make one. Name five small things from today. Not big ones. Small.
> This won't fix your life. It just changes what your brain keeps.
> What's the first one?

### Recipe 2 — CGP Grey: "flat thesis → mechanism → friction case → 'X isn't Y, it's Z'" (Teach-stage concept)

**Structure:** one-line thesis → plain plumbing → the thing that breaks it → reframe self-blame into mechanism → one warm undercut.

**BEFORE (flat):** "Sometimes you miss your habits. Don't be too hard on yourself, just try again tomorrow."

**AFTER (bent, Teach-stage lesson):**
> A habit isn't willpower. It's a cue, a routine, and a small reward, wired in that order.
> The cue is the trigger. No cue, no routine. That's the whole machine.
> So look at yesterday. You didn't skip your breath because you were weak. You skipped it because the thing that was supposed to remind you never fired.
> Fix the cue, not yourself. That part's almost embarrassingly fixable.

### Recipe 3 — Nerdwriter: "one concrete moment → craft question → zoom out → open pivot" (Mirror-stage close)

**Structure:** the small moment they just lived → the question about it → widen to what it proves → end on a question that follows them.

**BEFORE (flat):** "Great job finishing your session. You're building great habits. See you tomorrow!"

**AFTER (bent, Mirror-stage post-practice close):**
> There was a second in there, maybe on the fourth breath, where you stopped waiting for it to be over.
> Why did that happen? You didn't try harder. If anything you did less.
> That's the thing this practice is actually training. Not the breathing. The moment you stop bracing against your own day.
> See if you can catch where that shows up next, off the mat.

### Recipe 4 — Kurzgesagt scale-shift for Chronicle stage (reviewing history / streaks)

**Structure:** cosmic/long frame → collapse down to tonight's one block → resonant image, not a stat.

**BEFORE (flat):** "You've completed 12 sessions this month. Keep up your streak!"

**AFTER (bent, Chronicle-stage review):**
> For as long as there have been people, they've marked the day before letting it go. A notch, a prayer, a line in a book.
> You've done it twelve times this month. That's twelve days you didn't just let close over you.
> Tonight's the thirteenth. Same small thing. One line, and then you're allowed to be done.

## The blend

There are two different stacks, and they never do each other's job.

**Content voices** (Johnson = meaning, Newport = systems, Barada = identity) decide *what the line knows*. They are the source of the thesis truth. Johnson gives you the "why this matters, what a good life is" content. Newport gives you the "here's the mechanism, here's the system that actually works" content. Barada gives you the "who you are becoming" content.

**Style voices** (Kurzgesagt, CGP Grey, Nerdwriter) decide *how the line says it*. They are pure delivery. They carry no content of their own.

Style sits on top of content. Same truth, different shape. Watch one Newport truth ("a habit needs a cue or it won't fire") get dressed three ways:

- **Newport content + Grey style** → "A habit isn't willpower. It's a cue, a routine, a reward. No cue, no habit." (teaching it)
- **Newport content + Kurzgesagt style** → "Your brain won't run a routine no one asked it to. It waits for a signal, the way it always has. So we build the signal." (making them want it)
- **Newport content + Nerdwriter style** → "Notice it wasn't discipline that got you here. It was the reminder you set last week, quietly doing its job." (reflecting on it after)

The pairings that feel most natural, and where to start:
- **Johnson (meaning) → Kurzgesagt.** Meaning wants awe. The de-shaming, zoom-out voice carries "why this matters" best.
- **Newport (systems) → CGP Grey.** Systems want the plain, humbling mechanism reveal. Grey is the teacher.
- **Barada (identity) → Nerdwriter.** Identity wants the earned "oh," the reflection after the act, the pivot that keeps working on you. That's Mirror-stage, that's Nerdwriter.

These are starting pairs, not laws. Any content voice can wear any style. But you always pick content first (what's true), then style (how it moves), then run the whole thing through the gates. Style never invents the truth, and it never overrides the guardian: intimate, kind, on the user's side, no snark, no cheese, no emoji.
