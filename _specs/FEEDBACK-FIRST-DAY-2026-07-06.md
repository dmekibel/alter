## 0. HARD RULES (violations to fix everywhere, immediately)
These read as AI / cheapen the app. Apply app-wide, not just first-run:
- **No em-dashes anywhere.** They read as AI writing. David hates them. (They are currently "everywhere.") Use periods, commas, colons, parentheses instead.
- **No emojis anywhere.** Still present in the build. This is a REGRESSION against the existing law [[no-emojis-tabler-icons-only]]. "I told you no emojis. I'm so confused why there's still emojis." Purge them, use `ti ti-*` line-icons only.
- **No vague language.** Vagueness reads as AI. Everything must be thoughtful, specific, meaningful. (e.g. "where's your charge right now" is too vague.)
- **No corny / cheesy lines.** ("what did it taste like", "picture it already true, 3-2-1" both land corny.)
- **No oversimplified / abstract lines.** They feel cheesy, weak, unscientific. Use specific language and specific instructions.
- **No filler "like".** "Say it now. Like, I start before I feel ready." → just tell the person exactly what to say.

## 1. Copy & text delivery
- **Animate text one LINE at a time, not one word at a time.** People read faster than word-by-word; do not make them wait.
- **Minimize text, maximize value.** Give the perfect amount, made big, colorful, visual, always interesting to read, never boring. This applies to the how-to steps AND the "why that mattered" / "why you're here" sections (walls of text get skipped, and we need people to read them, so make them inviting).
- **Frame everything scientifically.** Skeptics of manifestation exist. Never force-feed woo. Nothing woo-woo from the get-go unless it feels scientific, effective, and pleasurable. Explicitly: the app should explain that placebo works, and make placebo the user's superpower.

## 2. Onboarding structure & philosophy
- **Experience first, extract later.** "How much do you want from me" and deep profiling should be established LATER in the app. First give people a chance to experience the app, then let them figure out what they need. Some users will only need some features.
- **As quick as possible.** Cut non-essential questions from onboarding. ("How easy is it to get out of bed?" is a decent question but not needed here.) Never overwhelming. Fun, easy, straightforward.
- **Hook from the very first second,** engineered like a viral TikTok, interesting enough to lead you through from the very beginning so people do not click away and close the app before they ever start.
- **Adaptive one-size-fits-all.** The story/journey mode should adapt so well that it naturally fits everybody by the way it adapts to you. Adapt to each user, while ideally working for everybody.
- **The first suggested collection of things to do should be more sophisticated.**
- **Tutorial.** Either (a) a real tutorial that points you at what to click by highlighting things (short, fundamental, not boring, not too long), OR (b) the journey first day IS the tutorial: going through the journey naturally guides you through all the features. (David leans toward journey-as-tutorial.)

## 3. Points / rewards
- **Never give points for nothing.** Answering "what work are you doing right now → my own thing" and getting points is bad. Points must always feel EARNED, never random.
- Idea: points earned when you press-and-hold (as an option). Then we build a relationship between press-and-hold and points, which could be powerful. (Ties to the compounding-tool / charge mechanic in `SPEC-PRODUCT-DESIGN-CORE`.)

## 4. The evening-entry problem (a real logic break)
- **Dropping a brand-new user straight into an evening ritual makes no sense.** Even if it is evening, the app should still give an onboarding, a chance to experience some tools, reflection. "Here's the day you lived" is wrong for a first-time user: we just started the app, there is no day lived yet. The language does not make sense.
- **Be evening-aware.** If it is evening, note that and give the person as much value as possible before they sleep, and encourage them to do more in the morning (unless they want to do it all now). 
- **Offer to plan tomorrow now.** On day one, "plan tomorrow" is a powerful, fundamental idea if there is time. Give people that option.

## 5. Tap, don't type
- **"Tomorrow's one thing, choose it now" should not make the user TYPE it.** Typing is the last thing they should do. It should be very tappable. Since onboarding gave the app information, the app should already know the one thing, or offer clever options based on onboarding info. Be clever, do not make them type.

## 6. The rewire tool (mantra / manifestation tool)
- **Too much text, too small, intimidating.** Nobody wants to read that much.
- **Lines are oversimplified / abstract / corny.** e.g. "Picture yourself already being the way you want to be vividly right now" is abstract and oversimplified (exactly the kind of thing to avoid). "I start before I feel ready / I am becoming who I choose / I can do hard things" are not bad but feel too short and oversimplified, which reads cheesy/weak/unscientific.
- **Make it a GUIDED MANTRA, not a press-next flow.** Ideally an audio tape, perfectly designed for the user, formatted like the Tony Robbins approach. The app should just tell the person exactly what to say. It can have a "next," but ideally it guides you by voice.
- **Optional tapping (EFT)** offered before you start. If we add tapping, the app must explain that it works scientifically, explain that placebo works, and that this app makes placebo your superpower.
- **Delivery = guided text spoken out loud + audio + optional tapping (if opted in) + on-screen text,** with perfect natural pauses. The app should know how long to pause; it should feel natural.
- **Breathing visual whenever it says to breathe:** a ball expanding, or a wave sign going up then down, or a circle, so the user can see when the breath is about to end (see the future of the breath).
- **Go back everywhere.** Very important, stated more than once. The user must always be able to go back / hold.

## 7. The posture / first exercise ("sit up, chest up, watch your thing")
- **The whole first exercise is bad:** bad visual (red circle, orange circle), bad text, bad concept. It is a bad way to introduce the app. Rethink the whole thing. Add a way to go back and hold.

## 8. Visual / color (structure first, then visuals, but noted)
- **The energy slider looks ugly,** and "where's your charge right now" is too vague. Use "your energy level" or similar (we do not have to use fewer words).
- **The circles going outward look ugly.** White on dark-dark-blue looks off, especially with those circles. It is not minimal, it is just off.
- **The "Part 1 of 3, About You" yellow circle is ugly** and not the app's aesthetic. The before-you-press state looks bad. The "Part 1 About You" title may be too small.
- **He / she step:** it does not really matter (gender). The coloring and vibe of the whole thing is off. In the plans we had a more beautiful, nuanced way to represent this.
- **Kill ghost mode everywhere.** Don't like everything being ghost/outline. Fix idea: he = blue button, she = pink button (or yellow, does not matter). Once clicked, it becomes brighter and the stripes come in. That is less boring. (This is the solid domain-color game-piece language, [[alter-component-language-game-pieces]], which the current build violated with ghost/dark buttons.)
- **The whole dark-button intro is not iconic.** Visuals to be perfected later, after the text and structure. The minimal color changes above are a good start.

## 9. Journey "why you're here" lesson (last thing of the evening day)
- **"Why that mattered" / "Why you're here" is too much text, hard to read, people will skip it.** We need it, so make it as inviting as possible to understand.
- **"Energy follows clarity. 60 seconds. No wrong answers, only true ones. Next." is weird and makes no sense.** Fix the framing. ("No wrong answers, only true ones" is vague/confusing.)
- The evening day being short is slightly weird but acceptable given it is the evening.

## Regressions this review exposed (drift from the constitution the first-run build introduced)
- Emojis present → violates [[no-emojis-tabler-icons-only]].
- Ghost / dark buttons → violates [[alter-component-language-game-pieces]] (solid domain-color chips, ink borders, hard shadows, gold-ring selection).
- Vague / corny / oversimplified copy → violates the voice quality bar.
- Points for nothing → violates the earned-reward principle (from the June's Journey honest-reward work, `JUNES-JOURNEY-HONEST-INVESTMENT`).
- Em-dashes throughout → new hard rule, add to the voice law.

## The through-lines under all of it (the principles to design to)
1. Experience first, extract later. Quick, hooky, never overwhelming.
2. Adapt so well it fits everybody. The journey can be the tutorial.
3. Tap, do not type. The app is clever enough to know or offer.
4. Points are always earned, never random.
5. Minimize text, maximize value: big, colorful, visual, never boring.
6. Scientific framing, placebo as a superpower, no woo up front.
7. Guided audio mantras with natural pauses; breath visuals; tapping optional and explained.
8. Go back everywhere.
9. No dashes, no emojis, no vague/corny/oversimplified/abstract language.
