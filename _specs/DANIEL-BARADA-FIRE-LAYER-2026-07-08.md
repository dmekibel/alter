## 1. His actual approach, in plain steps

Barada's whole thing is this: **you don't change your life by doing new tasks. You change your life by becoming a new person, and then the tasks come easy.** He says it a hundred ways, but it always lands on the same move. Here it is as steps you can actually do.

**Step 1. Decide who you are now. Out loud. Before there's any proof.**
He calls "I am this now" the most powerful sentence you can say. Not "I want to be fit." You say "I am a fit person." You don't wait to feel it. You say it first, and you let your actions catch up. He's blunt that this feels like lying at the start, and that's fine: "Becoming starts with lies. You lie to yourself first. Then you build the truth, one small piece of proof at a time."

**Step 2. Do one small thing the old you would never do.**
The fastest way to crack the old self is to act out of character, even for five minutes. "The second you act against your old label, even for five minutes, you prove that it's a lie." So the fit person does one push-up. The writer writes one sentence. It's tiny on purpose. The point is not the push-up. The point is the vote it casts.

**Step 3. Treat every action as a vote for who you're becoming.**
This is his core image. Each rep, each session, each small win is a ballot that says "this is who I am now." You are not trying to hit a goal. You are collecting evidence. "You don't rise to your goals, you fall to your standards. And your standards come from your identity."

**Step 4. Be boringly consistent. On purpose.**
He is honest that this part is dull, and that the dullness is the whole secret. "You need 100 average, boring, repetitive days. Not one perfect day." Real power is slow and repetitive, which is exactly why it's almost guaranteed to work if you don't quit. He wants you to expect boredom so it doesn't scare you off.

**Step 5. Use "delusional" confidence at the start, then earn the real thing.**
Early on you won't feel ready. He says act anyway. Fake the belief on purpose, take the action, and let the small win make the belief real. "The person who jumps before they feel certain becomes certain because they jumped." Confidence is not the thing you need before you start. It's the reward you get after you start.

**Step 6. Come back fast when you fall off. Speed of return beats a perfect streak.**
He doesn't ask for perfection. He asks for a quick comeback. "You don't need to be perfect, you just need to come back." When a streak breaks, the only number that matters is how fast you restart. Restarting in five minutes is a win worth celebrating, same as a 30-day streak.

**Step 7. Don't chase results. Let go of them, and let the person you've become pull them in.**
His quieter idea: stop gripping the outcome. "The moment you need something, you've already lost it." You become the person who doesn't need the result, you keep doing the daily thing, and the result shows up on its own. Calm beats desperate.

That's the whole engine. Decide who you are, act against the old label once, count every action as a vote, stay boringly consistent, borrow confidence until it's real, come back fast, and stop white-knuckling the outcome.

## 2. What Barada adds that Johnson and Newport don't

ALTER already has two voices. Johnson tells you **why it matters** (the meaning, the virtue, the point of it all). Newport tells you **exactly how** (the system, the time block, the concrete steps). Both are true. Both are useful. And both quietly assume something that a lot of people don't have yet.

They assume you believe you can change.

Here's the gap. You can hand a frozen person the perfect system. Clear steps, good schedule, everything. And they still won't touch it. Not because the system is wrong. Because underneath, they don't believe they're the kind of person who does this. So the system just sits there, and now they feel worse, because they had the plan and still didn't move.

**Barada works one layer below the plan.** His job is the belief and the destination. He answers two questions Johnson and Newport skip:

- **"Who am I becoming?"** He gives you a clear picture of the future you, so there's something to walk toward. Not a vague better-you. A specific person with habits and a way of moving through the world.
- **"Can I actually change?"** He hands you the belief before you've earned it, on purpose, and then shows you how to turn it real one small proof at a time.

So the three fit together like this:

- **Barada is the fire.** Who you're becoming, and the belief you can. He gets you to move at all.
- **Newport is the mechanics.** Once you're moving, here's exactly how.
- **Johnson is the meaning.** Here's why it's worth it, so you keep going.

A person won't use Newport's beautiful system until they believe they're someone who could. Barada's whole job is to make them believe it, and then to make the belief true through action. Without him, the other two are a great map handed to someone who's convinced they can't walk.

## 3. How it becomes journey stones (the fire in each rung)

A journey stone already has a shape: PRIME (see who you're becoming) -> DO (the actual small thing) -> NAME (say what you did) -> PLACE (put it in your life) -> SEAL (lock it in). Barada's fire wraps the two ends. He owns the **PRIME** at the start (the vision of the future you) and the **SEAL** at the end (the identity-proof: you didn't just do a task, you cast a vote for who you are).

The middle (DO, NAME, PLACE) stays Newport's mechanics and Johnson's meaning. Barada bookends it.

**Where he goes in a stone:**

- **PRIME (start):** a short line that shows the future self, and quietly says "you can be them, starting now." Not a task. A picture. "The version of you who moves like this already exists. Today you go be them for two minutes."
- **SEAL (end):** the identity-proof. You reframe the small thing they just did as evidence. "That wasn't a workout. That was a vote. You just told yourself who you are."

**Concrete stone examples:**

**Stone: the first tiny movement (for someone who says "I have no energy").**
- PRIME: "Somewhere in you is a person who moves. We're not going to convince you you're them. We're going to let you prove it. One small move."
- DO: (Newport) stand up, walk to the window, back. That's it.
- SEAL: (Barada) "You just did the thing the tired version of you said you couldn't. That's the proof. You came back to yourself for thirty seconds. Tomorrow, again."

**Stone: the scary task you keep avoiding (the footage you won't look at, the room you won't start).**
- PRIME: "The person who does this isn't braver than you. They just opened the file while still scared. You're going to be that person for sixty seconds."
- DO: (Newport) open it. Just open it. Don't fix anything. Close it after a minute if you want.
- SEAL: (Barada) "You acted while your hands were still shaking. That's not a small thing, that's the whole skill. You just outvoted the old story once. Do it again and it starts to lose."

**Stone: coming back after a lapse.**
- PRIME: "Seasons happen. The hero isn't the one who never falls. It's the one who came back. You're already doing the hard part by being here."
- DO: (Newport) one tiny thing, smaller than before.
- SEAL: (Barada) "You just proved you're someone who returns. That's worth more than the streak you lost. Fast comebacks are the real muscle."

The pattern is always the same. **PRIME shows the future self and lends the belief. The middle does the work. SEAL turns the work into proof of who they are.** That's the fire in every rung.

## 4. Per person: where his fire helps and where it HURTS

This is the honest part. Barada's energy is strong medicine. On the right person it's rocket fuel. On the wrong person, at full strength, it backfires and makes them feel worse. So he does not come out the same for all four.

**David — FULL FIRE. He's built for it.**
David has a sprawling pile of ambitions and an avoidance problem (room, gym, pot). Barada's "become unrecognizable, cast a vote, do the hard thing anyway" energy is exactly his language. The "identity, not tasks" reframe helps him hold many goals without drowning: he's not doing twelve projects, he's becoming one kind of person. Give him the full voice. The one thing to watch: David can use "become a new person" as a reason to plan a giant reinvention instead of doing one small thing today. So for him, always cut the fire with Barada's own boring-consistency line. Big identity, tiny daily vote. Don't let the fire become another grand plan.

**Brother — FULL FIRE, but aimed carefully.**
He wants to be a famous musician and filmmaker, but he's got ADHD, low self-esteem, and old school trauma, and he's literally scared to look at his film footage. Barada is almost tailor-made for the belief half of his problem. "You act while your hands are still shaking." "Delusional confidence first, then earn it." "The old label is a lie the second you act against it." This is fire he needs. **But** the danger is real: his fear is genuine, and if the fire is too loud it becomes one more voice he can't live up to, which feeds the low self-esteem. So for Brother, keep the fire, but always pair it with the smallest possible step and a fast-comeback safety net. Never "become a beast." Always "open the file for sixty seconds, that's the whole win." Fire plus a tiny, shame-free step. Not fire alone.

**Mom (66, gentle, Russian) — QUIET VERSION ONLY.**
Full Barada would be wrong for her. The "burn the blueprint, become unrecognizable, go 100% or you're lying to yourself" register is aggressive, young, and male, and to a gentle 66-year-old it reads as fake or exhausting, not inspiring. Her sports goal is blocked by a real wound (a trainer was once cruel to her). Loud fire near that wound is a mistake. **But the calm core of Barada still helps her, if you strip the heat.** Keep the gentlest parts: "you can be someone new, starting now," "one small proof," "you don't have to feel ready," "just come back, no guilt." Lose entirely: "delusional," "beast," "go all in," "kill the old you," "do the hard thing." For Mom, Barada becomes a soft, warm "you can, and it can be gentle." The destination stays. The aggression goes.

**Sister — ALMOST NO FIRE right now. Maybe a spark, much later.**
She's the hardest case for Barada, and honesty matters here. She has two kids, a chaotic house, and says she has no energy. She is frozen by overwhelm. To a frozen person, "become unrecognizable, go 100%, you don't rise to your goals you fall to your standards" doesn't light a fire. It lands as a weight. It says "look how far you are from where you should be," which is exactly the crush she's already under. **At full strength, Barada hurts her.** What she needs first is Newport's shrink-it-smaller and a lot of warmth, not a call to transform. So for Sister, hold the fire back almost entirely. The one Barada idea that's safe and even helpful for her is the smallest, kindest one: **the vote.** Not "become a new person," but "wiping this one counter is one small vote that you're someone who takes care of her space." Tiny, present-tense, no future giant to measure against. Save the real fire for much later, only once tiny wins have thawed her out and she's actually asking to go bigger. Give it before then and it backfires.

**Short version:** David and Brother get the fire (Brother's always tied to a tiny step). Mom gets the warm, quiet core with the aggression removed. Sister gets almost none of it now, just the gentlest single idea (the vote), because full-strength Barada makes an overwhelmed person feel worse, not better.
