# THE ALTER WRITING SYSTEM — the rule-set for every line
**Date:** 2026-07-07 · Standing artifact. **Why:** copy kept drifting into AI cadence (the line "measurable psychology, not magic" is the trigger case). This is the enforceable rule-set so it stops. Sits above the VOICE-BIBLE: the VOICE-BIBLE is ALTER's specific guardian register; this is the universal craft plus the anti-AI-voice gate that every line passes first.
**Built from:** Tim Danilov's Script/Niche Bending Protocol (fieldguide), the Balance `AI-TELL-DETECTOR` (David's LinkedIn system), and explainer-craft research (Kurzgesagt, Loewenstein's curiosity gap, Heath brothers' Made to Stick, Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing").

---

## THE ONE LAW
**Specificity beats abstraction. Every time.** The single thing that separates human writing from bot output is a concrete, named, sensory particular. A "flower" is bot. "A bruised magnolia petal caught under a windshield wiper" is human. When a line feels off, the fix is almost never a cleverer phrasing; it is a more specific noun. Symmetrical, profound-sounding, balanced sentences that say little are the core failure mode; a concrete detail is the cure.

### THE ONE LAW'S FAILURE MODE (added 2026-08-15, from the humanization-corpus audit)
**Specificity must come from the source, never from the model.** The ONE LAW sets a trap: told to be concrete, a writer with no concrete material manufactures it. Every showcase "improved" example in the audited corpus did exactly this. A funding rewrite added "led by Sequoia," a detail absent from the original. A hydration rewrite invented an entire personal memory ("I used to forget to drink water during long hikes"). Both shipped as the model answer. **No specific available means cut the claim, never invent one.** In Balance client copy this stops being a style question: a manufactured fact about a client's business ships as a lie.

## THE BAN LIST (auto-fail, rewrite before it ships)
1. **The negation-reversal cadence.** "X, not Y" · "not just X, but Y" · "it's not X, it's Y" · "X, rather than Y" · "No X. No Y. Just Z." This is the loudest LLM tell; it performs balance instead of stating a point. FIX: delete the negation, state the specific positive thing. ("Measurable psychology, not magic" dies here.)
2. **Em-dashes and en-dashes** ( — – ). Banned outright. Use a period, comma, colon, or a rewrite. (Already law; enforced here.)
3. **Emoji.** Banned app-wide. `ti-*` line icons only.
4. **Slop words:** powerful, game-changer, revolutionary, unlock, leverage, elevate, supercharge, seamless, robust, foster, delve, crucial, pivotal, showcase, tapestry, landscape/realm (metaphorical), testament, vibrant, meticulous, intricate, future-proof.
5. **Template openers:** "In a world where…", "Imagine a world where…", "In today's fast-paced…", "Let's face it", "Let's dive in", "The secret nobody tells you", "Let that sink in".
6. **Throat-clearing:** "It's important to note", "It's worth noting", sentence-start "Additionally", "When it comes to".
7. **Copula-avoidance** (dressing up "is"): "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "boasts", "features". Just write "is".
8. **The wrap-up bow / moralizing close:** "At the end of the day", "Ultimately", "This teaches us", "The lesson here", any closer that restates what was already said. End on the sharpest line or a real question.
9. **Hedging** when uncertainty isn't the point: "can help", "may", "arguably", "in many ways", "that said". State the plain claim.

## THE SMELL TESTS (more than two hits = not ready)
- **Rule-of-three overuse.** Triplets ("faster, cheaper, better") are a machine fingerprint, worse when fused with negation. Vary the count.
- **Metronome rhythm.** Read it aloud. If every sentence is the same length and shape, break it. Humans write uneven.
- **Clever-for-clever.** A line that sounds good but teaches nothing dies, even if it won a first read.
- **The anti-template test (the master gate):** *could this exact line have been written by any AI, about any other topic?* If yes, kill it. A line must be un-copy-pasteable to anything else.

## THE POSITIVE CRAFT (what to do, from the three sources)
**Hooks (Tim + Kurzgesagt):**
- Start mid-action, never with setup, a date, or a definition. First sentence = the thing itself.
- The hook must make the reader think "wait, why?" If it doesn't, rewrite it.
- Answer who/what instantly. "How everything collapsed" is vague; "How Enron hid 60 billion from Wall Street" is a hook.
- Open on a specific, concrete, imaginable question ("what would a black hole in your pocket do?"), not a general one.

**Curiosity (Loewenstein):** curiosity fires on a *specific bounded gap*, not general mystery. Name the gap, give one priming fact, withhold the payoff.

**Concreteness (all three):** named, dated, sourced specifics. "Sapolsky's 2004 Stanford baboon study", not "a scientist proved". Pair every abstract number with a physical comparison. One vivid sensory detail beats a list of three.

**Made to Stick (Heath):** one core idea per piece · break the pattern (unexpected) · tangible sensory language · earn belief with vivid internal detail · make ONE person care, not "users" · let the reader mentally simulate a scene.

**Voice:** no hype, no motivational language, no guru tone. Show, don't declare (credibility is shown, never stated). Use "therefore" (argument) and "but" (tension), not "and then". Frame change as evolution ("extends", "layered onto"), never battle ("kills", "replaces"). No absolute claims; let the reader self-diagnose ("if you tend to…").

**Structure:** hook → escalation/beats → payoff → loop back (Tim). Or SETUP → PIVOT → INSIGHT → APPLICATION → soft-question close (Balance). Pick ONE reveal device per piece, never two.

## THE TWO WORKING MOVES
- **Bend, don't free-write.** Keep a proven structure and swap the content; do not compose every microcopy string from a blank page. Identify the sentence shapes that already land in ALTER and re-fill them with new substance.
- **The 20 percent cut.** After drafting, try to cut a fifth of the words with no loss of information. If you can, it was bloated. Ship the shorter one.

## RECONCILING WITH THE VOICE-BIBLE (the aphorism question)
The VOICE-BIBLE's "THE LAW" device (parallelism, inversion) is not automatically banned. The keeper "What's wrong is always available. So is what's right." earns its balance because it states a real, usable truth. "Measurable psychology, not magic" does not; it performs profundity and says little. **The test: does the balanced structure carry a specific true payload, or is the balance the whole point?** If the balance is the point, it is the AI tell, cut it. When unsure, choose the plainer, more concrete sentence.

## THE OVER-CORRECTION GATE (second-order tells, added 2026-08-15)
The BAN LIST catches prose that sounds like a machine. It does not catch prose de-machined too hard, which carries its own fingerprint. Tag a line OVER-CORRECTED and rewrite when it shows:
- **Humanizer dialect.** Staccato fragments plus manufactured drama plus a rhetorical question. This is a fingerprint too, just a fashionable one.
- **Syntax salad.** A plain word swapped for a fancier one to dodge a kill list ("paramountly significant" for "important"). If the rewrite is harder to say out loud, revert it.
- **Purple compensation.** Abstraction replaced with ornament instead of with fact. "Paints the sky in fiery oranges and soft pinks" is not more specific than "beautiful," only longer.
- **Fabricated first person.** Invented experience or opinion dropped into prose that had no author in it. Voice comes from David or not at all.

### Corpus specimens (KILLED). Not David-rated, so these stay here and do NOT get merged into COPY-ANCHORS.md.
Each was published as the *corrected* version. Each breaks a rule its own source states elsewhere.
1. `"Acme Analytics raised a $40M Series B led by Sequoia."` → invented specific. Sequoia appears nowhere in the source text.
2. `"Look, the wishlist is huge—new features, smarter ops, maybe even a moonshot or two. Cash, though? That's the leash."` → humanizer dialect, plus a banned em dash.
3. `"I used to forget to drink water during long hikes, until one trip left me with a pounding headache..."` → fabricated first person. A memory invented to satisfy a specificity rule.
4. `"The sunrise paints the sky in fiery oranges and soft pinks. The cool morning air fills your lungs..."` → purple compensation. Longer, not more concrete.

### Corpus specimen (EPIC), the single clean one, with the invented clause removed:
`"Acme Analytics raised a $40M Series B. The Boulder-based startup makes an observability platform that runs queries in under a second and plugs into existing monitoring stacks without custom integration work."`
Why it works: every clause carries a checkable fact, no hype adjective, no em dash, no balance-for-its-own-sake.

### Evidence note, so nobody "corrects" this file later
A citation audit of the 21-source humanization corpus found the numeric thresholds circulating in this field are unsourced: burstiness ranges, TTR 0.50 to 0.65, Flesch 60 to 70, the "111x" word-frequency multipliers (GPTZero vendor data, and they sell the detector), and the one-em-dash-per-1000-words ceiling. What *is* peer-reviewed is only that detectors are unreliable (Rashidi et al. 2023 measured a 10.24% false-positive rate on NEJM abstracts; Jabarian & Imas 2025 up to 78% on open-source classifiers). **Consequence: never tune this file toward a detector score.** The em dash ban and every threshold here stand on David's taste and on lines he rejected, which is a stronger basis than the folklore, not a weaker one.

## ENFORCEMENT
Before any user-facing copy ships: run it through the BAN LIST (grep-able: dash, the negation cadence, the slop words), then the OVER-CORRECTION GATE, then the anti-template test, then read it aloud for metronome rhythm. New copy is written by *bending* an existing landed line, not from scratch. Any line David flags as "sounds AI" gets added to the ban list here.
